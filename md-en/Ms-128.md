# Ms-128

### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/1.webp)</span>

If now I say “I expect…” – is this stating that the situation, my actions, thoughts, etc., are those of expecting a certain event? Or do the words “I expect…” themselves belong to the processes of expecting? Under certain circumstances, “I expect…” will simply mean (…), “I believe that this and that will happen,” sometimes even “Be prepared that…” I say to someone, “I heard that he will come; I have been expecting him all day.” This is a report of how I have spent the day. In a conversation, I arrive at the conclusion that a certain event is to be expected, and I draw this conclusion by saying, “I must now expect his visit.” This can be the first thought of this expectation. The exclamation “I am eagerly awaiting him!” is an act of expecting, if the tension of the expectation finds expression in it. But I could also have the same words as a result—

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/1.webp),[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/2.webp)</span>

How do I know that I meant the sentence as a communication? Was the little that pointed in that direction when I spoke it really enough for me to say with certainty that I meant it that way? But if we can speak of interpretation here, can I _misinterpret_ those signs? (What would be the criterion for that?) Is it not the case that, in my interpretation, I proceed in a certain direction? It is as if I had simply allowed the seed that lay in those rudimentary actions to grow. And it now shows what it was the seed of.

### <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/2.webp)</span>

But what if I _recall_ what I meant back then? How could I provide a proof that my memory is deceiving me?

### <span class="fac">[2\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/2.webp)</span>

Can the evidence not be too sparse? Yes, if one examines it, it seems extraordinarily sparse; but that is perhaps because one disregards the history of that evidence. If I am about to come to my teacher with feigned discomfort, this did not happen out of the blue. All sorts of thoughts, among other things, must have preceded it.

### <span class="fac">[2\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/2.webp)</span>

And if the evidence were too sparse—what would I say then? That it almost seems as if I meant the sentence as a communication? But does such a case occur? And why not?

### <span class="fac">[2\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/2.webp),[3\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/3.webp)</span>

Does one ever say: “When I think back on the tone and manner in which I said that, it seems I meant …”? No. And that can only be because one does not interpret evidence here _on the basis of conjecture_. One does not treat it at all as evidence from which conclusions are drawn. Nor, however, as insignificant side effects. The interpretation is simply the _aftermath_ of these actions.

### <span class="fac">[3\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/3.webp)</span>

But let us assume that I said to myself, before I uttered the sentence aloud, “I am not feeling well,” “I will mean this sentence as a communication.” (And one could not ask for more evidence!) But I could also have said that sentence without seriously meaning it. If I now remember saying this to myself, is not an interpretation on my part still necessary here? Is it enough to remember that I said this to myself? Is not even this evidence still insufficient?

### <span class="fac">[3\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/3.webp),[4\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

It is here again, as if we remember that subtle nuance of feeling that only made whatever I did or said into an expression of meaning. My movement, my facial expression, my tone of voice would certainly not have been evidence of meaning in themselves, one might think; it was the meaning that made them into an expression of meaning. Only it is strange that one clings to this “expression” at all. And even stranger: If I recall something that happened, I only find this expression, and indeed, it feels like an expression. If, indeed, I am playing some role, then a glance also feels like this and that. And what does that mean?

### <span class="fac">[4\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

If I quote, “Yes, yes, it is understandable, Mr. Minister…,” I adopt an ironic tone and know exactly that it is one and what is meant by it.

### <span class="fac">[4\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

“Thank God, at least something has escaped the clutches of the Croats.” If I read that aloud, someone might say that there is not enough evidence to say how it is meant.

### <span class="fac">[4\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

And what, then, was it: to mean it ironically? To live in this situation & say it as I would say it in this situation.

### <span class="fac">[4\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

If I cast an ironically smiling glance at someone, thinking something like “What an ass!”, is there any doubt as to how this glance should be interpreted?

### <span class="fac">[4\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

Could I say: I feel much more precisely _how_ I smile than the other person sees?

### <span class="fac">[4\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/4.webp)</span>

Couldn't I, if I wanted to, repeat the glance, the tone of the sentence, the movement of my body? And do so, more or less, with the sensations, etc., that accompanied it? And isn't what I repeat evidence enough?

### <span class="fac">[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/5.webp)</span>

My remembering what I did as an expression of my opinion is connected with my ability to reproduce the situation! And when reproducing, how do I experience the opinion that I don't even have then?

### <span class="fac">[5\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/5.webp)</span>

One sometimes says: “In this tiny movement, this shadow of a smile, lay the whole meaning.” But wouldn't a mistake still be possible?

### <span class="fac">[5\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/5.webp)</span>

I mean: _must_ I not be able to be mistaken if the other person observing me can be mistaken? (_Admittedly_, he does not see everything that I perceive.)

### <span class="fac">[5\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/5.webp)</span>

But can _he_ always be mistaken? Do I _always_ know better than he does what I meant? Can’t he say: “I saw and heard _exactly_ what you meant; if you now believe something else, you are mistaken.”

### <span class="fac">[5\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/5.webp),[6\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/6.webp)</span>

Could one say: My embryonic behaviour may be _absolutely_ characteristic of a certain developed behaviour; just as a small fragment of a leaf is characteristic of a plant. Or it may allow for different interpretations. If, in this case, I interpret it with certainty, do I mean to say that I am developing the undeveloped further?

### <span class="fac">[6\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/6.webp)</span>

If I say “I meant it this way,” do I judge the seed & say that this is the seed of such a plant; or do I let it grow & _show_ what plant will come from it?

### <span class="fac">[6\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/6.webp),[7\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

Let's say I say: “I wanted to deceive you for a moment.” If it was only for a moment, was it a fully developed intention to deceive? (Of course, I _can_ be wrong, because my memory can always be wrong; but that's not what it's about.) If I clearly remember everything that happened at the time, do I have the right to say that it was the will to deceive?

### <span class="fac">[7\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

Yes, I only have a seed, & perhaps I'm not a seed expert; but I took it from a poppy. (Its history was decisive.)

### <span class="fac">[7\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

“How can you be so sure that you wanted to deceive me? Weren't your actions & thoughts too rudimentary?”

### <span class="fac">[7\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

If Illo says “It wasn't Mr. Minister this time…,” does he already know what he will say later? Should we say: “He has an approximate idea of it”?

### <span class="fac">[7\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

The opinion, I would like to say, _develops_.

### <span class="fac">[7\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

If I say “I wanted to deceive you for a moment,” one can ask: How far did your intention go? There are details!

### <span class="fac">[7\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/7.webp)</span>

And if that is the case, then there must be a case in which the details are not sufficient to _determine_ the intention.

### <span class="fac">[8\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/8.webp)</span>

If someone says to me “For a moment I wanted to deceive you,” how is it that I _understand_ him?! How have I learned to apply these words? – And what do I think he means? Well, the thought occurred to him.

### <span class="fac">[8\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/8.webp)</span>

“This thought connects to thoughts I once had.” – How does he do that? Through a _feeling_ of connection? But can a feeling _really link_ thoughts? That a connection exists can be shown later.

### <span class="fac">[8\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/8.webp)</span>

So is there _nothing_ in my idea that the interpreting words grow out of the interpreted as its continuation?

### <span class="fac">[8\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/8.webp)</span>

Is “I meant…” always just a report?

### <span class="fac">[8\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/8.webp),[9\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/9.webp)</span>

Someone says “I wanted to deceive you for a moment.” I reply: “You're only reading that into the situation.” He: “No, I remember clearly – I wanted to deceive you.”

### <span class="fac">[9\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/9.webp)</span>

On the other hand, someone says: “With this remark, I meant _you_!” He wants to hit him with it again.

### <span class="fac">[9\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/9.webp)</span>

I am ashamed that I wanted to deceive him back then. That I had this intention. What am I ashamed of now? I recall the incident in my memory – how I looked at him, etc., etc. So I am quite certain that I did something bad at the time.

### <span class="fac">[9\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/9.webp)</span>

If one asks: ‘Was it really so bad to look at him like that, etc., to say to him “I want to deceive him”?’ the answer is: ‘The bad thing was the essence of the whole thing.’

### <span class="fac">[9\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/9.webp),[10\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/10.webp)</span>

If I say: “I hated him at that moment,” what happened? Doesn’t it consist of thoughts & feelings? And if I mime such a scene, I make a certain face, think of certain facts, breathe in a certain way, bring about certain feelings within myself. I could write down a conversation, imagine an entire scene in which this hatred would flare up. And I could play this scene with feelings that are very close to those in a scene from real life. Of course, the fact that I have actually experienced such scenes will help me.

### <span class="fac">[10\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/10.webp)</span>

If I am now ashamed of this incident, I am ashamed of the whole thing: the poisonous tone, the words, etc.

### <span class="fac">[10\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/10.webp)</span>

I can utter certain words in a characteristic tone; let’s say: “You know better than I do.” I utter it in the tone in which it is an _allusion_ to something that I do not name. – I can now imagine various scenes around this utterance (with always the same tone of voice & look). And these scenes will give my words, the tone of voice, etc., different meanings.

### <span class="fac">[11\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/11.webp)</span>

The words could be, for example: “I don’t feel well.” But in order to be able to mean them in the special way that I have been talking about, it requires the scene with the interpretation. And the scene would have to have a certain length & one would have to imagine certain circumstances for it.

### <span class="fac">[11\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/11.webp)</span>

“I am not ashamed of what I did at the time, but of the intention I had.” – But was the intention not also in what I did? What did intending consist of? Only in what I thought at the time, what I said to myself? What gives the intention? The whole story.

### <span class="fac">[11\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/11.webp)</span>

The present must be replaced here by a story.

### <span class="fac">[11\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/11.webp)</span>

The words “Thank God, just a little bit more…” spoken with the right tone & look seem to contain their whole meaning in themselves. But this is because we see these words in this scene. Let us take the trouble to build a completely different scene around them (the tone of the words, etc., remains the same), and we will see that it is not the words with their tone of voice that contain the meaning in themselves.

### <span class="fac">[12\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/12.webp)</span>

Let a person look angry, haughty, ironic; & now cover his face with a cloth, but leave the eyes, in which all the expression seemed to be concentrated, uncovered. You will see how surprisingly ambiguous the expression of these eyes now is.

### <span class="fac">[12\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/12.webp)</span>

“It is all already in…” How does it come about that the arrow _points_?

→

Doesn’t it already seem to contain something outside of itself? “No, it is not the dead strokes; only the psychological, the meaning, can do this.” That is true & false. The arrow only points in the application that the living being makes of it. This pointing is not a hocus pocus that only the mind can accomplish.

### <span class="fac">[12\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/12.webp),[13\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/13.webp)</span>

In a story it says, for example, “N looked at him with a hostile gaze & said…” The reader understands it without a doubt. Now you say: “Well, he adds the meaning to it, he guesses it.” But in this story, this description is unambiguous. Certainly, under other circumstances, the one who says something with a hostile look could be pretending or the whole thing could take place on stage. It is also possible that the reader, when he reads this sentence, does not yet know the true interpretation & only finds it out later. But what comes to light in such a case? –

### <span class="fac">[13\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/13.webp)</span>

But in certain cases, the reader can be doubtful about how these looks & words were really meant & can indeed guess at something, even if that is not the usual process. – But then he guesses primarily at a different context. He says to himself, for example: the two who are behaving so hostilely are actually friends, etc., etc.

### <span class="fac">[13\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/13.webp),[14\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/14.webp)</span>

“I know I wanted to deceive you at that moment, and I am ashamed of it.” – But it may be that he wanted to reproach himself for everything he did, interpreting every movement, every glance as a sign of evil intention. Yes, that he often _played_ the villain, as it were, so that he could then reproach himself. So that in this case one would have to say that he did this and that in order to be able to interpret it that way later, and then gave it that interpretation.

### <span class="fac">[14\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/14.webp)</span>

But he himself would not admit that, but say that he remembers clearly … .

### <span class="fac">[14\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/14.webp)</span>

But how could I give such a description at all (someone plays the villain in order to be able to reproach himself later)? What kind of experience could authorize me to do so?

### <span class="fac">[14\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/14.webp),[15\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/15.webp)</span>

“I wanted to deceive you for a moment” – “That is to say, I have a certain feeling, an inner experience; &amp; I remember it.” – And now remember _quite precisely_ – – – there the “inner experience” of wanting seems to vanish again. Instead, one remembers certain thoughts, connections to earlier situations, and other details more or less characteristic of the situation of deception. (It is as if one had changed the setting of the microscope, and what is now in focus was previously outside it.)

### <span class="fac">[15\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/15.webp)</span>

“Well, that only shows – you say – that you have set your microscope incorrectly. You should look at one layer of the preparation & you are now looking at another.”

### <span class="fac">[15\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/15.webp)</span>

“I wanted to deceive you.” – Are you sure? Are you not putting that into your words now? – “No, no; I know exactly how it was. I had this intention before…” – Or rather:

“No, I am not mistaken; an evil spirit suddenly came into me.” – Now in this case we would not have to believe him.

### <span class="fac">[15\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/15.webp),[16\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/16.webp)</span>

“I wanted to deceive you for a moment” is no different from “I wanted to turn to you for a moment” or “I wanted to point at him for a moment,” etc., etc. In doing so, I recall—correctly or incorrectly—various facts that can also be confirmed by others. The question is simply whether the presentation of these facts _as an intention_ is an interpretation.

### <span class="fac">[16\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/16.webp)</span>

_If_ the other person interprets what he sees in me as an intention, why shouldn’t _I_? Can one say that _I_ know the _essential_ thing that he merely **suspects**?! Or doesn’t he also always interpret my behaviour as an intention, but rather _sees_ the intention in it, just as I see it.

### <span class="fac">[16\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/16.webp)</span>

“Only I know my innermost thoughts.” – Or should I continue: “& I don’t”?

### <span class="fac">[16\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/16.webp)</span>

How did I learn the words “I had the intention …”, “I was already in the process of …”? Is one shown what it is like to have an intention? – Well, in a certain sense, _yes_. –

### <span class="fac">[16\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/16.webp),[17\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/17.webp)</span>

“Only you can know whether you had the intention.” One could say that to someone when giving an explanation of the meaning of the word “intention.” It then means: _that_ is _how_ we use it.

### <span class="fac">[17\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/17.webp)</span>

And if someone now says: “Yes, I saw that you had the intention to say something.” _How_ does he interpret what he saw? What is the sense of this interpretation? What do his words tell us? _What_ did he guess? – He could have, for example, whispered to someone else: “L.W. is about to say something.”

### <span class="fac">[17\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/17.webp)</span>

What good does it do if _I_ say “I already had the intention …”? What does this communicate to the other person? I could just as well have communicated this—for roughly the same purpose—to him.

### <span class="fac">[17\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/17.webp)</span>

We could also imagine the case that I write down my intentions. Perhaps in order to have testimony later. Important conclusions can be drawn from these notes.

### <span class="fac">[18\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/18.webp)</span>

“I have the intention…” is, under certain circumstances, an utterance, e.g. the utterance of a decision. Sometimes it is the description of a situation. “Why are you packing?” “I have the intention to travel to…” I may have already taken my tickets.

### <span class="fac">[18\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/18.webp)</span>

“As you know, I had the intention of traveling to ….” The other person _may_ know this as surely as I do. Indeed, they may remember my intention more precisely than I do.

### <span class="fac">[18\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/18.webp)</span>

If I remember my intention, must I remember a single state of mind, or thought?

### <span class="fac">[18\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/18.webp)</span>

Why should an expressed intention always be more unambiguous than one expressed through gestures, etc.?

### <span class="fac">[18\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/18.webp),[19\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

Why should remembering the words I said back then tell me more than remembering a look or a movement? Consider: these too are part of an old context.

### <span class="fac">[19\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

But I said those words back then, and now I'm saying _different_ words. Am I not interpreting the earlier words through the new ones?

### <span class="fac">[19\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

For example, I said to you back then, “I intend to…” _Now_ I say: “As I already said to you back then, I intended to…”

### <span class="fac">[19\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

“If I had said the words ‘I want to deceive him now,’ I wouldn’t have had the intention any more certainly than I do now.” – But if you had said those words, would you not have meant them in all seriousness?

### <span class="fac">[19\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

I remember the whole setup.

### <span class="fac">[19\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

I said that I later interpret my rudimentary actions as intentions. But why do I do that? What is the purpose? Is it a kind of madness?

### <span class="fac">[19\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp)</span>

Could I have given one of two descriptions: on the one hand, a description of the remembered actions and perhaps the thoughts and feelings – on the other hand, a description of the events as intentions?

### <span class="fac">[19\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/19.webp),[20\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/20.webp)</span>

It seems that sometimes the description of the actions, etc., without mentioning intention is sufficient, and sometimes it is not.

### <span class="fac">[20\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/20.webp)</span>

I say, “I got up and went to the window so as not to have to listen to the conversation.”

Or: “I thought, ‘I can’t stand this chatter any longer,’ got up, and went to the window.”

### <span class="fac">[20\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/20.webp)</span>

The latter is not so clear. Why not?

### <span class="fac">[20\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/20.webp)</span>

One says now: “I wanted to get away from them.” But what did I think and feel then? The fact that I went to a remote corner where nothing attracted me, that I made a disgusted face, that it relieved me to move away from the group (the whole setup), even without the thought “just get away!” – that constitutes the intention of moving away from the people. (An actor could bring this intention to full display.)

### <span class="fac">[20\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/20.webp),[21\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/21.webp)</span>

Would it have been _possible_ to give another (description) instead of the description “I wanted to deceive you for a moment,” in which intention is not mentioned? A description that, as it were, analyzes the process of intention, without letting anything be _guessed_ that the word “intention” would have implied?

### <span class="fac">[21\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/21.webp)</span>

Well, to this it must be said that when the word “intention” comes to mind as I recall the event, that is rather something different than if, instead, the analysis of the intention comes to mind. I do not _first_ think of the analysis and then casually summarize it with the word “intention.”

### <span class="fac">[21\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/21.webp)</span>

I could also say, “I had the intention to speak,” without my mind being on the process of opening my mouth, etc., etc. My memory reaction was: “I had the intention.”

### <span class="fac">[21\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/21.webp)</span>

One can also say, “I made preparations to leave at that time,” and we are not conscious of all these preparations.

### <span class="fac">[21\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/21.webp),[22\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/22.webp)</span>

The narrator also says: “For a moment, the thought flashed through his mind to deceive him; then…” Is the narrator _interpreting_ here? Why would he do it?

### <span class="fac">[22\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/22.webp)</span>

But think of the dream. And consider it as a phenomenon of memory. And then imagine someone says, “Only I can know what I dreamed!”

### <span class="fac">[22\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/22.webp)</span>

“Only I can know what I think.” Now, of course, only I _say_ it. – But do I only know it when I say it? Well, knowledge is a state. And what is the criterion for it?

### <span class="fac">[22\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/22.webp)</span>

My memory reaction is: “I had the intention to deceive you”; _his_: “You seemed to have the intention to…”

### <span class="fac">[22\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/22.webp)</span>

But couldn’t _his_ memory reaction also be: “You wanted to deceive him”? Couldn’t he also be completely sure?

### <span class="fac">[23\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/23.webp)</span>

In all cases of such a memory, to speak of an _interpretation_ makes no sense, because then “interpretation” simply means the same as “memory.”

### <span class="fac">[23\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/23.webp)</span>

What, for example, is not contained in the arrangement of the words, or in the choice of a word for a _nearly_ synonymous one? A word, for example, alludes to a circle of ideas. Only someone who is at home in different circles of ideas will understand the joke of this allusion, etc. How do we understand this allusion? Do we immediately, when we read this word, take a detour into its circle of ideas? Well, we primarily react to the word with laughter or something else. If we think further about it or want to explain it to someone else, then we go into it more closely.

### <span class="fac">[23\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/23.webp)</span>

I believe that the main problem lies in the question: What criterion is there for whether the memory of my intention is correct or incorrect? If there is _no_ such criterion, then one can speak of an interpretation rather than a memory.

### <span class="fac">[23\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/23.webp),[24\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/24.webp)</span>

You got up and walked to the window. Later you say, “I got up to get away from the people.” Another person says: “You are only putting that in now. You just got up to stretch your limbs.”

### <span class="fac">[24\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/24.webp)</span>

But _what_ did I put in there? That elusive thing, the intention?

### <span class="fac">[24\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/24.webp)</span>

And why is it elusive? Toothache does not seem elusive to us in this sense. Or I could say: an intention seems to us to be even more elusive than pain. That is far too suspicious. It must be because we are tempted to apply a certain kind of description here and then abandon this attempt. (Something like in the case of “_this_ pain” and other cases.)

### <span class="fac">[24\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/24.webp),[25\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/25.webp)</span>

It is indeed true: We say, “I wanted to get away from these people.” We are conscious that this expression covers a lot of possible cases and that, on the other hand, it is not easy to describe the particular case. One kind of description would be, for example, to repeat the whole scene and reproduce my facial expression, gait, etc. I could remember some of the thoughts that accompanied my action. (Another person who saw this reproduction could say: “I assure you, you didn’t do it that way back then.”)

### <span class="fac">[25\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/25.webp)</span>

“I don’t remember my words anymore, but I do remember the intention with which I spoke them.” Another person says: “I can attest to that, you said at the time…”

Similarly, one can sometimes say: “I don’t remember my words anymore, but I do remember the spirit of my words.”

### <span class="fac">[25\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/25.webp)</span>

One could say that every narrative is not only a different narrative but also ‘narrative’ in a different sense.

### <span class="fac">[25\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/25.webp),[26\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/26.webp)</span>

One also says of an animal that it _wanted_ to escape. What it _did_ is precisely what one calls “wanting to escape.”

### <span class="fac">[26\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/26.webp)</span>

How is it that I am nevertheless inclined to see an interpretation in it when I say “I wanted to deceive you for a moment”? Is it because I interpret what happened in that moment through the environment and the history, i.e., characterize it together with it?

### <span class="fac">[26\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/26.webp),[27\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/27.webp)</span>

There is some truth in that.

But suppose I remembered (with a certain adjustment of the lenses) _one_ sensation – how can I say that it is what I call ‘intention’? It may be that a certain tingling sensation, for example, accompanied all my intentions. Do we learn the word intention the same way we learn the word for a sensation? What is the natural expression of intention? Look at a cat when it creeps up on a bird, or an animal that wants to escape.

### <span class="fac">[27\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/27.webp)</span>

“You had the intention yesterday to go to town.” “I didn’t have that intention.” “Well, I couldn’t have looked inside you, could I?” What would he have seen if he had looked inside me? Well, perhaps my thoughts. He could also have found my notebook in which I write down my secret thoughts (in which I, as it were, interpret them).

### <span class="fac">[27\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/27.webp)</span>

“Why are you packing?” “I intend to travel to… tomorrow.” Is the packing, together with other activities, the intention? Look in the drawer where you expect the intention – it is empty. _When_ do you have the intention? Always or intermittently? Look in the drawer where you expect it. The drawer is empty. You had looked for it among the sensations.

### <span class="fac">[27\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/27.webp)</span>

“I have the intention” tells you – casually – what you can expect.

### <span class="fac">[28\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/28.webp)</span>

If the other person can see that for a moment I was about to …, why shouldn’t _I_ know _it_? But if the other person could be mistaken about that, why shouldn’t I be too—_admittedly_, I know more than he does.

### <span class="fac">[28\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/28.webp)</span>

And can’t I be mistaken?

### <span class="fac">[28\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/28.webp),[29\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/29.webp)</span>

Someone wants to persuade me to give up a plan. I say, “That doesn’t convince me; I still intend to travel.” These words are an utterance of intention. Are they an utterance of a feeling? They are, in any case, the vehicle for a certain tone, and one could call that the expression of a feeling. But it is not always the same tone, and therefore not always the same feeling. On the other hand, it is a verbal reaction that refers to a future action by the speaker and not a conjecture about his action.

### <span class="fac">[29\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/29.webp)</span>

The sentence is similar to: “I will now get up.” This is not a prediction like “He will now get up” or “I will probably vomit now.” This ‘utterance of will’ belongs to a completely different language-game.

### <span class="fac">[29\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/29.webp)</span>

“I will now turn to him.” “I wanted to turn to him at that time, just as he suddenly stood up.”

### <span class="fac">[29\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/29.webp)</span>

“I will now turn to him” is of the type of sentence: “I will now raise my hand.” It would surprise everyone if, instead, I said: “My hand will now raise itself,” although this prediction would also be fulfilled if I raise my hand. Does the sentence “I will now…” say something that is so difficult to understand, and which the superficial layman can simply accept?

### <span class="fac">[29\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/29.webp),[30\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/30.webp),[30\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/30.webp)</span>

Consider the language-game: A commands B to make a movement; B makes it. A variation of this is: Make this movement (it is demonstrated, or has already been named) when I clap my hands. Another variation: Give yourself the command and carry it out when I clap my hands.

### <span class="fac">[30\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/30.webp)</span>

Consider these two language-games:

1) A commands B to make certain movements with arms and legs or to assume certain body positions (the gymnastics teacher and the students).

2) One observes certain regular processes (for example, the reactions of different metals to different acids) and then makes predictions about which reaction will occur between this substance and that.

### <span class="fac">[30\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/30.webp),[31\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/31.webp)</span>

A variation of this language-game is: The student commands himself to make the movement he will carry out, and carries it out, perhaps after a certain interval.

### <span class="fac">[31\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/31.webp)</span>

There is an obvious kinship and also a fundamental difference between these two language-games. In both, one could call the words a prediction. (A command is often given in the words: “You will now…”) But compare the training that belongs to the first technique with the training for the second!

### <span class="fac">[31\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/31.webp)</span>

I can bring the two cases even closer together externally by experimenting with my own body in the second one. (“I will now perform 3 squats; &amp; then my heart will beat rapidly.”) There is no explanation, however, if I say that in the first case I am the agent, while in the second I am merely the observer. Or: in the first case I would see the causal connection from the inside, in the second from the outside, &amp; _many_ similar things.

### <span class="fac">[31\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/31.webp)</span>

It is also not relevant to say that a ‘prediction’ of the kind “I will do squats” is as little always correct as a prediction of the second kind.

### <span class="fac">[31\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/31.webp),[32\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/32.webp)</span>

The fundamental difference lies in the fact that we make the two under entirely different circumstances. I had not observed myself &amp; now say “it seems I will now do 3 squats.” The statement “I will” was not based on the observation of a regularity; nor, of course, on the fact that most of the time when I want this, it happens. Nor do I want to say that in this case the prediction is the cause and its fulfilment the effect. (We’ll leave that for physiologists to puzzle over.) Although _there is_ something to that statement: we can indeed often predict people’s actions from their expressions of resolve. An important language-game.

### <span class="fac">[32\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/32.webp),[33\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/33.webp)</span>

But when you say, “I intend to leave,” you do mean it! Here, again, it is the mental intention that animates the sentence. If you utter the sentence as a language exercise, or if you repeat it to someone in order to imitate their way of speaking, then you utter it without that intention.

### <span class="fac">[33\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/33.webp)</span>

Yes, that seems to be the case when we philosophize. But consider various situations, conversations, etc., and how the sentence is uttered in them. Do you really discover a mental undertone there? And there is also the case in which I communicate my intention to someone, and at the same time (perhaps for fun) imitate the tone of another.

### <span class="fac">[33\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/33.webp),[34\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/34.webp)</span>

“I am toying with the intention of leaving.” From this, one would primarily infer what the other person is thinking and feeling. He might also add, “I can’t think of anything else right now.” (Description of a state of mind.) Compare this to: Someone asks me, “How long will you stay here?” I reply, “I’m leaving tomorrow; my vacation is ending.” On the other hand, at the end of an argument, I say, “If that’s how it is, I’m leaving tomorrow.” (I make a decision.)

### <span class="fac">[34\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/34.webp)</span>

“I had already opened my mouth to say it.”

### <span class="fac">[34\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/34.webp)</span>

To think oneself back into a situation. One recalls all the circumstances. When I was interrupted, I was about to say… “I don’t know what words I would have spoken, but I wanted to give him an argument of _this_ kind…” Did I have to have already formulated the argument? Perhaps like with a note.

### <span class="fac">[34\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/34.webp)</span>

“What did you want to say when you were interrupted (back then)?” – “I wanted to say something along the lines of…”

### <span class="fac">[34\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/34.webp),[35\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/35.webp)</span>

“You said ‘…’ back then, and then you were interrupted. Do you remember what you wanted to say?” If I now know it and say it, does that mean that I had already thought it earlier and just hadn’t _said_ it? No. Unless you take the certainty with which I continue the interrupted sentence as a criterion for the fact that the thought was already complete back then. But, of course, there was already all sorts of things in the situation and in my thoughts that helped to continue the sentence.

### <span class="fac">[35\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/35.webp)</span>

If I later continue the interrupted sentence and say _it the way_ I would have wanted to continue it back then; that is, if I continued it based on notes I had made at the time.

### <span class="fac">[35\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/35.webp)</span>

And am I not interpreting these notes? Was only _one_ continuation possible under those circumstances? Certainly not. But I didn’t choose one of these interpretations. I remembered that I wanted to say that.

### <span class="fac">[35\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/35.webp),[36\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

Because if I remember that I wanted to say that, I am certainly not remembering what would have happened if I had said, “I want to say…” However, a friend could have continued my interrupted sentence, without me knowing, and he says, “I know that you wanted to say _that_.”

### <span class="fac">[36\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

“Do you know that you wanted to say each word of the sentence _exactly_ like that?” – “No, but the course of the argument was already fixed in my mind.”

### <span class="fac">[36\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

“I wanted to set out towards…”

### <span class="fac">[36\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

If my train of thought seems obvious to another person, why shouldn’t it seem obvious to me?

### <span class="fac">[36\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

“I saw that you wanted to get away from there as quickly as possible.” The intention, the tendency, is to describe what someone is doing from a certain point of view.

### <span class="fac">[36\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/36.webp)</span>

I don’t mean: you are adding something that wasn’t there – because why would you do that? But you are describing it – perhaps _completely_ correctly – through a certain concept.

### <span class="fac">[37\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/37.webp)</span>

To say that I am interpreting when I remember what I wanted to say is actually false, because that would mean that I am constructing a possible interpretation around certain [unreadable] things. Whereas I cannot do anything other than follow this train of thought.

### <span class="fac">[37\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/37.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[37\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/37.webp)</span>

“Try to reach this house, whatever happens” characterizes a different language-game than: “Go 100 steps straight ahead, then turn right …, and then you will get to the house.” Similarly, “I am trying to get to this house” and “I walked the road that leads to the house” belong to different language-games.

### <span class="fac">[37\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/37.webp)</span>

What happens if I am looking for a specific book? How does it differ from the fact that I am looking for a different book, assuming that I don’t find what I’m looking for?

And now think of the case when one is interrupted while searching & then says: “What was it that I was looking for? Oh yes, it was …!”

I give someone an explanation but get stuck & say to give them a casual term: “I am aiming at this thought: …”. This is similar to when I say “I am looking for the book…”.

The command “Look for the book…!” is not the same as “Read the titles of all these books!”. But in both cases, following the command might be the same action.

### <span class="fac">[38\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/38.webp)</span>

“I am looking for this book” does not mean the same as “I am reading the titles of all these books”, although I might be doing just that. ### <span class="fac">[38\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/38.webp)</span>

Looking for book A is not the same command as “Look for book B”. But what I do in following these two commands might be exactly the same.

I can give a series of different descriptions of what happened. Select different things & summarize them. Describe it from different perspectives through different concepts. “I am striving to get there by all means” characterizes what happens in a certain way. From [Man on the steep street] a certain perspective… it gives a certain picture of the events, brings out certain features of the events.

Even if you somehow _see_ that intention is a kind of sensation—why should you believe that the same intention is always the same sensation? One might imagine that in an army, on certain occasions the general must wear a uniform that the lieutenant wears on other occasions, and vice versa. (The general should, for example, appear on certain occasions as the servant of the entire army, and on others as its master.)

### <span class="fac">[40\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/40.webp)</span>

The language-game: “Strive to get there”. (Training for it.)

### <span class="fac">[40\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/40.webp)</span>

Whoever plays this game says to himself or to another: “I _must_ reach the house!” If I say it to myself in this way, it is a verbal reaction like “It _must_ work!” Or an explanation of my way of acting for the other person, a request for help, etc.

### <span class="fac">[40\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/40.webp)</span>

In this book, I publish philosophical remarks that I have written down over the past 16 years. They concern a great variety of subjects…

### <span class="fac">[40\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/40.webp)</span>

This work is now a conglomerate of such remarks.

### <span class="fac">[40\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/40.webp),[41\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/41.webp)</span>

I finally had to put them in some kind of order, even if this is only loose in many cases, and this is how the conglomerate that I am publishing here came about.

Nevertheless, it was necessary to arrange my remarks in some way, even if…

### <span class="fac">[41\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/41.webp)</span>

These remarks are thus, as it were, pictures taken on a long & winding journey through a vast terrain. These remarks are thus, as it were, a collection of landscape sketches that arose on these long & winding journeys through a vast terrain.

These remarks are thus, as it were, depictions of a landscape that were drawn on long & winding journeys.

### <span class="fac">[42\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-128/42.webp)</span>

In what follows, I want to publish some of the philosophical remarks that I have written over the last 16 years. They concern: the concept of meaning, understanding, the sentence, logic, the foundations of mathematics, sense data & much else. Originally, I wrote all my thoughts about these objects as _remarks_, short paragraphs.
