### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#2r.3) <span class="fac">[2r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/2r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-2r3}

April 24, 1950

The fact that our evidence only makes the experience of another probable does not lead us very far; but the fact that this difficult-to-describe pattern of experience is an important piece of evidence for us does.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#2r.4+2v.1) <span class="fac">[2r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/2v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-2r42v1}

The fact that this fluctuating thing is an important part of our lives. But how can one even say that something is _fluctuating_? What do I measure its fluctuation against? Well, there are countless forms of smiling, for example. And smiling, which is a smile, & smiling, which is not a smile.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#2v.2) <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/2v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-2v2}

What do we take note of in life? – “...He smiled.” – that can be infinitely important. But _must_ a slight contortion of the face be important? And must it be so because of the probable practical consequences?

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#2v.3) <span class="fac">[2v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/2v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-2v3}

“What is going on in him, he _cannot_ know.” But he can still suspect it. – So he can only not _know_ it. So we are only making a distinction in the use of the word “know.”

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#3r.1) <span class="fac">[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/3r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-3r1}

But does the astronomer, who calculates a lunar eclipse, say that one cannot naturally _know_ the future? We say it when we feel uncertain about the future. The farmer says it with regard to the weather; but the carpenter does not say that one cannot know whether his chairs will break.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#3r.2+3v.1) <span class="fac">[3r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/3v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-3r23v1}

“I know that he was pleased to see me.” _What_ do I know? What are the consequences of this fact? I feel secure in my relationship with him. But is that knowledge? What is the difference between suspecting & knowing that he was pleased? If I know it, I will assert it without any signs of doubt; & others will understand this statement. Well, it does have certain practical consequences; something can be inferred from it, but that seems to be only its shadow. What is the interest of his inner state of joy?

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#3v.2) <span class="fac">[3v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/3v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-3v2}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4r.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4r1}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4r.2) <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4r2}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4r.3) <span class="fac">[4r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4r3}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4r.4+4v.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4r44v1}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4v.2) <span class="fac">[4v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4v2}

First question: how does one know, how does one judge whether one’s inner face is smiling? Second question: what importance does it have? – But the two are connected. And one could pose a _different_, albeit related, question in this way: what importance does one’s – _outer_ – smile have? For if the inner is of importance, then the outer must also be of importance – in a (somewhat) different way.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#4v.3) <span class="fac">[4v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/4v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-4v3}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#5r.3) <span class="fac">[5r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/5r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-5r3}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#5v.1) <span class="fac">[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/5v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-5v1}

“I am sure that he was pleased to see me,” this could be said in a courtroom. The possible ‘practical’ consequences are clear here. And so too if the statement were, “I am sure he was not pleased, but pretended to be.” Different things are to be expected from someone who is pleased and from someone who feigns pleasure.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#5v.3+6r.1) <span class="fac">[5v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/5v.webp),[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-5v36r1}

But is it really important to me that the other person is happy to see me, because it has other consequences? I feel good because _this_ person (with _this_ past, etc.) behaves _in this way_. And that ‘_in this way_’ is, of course, a very complicated pattern.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#6r.2) <span class="fac">[6r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-6r2}

If one does not want to _solve_ philosophical problems, why not give up trying to deal with them? For to solve them means to change one’s point of view, the old way of thinking. And if you do not want to do that, then you should call the problems unsolvable.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#6r.3+6v.1) <span class="fac">[6r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-6r36v1}

It is always assumed that the one who is smiling is a human being, not just that what is smiling is a human body. Certain circumstances & connections of the smile with other forms of behaviour are also assumed. But if all of that is assumed, then the smile of the other person is pleasing to me. If I ask someone on the street for directions, I prefer a friendly answer to an unfriendly one. I react immediately to the behaviour of the other person. I assume the _inner_ aspect insofar as I assume a _human being_.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#6v.2) <span class="fac">[6v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-6v2}

The ‘inner’ is an illusion. That is to say: the whole complex of ideas that this suggests is like a painted curtain drawn in front of the actual application of the words.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#6v.3+7r.1) <span class="fac">[6v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/7r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-6v37r1}

It seems to me: if one cannot actually _know_ whether someone is annoyed (for example), then one cannot actually believe or suspect it either.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#7r.2) <span class="fac">[7r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/7r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-7r2}

Isn't it true that what I can ‘be sure’ of, I can also ‘know’?

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#7r.3) <span class="fac">[7r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/7r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-7r3}

Wouldn’t it be ridiculous if a lawyer in the courtroom said that the boy couldn’t _know_ that so-and-so was angry, because anger is something inner? – Then one also couldn’t know whether hanging is a punishment.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#7r.4) <span class="fac">[7r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/7r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-7r4}

Whoever says “_that_ one cannot know” makes a distinction between language-games. He says: In _such_ language-games there is knowledge, in _such_ ones there is not. And with that he limits the concept of ‘knowledge’.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#7v.1) <span class="fac">[7v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/7v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-7v1}

This restriction could be useful if it emphasized an important difference that our ordinary linguistic usage overlooks. But I don’t think that’s the case.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#8r.2) <span class="fac">[8r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/8r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-8r2}

But isn't mathematical certainty greater than any physical certainty, and all the more so than the certainty about what the other person is feeling?

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#8v.1) <span class="fac">[8v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/8v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-8v1}

And can’t one express the greater certainty of mathematics in the same way: that there is knowledge in mathematics?

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#8v.2) <span class="fac">[8v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/8v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-8v2}

In mathematics, a certain, clearly identifiable evidence leaves no room for doubt. This is not the case when we know that someone was pleased. Whether a calculation yields this or that can quickly lead to a dispute in the courtroom; but whether someone was angry or not. But does it follow that one can know one thing, but not the other? Rather, it is that in one case one knows the outcome almost always, in the other case one _often_ does not know it.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#8v.3+9r.1) <span class="fac">[8v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/8v.webp),[9r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/9r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-8v39r1}

If one says that one never knows whether the other person actually felt that way, then not because he may in reality have felt differently, but because, as it were, even God cannot _know_ that he felt that way.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#9r.2+9v.1) <span class="fac">[9r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/9r.webp),[9v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/9v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-9r29v1}

For example, I am convinced that my friend was pleased to see me. But now, while philosophizing, I tell myself that it might be different; perhaps he was only pretending. But immediately I tell myself that, even if he admitted this himself, I would not necessarily be sure that he is not mistaken, that he knows himself. So in the whole game there is an uncertainty. One could say: in a game in which the rules are uncertain, one _cannot_ know who has won and who has lost.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#9v.2) <span class="fac">[9v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/9v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-9v2}

There is a ‘why’ for which the answer does not allow predictions. This is the case, for example, with animistic explanations. Many of Freud’s explanations, or Goethe’s in the Theory of Colors, are of this kind. The explanation gives us an analogy. And now the phenomenon is no longer isolated, but connected with others.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#9v.3+10r.1) <span class="fac">[9v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/9v.webp),[10r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-9v310r1}

When someone ‘pretends to be friendly and finally shows or confesses his true feelings’, we usually don’t think about doubting this confession and saying that we can’t know what is really going on in him. Rather, _certainty_ now seems to have been achieved.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#10r.2) <span class="fac">[10r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-10r2}

This is important: I may know, from certain signs and my knowledge of a person, that he is pleased, etc. But I cannot describe my observations to a third person, and if he trusts him, convince him of the authenticity of that joy, etc.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#10r.3) <span class="fac">[10r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-10r3}

One says of an emotional expression, “It looks genuine.” And what would be the point of that if there were not _convincing_ criteria for authenticity? One cannot say, “It seems genuine,” if one cannot also say, “It _is_ genuine.”

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#10v.2) <span class="fac">[10v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-10v2}

“This crying makes a real impression” – so there is a _real_ crying. So there is a criterion for it. “But not a certain one!”

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#10v.3) <span class="fac">[10v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-10v3}

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#10v.4+11r.1) <span class="fac">[10v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/10v.webp),[11r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/11r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-10v411r1}

But does not acknowledging no certain criterion mean: never being certain that the other feels so & so? Can I not be quite certain, & yet not acknowledge a certain criterion? I am (behave) certain & perhaps do not know _why_ I am.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#11r.2) <span class="fac">[11r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/11r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-11r2}

What would it look like if all people were always uncertain about the feelings of others? They would apparently always be somewhat doubtful, always making some doubtful facial expression or gesture while pitying others, etc. – But if we now leave out this _constant_ gesture, _because_ it is constant, what behaviour remains? Perhaps just a cool, or indifferent, superficially interested one? But then we don't need to interpret their behaviour again as an expression of doubt. – So it means nothing that _all are always_‥‥

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#11r.3) <span class="fac">[11r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/11r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-11r3}

There is uncertainty & there is certainty; but it does not follow that there are certain criteria.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#11v.1) <span class="fac">[11v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/11v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-11v1}

How if someone now says: “I know that he is happy” means nothing other than: I am certain of his happiness, so again: _I_ react to him in this way & that, without uncertainty. It would then be approximately the same as: “I know that everything is for the best” – the expression of my own statement about whatever it is. And here there would be reason to say that this is not really knowledge. But even in the courtroom, no one would be convinced by the latter statement that everything is for the best.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#11v.2+12r.1) <span class="fac">[11v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/11v.webp),[12r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-11v212r1}

And here lies something important: the statement “I know that he is happy” would not be regarded as more than: “I have the certain impression that he is happy” even in the courtroom. The case would not be the same as when a physicist testifies that he made this experiment & it gave _this_ result; or as when a mathematician makes a statement about a calculation. – If I have known the other person for a long time, the court will probably also accept my statement, give it weight. But my absolute certainty will not mean _knowledge_ to him. Because he must be able to draw quite specific conclusions from knowledge.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#12r.2) <span class="fac">[12r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-12r2}

And one cannot object: “_I_ draw certain conclusions from my knowledge, even if no one else can” – because _conclusions_ must apply to everyone.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#12r.3+12v.1) <span class="fac">[12r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12r.webp),[12v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-12r312v1}

The connection between the evidence & that for which it is evidence is not necessarily the case here. & I mean _not_: “the connection of the external with the internal”.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#12v.2) <span class="fac">[12v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-12v2}

One could even say: the uncertainty about the inner is an uncertainty about something external.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#12v.3) <span class="fac">[12v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-12v3}

If “I know…” means: I can convince the other person if he believes the evidence, then one can say: I may be as certain about his mood as about the truth of a mathematical proposition, but it is still wrong to say that I _know_ his mood. (But it is still wrong to say: knowledge is a different state of mind than being certain. (I am a different person than L.W.))

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#12v.4+13r.1) <span class="fac">[12v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/12v.webp),[13r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/13r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-12v413r1}

That is: ‘knowing’ is a psychological concept of a different kind than ‘being certain’, ‘being convinced’, ‘believing’, ‘guessing’, etc.. The evidence for knowledge is of a different kind.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#13r.2) <span class="fac">[13r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/13r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-13r2}

Russell's example: “I know that the current Prime Minister is bald”; the one who says it is certain, because he wrongly believes that X is Prime Minister; but also the current one is bald & so his _statement_ is true & yet he does not _know_ that it is.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#13r.3) <span class="fac">[13r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/13r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-13r3}

“I _know_ that it is so” is indeed an expression of my complete certainty, but it still follows that something else follows from it than my certainty.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#13r.4) <span class="fac">[13r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/13r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-13r4}

Firstly, “I cannot know his feelings” naturally does _not_ mean: …in contrast to _my_ feelings. Secondly, it does not mean: I can never be completely certain about his feelings.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#13v.1) <span class="fac">[13v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/13v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-13v1}

Statement: “I know that the bottle stood there.” – “How do you know that?” – “I saw it there.” – Now, if the statement is: “I know that he was happy,” & one asks “How do you know that?” – What is the answer? It is not simply the description of a physical fact. For example, it includes the fact that I know the person in question. If a film could be shown in the courtroom in which the entire scene was reproduced, his facial expressions, his gestures, his voice, it could sometimes be very convincing. At least if he is not an actor. But it only works, for example, if those who judge the scene belong to the same culture. For example, I would not know what genuine joy looks like in Chinese people.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#14r.1) <span class="fac">[14r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/14r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-14r1}

We are not focusing on the fact that one cannot _know_ what another experiences, that an experience is in some sense the secret of the one who has it, but on the rules of evidence in general, which relate to experiences.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#14r.2) <span class="fac">[14r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/14r.webp)</span> {#ms-174-14r2}

It is important, for example, that one must ‘_know_’ a person in order to be able to judge what meaning should be assigned to an expression of feeling from him, & that one cannot describe what one knows about him. Equally important is that one cannot say in what the essential observable consequences of an inner state consist. If he was really happy, for example, what is to be expected of him, & what is not? There are, of course, such characteristic consequences, but they cannot be described in the same way as the reactions that characterize the state of a physical object.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#14v.1) <span class="fac">[14v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/14v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-14v1}

One must also consider this: Authenticity & inauthenticity are not the only essential characteristics of an expression of feeling.

For example, one cannot say whether a cat that purrs & then scratches is being insincere. It could be that a person gives signs of joy & then behaves in a completely unexpected way, & that we still cannot say that the first expression was not genuine.

### [Ms-174](/ms-174/#14v.2) <span class="fac">[14v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/14v.webp)</span> {#ms-174-14v2}

It seems to me that it is as uncertain that there can only be genuine or feigned expressions of feeling, as it is that there can only be major or minor keys.
