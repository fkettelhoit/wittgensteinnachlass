## Volume “S”

### <span class="fac">[1a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span>

January 15, 1949

I was ill this week. Had the flu. I still can't work now, & who knows if I'll be able to again.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.2) <span class="fac">[1a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a2}

I want to call the aspects of ‘black cross’, ‘white cross’ the main aspects of the double cross, for short. Likewise, let us speak of the two main aspects of the stage. There is a fundamental difference between them & the aspect of the triangle, for example, as an inverted triangle.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.3) <span class="fac">[1a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a3}

The difference lies in the description for the communication of the aspect.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.4) <span class="fac">[1a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a4}

The aspect experiences have in common the form of expression: “I see it now as _this_” or “I see it now like this” or “Now it is _this_ – now _this_” or “I hear it now as …; before I heard it as …”. But the explanation of this ‘_this_’ & ‘_like this_’ is of a very different kind in different cases.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.5) <span class="fac">[1a\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a5}

Seeing the triangle as half a parallelogram requires imagination; seeing the main aspects of the double cross does not.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.6) <span class="fac">[1a\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a6}

These seem to be of a more fundamental nature than that.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1a.7) <span class="fac">[1a\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1a.webp)</span> {#1a7}

Only someone who has the shape of those animals can ‘see the H. & E. aspect’; the main aspects of the double cross could manifest in the primitive reactions of a child who cannot yet speak.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1b.1) <span class="fac">[1b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1b.webp)</span> {#1b1}

– – – These two aspects _A_ (I want to call them the aspects _A_) could, for example, be communicated simply by the observer alternately pointing to a free-standing white cross & a free-standing black cross. Yes, one could imagine that this would be a primitive reaction of a child who cannot yet speak. In the communication of the aspects A, a part of the double cross figure is therefore pointed out. The H. & E. aspect could not be described in an analogous way.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1b.2) <span class="fac">[1b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1b.webp)</span> {#1b2}

Only of the one will we say that he sees the H. & E. aspects, who has the shape of those two animals. Analogous conditions do not exist for the aspects A.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1b.3) <span class="fac">[1b\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1b.webp)</span> {#1b3}

Someone can take the H. & E. head for the image of a rabbit, the double cross for the image of a black cross, but not the mere triangular figure for the image of something inverted. Imagination is needed to see this aspect of the triangle.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1b.4) <span class="fac">[1b\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1b.webp)</span> {#1b4}

Whoever is deceived by the cube schema, so that he _takes it for_ a cube, primarily sees it as this cube, whether he later tries to see it differently, & whether he succeeds in doing so. (Comparison with the double cross.)

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-1b.5+2a.1) <span class="fac">[1b\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/1b.webp),[2a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2a.webp)</span> {#1b52a1}

The aspects A are not essentially spatial aspects. A black cross on a white background is not necessarily a black cross that lies on a white surface. One could teach him this concept by only showing him black crosses painted on paper; provided that the environment of these crosses changes & the cross is the _important_ thing in what is perceived: If, for example, it is copied, then always, or primarily, the cross is copied, etc.. The aspects A are not related in the same way to a _deception_ as the spatial aspects of the cube schema.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2a.2) <span class="fac">[2a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2a.webp)</span> {#2a2}

January 16, 1949

From any script – this one, for example <math display="inline"><mtext>𝖄</mtext></math> – I can imagine that it is a perfectly correctly written letter of some foreign alphabet. – Or, it is a poorly written letter; & in a way that is one of several different ways: e.g. hastily, or typically childish – clumsy, or bureaucratically ornate. It could deviate from the correctly written in various ways. – & depending on the fiction with which I surround it, I can see it in different aspects. – Here there is a close kinship with the experience of the meaning of an isolated word.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-2a.3) <span class="fac">[2a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2a.webp)</span> {#2a3}

January 17, 1949

There are indeed cases in which the sense of what one wants to say is much clearer to one than one is able to express it in words. (This happens to me very often.) It is then as if one clearly remembered a dream, but could not tell it well. Yes, the image often remains permanently behind the words for the writer (me), so that they _seem_ to describe it _for me_.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-2a.4+2b.1) <span class="fac">[2a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2a.webp),[2b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2a42b1}

A mediocre writer must be careful not to replace a rough, incorrect expression too quickly with a correct one. In doing so, he kills the initial idea, which was still a living seedling. And now it is withered & _completely_ worthless. One can now throw it on the compost heap. Whereas the pitiful little seedling still had some value.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2b.2) <span class="fac">[2b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2b2}

“I noticed the similarity between him & his father for perhaps 5 minutes.” One can say this if his face changes & only resembled his father’s for those 5 minutes. But it can also mean: the similarity to his father only struck me for a few minutes, after which I forgot it.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2b.3) <span class="fac">[2b\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2b3}

“It no longer strikes me” – but what happens when it strikes me? – Well, I look at the face, with an expression of astonishment, perhaps, in my mien, maybe also in words. – But is that the striking of the similarity? No; these are the appearances of the striking, but these _are_ ‘what happens’. ‘Striking’ is a different concept. –

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2b.4) <span class="fac">[2b\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2b4}

‘Thinking’ & ‘speaking in the imagination’ (I don’t say: “talking to oneself”) are different concepts.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2b.5) <span class="fac">[2b\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2b5}

Is the striking looking & thinking? No. Many concepts _intersect_ here.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-2b.6) <span class="fac">[2b\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/2b.webp)</span> {#2b6}

“If one did not _experience_ the meaning of words, how could one laugh at puns?” [Hairdresser & sculptor.] – One laughs at such jokes: & in that sense, one could say that one experiences the meaning.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3a.1) <span class="fac">[3a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3a.webp)</span> {#3a1}

Just think of the words that lovers speak to one another! They are ‘charged’ with feelings. And they are certainly not – as technical terms are – to be replaced by any other sounds according to an agreement. Isn’t this because they are _gestures_? And a gesture does not have to be innate; it is learned, but precisely _assimilated_. – But isn’t that a myth?! – No. For the characteristics of assimilation are precisely that I use _this_ word & prefer to use none rather than one that is imposed; & similar reactions.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3a.2) <span class="fac">[3a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3a.webp)</span> {#3a2}

A word has, for example, become the carrier of a _tone_ for us; & I cannot, on command, utter another word in the same felt tone.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-3a.3) <span class="fac">[3a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3a.webp)</span> {#3a3}

January 18, 1949

The fading of writers who were, after all, _something_, is connected with the fact that their writings, supplemented by the entire environment of their time, speak strongly to people, but that without this supplementation they perish, as it were, deprived of the illumination that gave them color. And with this, I believe, is connected the beauty of mathematical demonstrations, as Pascal himself perceived it. In _this_ view of the world, these demonstrations had _beauty_ – not what superficial people call beauty. Also, a crystal is not beautiful in every ‘environment’ – although perhaps it is _appealing_ in every one.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-3a.4) <span class="fac">[3a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3a.webp)</span> {#3a4}

How entire epochs cannot free themselves from the constraints of certain concepts – of the concept of ‘beautiful & beauty’, for example.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3b.1) <span class="fac">[3b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3b.webp)</span> {#3b1}

The similarity (for example) strikes me, – & the striking ceases. It only struck me for a few minutes, then no longer.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3b.2) <span class="fac">[3b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3b.webp)</span> {#3b2}

What happened then? First, I looked at the face with a peculiar expression in my own, & if someone had asked me, “Why are you looking at him so intently?”, I would have answered, “Because he looks _so_ much like his father.” Perhaps he is speaking to me & I don’t even pay attention to what he is saying, because I am only thinking about this similarity. – That is approximately what occurs to me in answer to the question, what happened.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3b.3) <span class="fac">[3b\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3b.webp)</span> {#3b3}

But in this answer, there is a heterogeneous element: “& if someone had asked me …”. That is not something that ‘happened’ when the similarity struck me. – Yes, my distraction is also not of the same kind as my facial expression. – So only my mien, gestures, perhaps words that I say to myself or others, remain.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3b.4) <span class="fac">[3b\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3b.webp)</span> {#3b4}

The striking is related to thinking.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-3b.5+4a.1) <span class="fac">[3b\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/3b.webp),[4a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#3b54a1}

What happened there? – Whose face do I remember? My own _facial expression_ comes to mind; I could imitate it. If someone who knows me had seen my face, they would have said, “Something about his face has struck you.” – Words also come to mind that I might utter aloud, or to myself, on such an occasion. And that is all. And is that what it means to be struck by something? No.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-4a.2) <span class="fac">[4a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#4a2}

My own thinking about art & values is far more disillusioned than that of people 100 years ago _could_ have been. And yet, that does not mean that it is therefore more correct. It simply means that the focus of my mind is on collapses that were not in the _focus_ of theirs.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4a.3) <span class="fac">[4a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#4a3}

To notice, to pay attention, to be attentive.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4a.4) <span class="fac">[4a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#4a4}

19.01.1949

“Do _you_ see this leaf as always _green_, as long as you see it, & does its color not change for you?” Does this question have a clear sense? An answer to it might be: “Well, I don’t keep saying to myself all the time ‘Oh, how _green_!’”.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4a.5) <span class="fac">[4a\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#4a5}

“Are you _conscious_ of its color all the time?” First, I would like to say: “Certainly not!” But when & for how long am I conscious of it? It seems I cannot say anything sensible about that; I do not know what criteria should be applied. Should I say: “Only as long as I _think_ of it”?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4a.6) <span class="fac">[4a\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp)</span> {#4a6}

Someone tells me: “I looked at the flower, but I was thinking about something else & was not conscious of its color.” Do I understand that? – I can imagine a meaningful connection to it; it would continue something like: “Then suddenly I _saw_ it & realized that it was the one that…”.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4a.7+4b.1) <span class="fac">[4a\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4a.webp),[4b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span> {#4a74b1}

But what about this answer: “If I had turned away at that time and someone had asked me what color it was, I wouldn’t have known”? “He looked at him without seeing him.” That happens. But what is the criterion for that? There are just different cases.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4b.2) <span class="fac">[4b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span> {#4b2}

“I’m looking more at the shape now than at the color.” Don’t let yourself be confused by such turns of phrase. Above all, don’t think, “What might be happening in the eye or in the brain?”

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-4b.3) <span class="fac">[4b\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span> {#4b3}

Worries are like illnesses; one must accept them: the worst thing one can do is to resist them. They also come in attacks, triggered by internal or external causes. And one must then say to oneself: “Another attack.”

### <span class="fac">[4b\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span>

I am in pain. Should I take medicine again, or not; should I undergo a major operation, or not? It is often difficult to decide. Often one can only _wait_, & in case of doubt, waiting is the hardest thing.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4b.5) <span class="fac">[4b\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span> {#4b5}

“A thought echoing in seeing” – one might say.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-4b.6) <span class="fac">[4b\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/4b.webp)</span> {#4b6}

“The word has an atmosphere” – a figurative expression; but quite understandable in certain contexts. For example: The word “Sabel” has a different atmosphere than the word “Säbel”. They have the same _meaning_, insofar as both are names for the same kind of objects. But what is one to say here? Do they have different meanings?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.1) <span class="fac">[5a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a1}

Should I now distinguish this many types of meaning? I do _not_ want to do that. Such a classification can be useful for a specific practical purpose. Because for such a purpose, one – out of the countless possible classifications – would be better than another.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.2) <span class="fac">[5a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a2}

The botanist classifies plants. But to show the infinite variety of plants and the diversity of the fine transitions, no classification is needed.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.3) <span class="fac">[5a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a3}

I saw the face clearly (in front of me) as before, – but I no longer noticed the similarity to that other face.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.4) <span class="fac">[5a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a4}

It could also be that one similarity faded for me and another came to my consciousness.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.5) <span class="fac">[5a\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a5}

Let’s suppose – as a hypothetical construct – that certain memories become more or less vivid while I look at his face, & that _this_ is responsible for the change of aspect. Should I still say that I _see_ now this, now that?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.6) <span class="fac">[5a\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a6}

_Is_ the noticing of similarity a seeing, or is it not? How am I to decide? They are unequal here, but related concepts.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.7) <span class="fac">[5a\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp)</span> {#5a7}

20.01.1949

One perceives an internal relation through noticing the aspect, & yet it is related to _imagining_.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5a.8+5b.1) <span class="fac">[5a\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5a.webp),[5b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5a85b1}

21.01.1949

Only someone who has learned & mastered that, and _can_ do it, has any reason to say that he has experienced something.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5b.2) <span class="fac">[5b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b2}

– – –Does one now perceive the timidity, or does one not? With the concept of ‘timid,’ one can describe what is visually perceived, just as with the concepts of ‘major’ or ‘minor,’ one can describe what is heard.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-138-5b.3) <span class="fac">[5b\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b3}

Scholarly questions may interest me, but they never truly captivate me. Only _conceptual_ & _aesthetic_ questions do that for me. The solution to scientific problems is, in principle, indifferent to me; but not those other questions.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5b.4) <span class="fac">[5b\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b4}

How could one see that the facial expression is mean, fearful, cruel, if one did not know that this is an _expression_, not the anatomy of the essence?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5b.5) <span class="fac">[5b\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b5}

But does that not simply mean that I could not apply _these_ concepts, which do not only relate to the visual, to the description of what is _seen_? Could I not still have a _purely_ visual concept, let's say, of a frightened face? (I could then use a different word.)

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5b.6) <span class="fac">[5b\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b6}

I must already _know_ quite a lot in order to be able to describe a text as “childish”. But can I also say: “in order to be able to _see_ it as ‘childish’”?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-5b.7) <span class="fac">[5b\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/5b.webp)</span> {#5b7}

“Childish” can describe a script, i.e., what I see, but ‘childish’ is not a purely visual concept.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.1) <span class="fac">[6a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a1}

Is it now correct to say: “We _could_ have a purely visual concept, which coincides entirely with the visual part of the concept ‘common’ (e.g.)”?

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.2) <span class="fac">[6a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a2}

Such a concept would then truly be comparable to the concepts ‘major’ and ‘minor’, which also have an emotional value, but which can also only be used to describe the structure of what is perceived.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.3) <span class="fac">[6a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a3}

‘Major’ & ‘minor’ is therefore compared here with ‘oblique’ & ‘right-angled’, for example.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.4) <span class="fac">[6a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a4}

But wouldn't it also be correct to say that whoever does not have our concept of ‘timid’, ‘childish’, ‘mean’, could not experience the writing, the facial expression, in the same way as we do, even if he has a concept that is always applicable wherever ‘timid’ is, for example? Couldn't I say: The two of them _see_ the same thing, but _experience_ it differently? Just as they can both hear something, but experience it differently.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.5) <span class="fac">[6a\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a5}

Just think of the expression “I heard a _mournful_ melody”! And now the question: “Does he _hear_ the lament?”

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6a.6) <span class="fac">[6a\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6a.webp)</span> {#6a6}

And if I were to answer: “No, he doesn’t hear it; he only experiences it” – what would that accomplish? One cannot even specify a sensory organ for this ‘experience’. Some might answer: “Of course I hear it!” – Others: “I don’t actually _hear_ it.” – However, distinctions in concepts can be identified.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6b.1) <span class="fac">[6b\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6b.webp)</span> {#6b1}

(One can draw a boundary for a concept. But where does the idea of ‘experiencing’ the common, fearful, etc. come from?) (Now,) we _react_ differently to the hesitant facial expression than to the one who does not recognize it as hesitant (in the _full_ sense of the word). – Now, I do _not_ want to say that we _sense_ this reaction in the muscles & joints. – No, we have here a modified **experience** concept.

### [LW](/w-lw/#ms-138-6b.2) <span class="fac">[6b\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-138/6b.webp)</span> {#6b2}
