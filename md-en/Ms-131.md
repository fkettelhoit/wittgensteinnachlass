# Ms-131

### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/1.webp)</span>

10.08.1946

Why does one say “He knows what he means”? How does one know that he _knows_ it? If he knows it, but I don’t _know_ what he means, what if I were to know it? Yes, what if I knew it, but he didn’t? How would one have to behave so that we would say: “He _knows_ what the other person is experiencing”?

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/1.webp),[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/2.webp)</span>

But must there be a case that we would, consistently, describe in this way? It is not even clear that any phenomenon would have to be described with the words “A is in pain in B’s body.” That is, one can say “Wouldn’t that be a logically correct application of this expression!” but I may, or may not, be inclined to call it logically correct.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-131-2.2) <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/2.webp)</span> {#22}

One can say that Schubert’s melodies are full of _points_, and one cannot say that of Mozart’s; Schubert is baroque. One can point to certain passages in a Schubert melody and say: look, that is the point of this melody, here the thought comes to a head.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-131-2.3+3.1) <span class="fac">[2\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/2.webp),[3\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/3.webp)</span> {#2331}

One can apply this principle of consideration to the melodies of the various composers: each type of tree is ‘tree’ in a different _sense_. That is, don’t be misled by the fact that one says that all of these are melodies. They are stages on a path that leads from something that you would not call a melody to something that you would not call a melody either. If one only looks at the sequences of notes and the changes of key, then all these formations do indeed appear in coordination. But if you look at the field in which they stand (i.e., their meaning), you will be inclined to say: here the melody is something quite different than there (it has a different origin here, plays a different role, etc.).

### <span class="fac">[3\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/3.webp),[4\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/4.webp)</span>

“When you started talking, I thought you meant…” In this utterance, one refers to the past. – Is this perhaps incorrect? – Certainly not. Just as little as when one tells a story: “I dreamed…”

### <span class="fac">[4\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/4.webp)</span>

One also says: “You understood me right from the start. I could see it on your face.”

### <span class="fac">[4\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/4.webp)</span>

“The same thing happened to you that happened to me!” The same _what_? Red and green are not the same _color_, but we could have a concept X that encompasses both of them.

### <span class="fac">[4\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/4.webp),[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/5.webp)</span>

Remember especially the expression in the dream narrative: “And I knew that…” One might think: it is rather strange that one can dream that one _knew_. One also says: “and I knew in the dream that…”

### <span class="fac">[5\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/5.webp),[6\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/6.webp)</span>

Not everything I do, I do with an intention. (I whistle to myself, etc., etc.) But if I were to get up now and go out the front door and come back in, and in answer to the question “Why did you do that” answered “For no particular reason,” or “Just like that,” then that would seem strange. And someone who often did something like that without a particular intention would deviate greatly from the norm. Would he have to be what one calls “mentally deficient”?

### <span class="fac">[6\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/6.webp),[7\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/7.webp)</span>

Now imagine someone who, one would say, could never remember an intention except by remembering the utterance of an intention. Someone could do what we normally do ‘with a certain intention’ without such an intention, but it would still prove useful. And we might perhaps say in this case that he acted with _unconscious_ intention. For example, he suddenly climbs onto a chair and then climbs down again. When asked “Why?” he has no answer; but then he reports that from the chair he noticed this and that, so that it seems as if he had climbed up to observe this.

### <span class="fac">[7\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/7.webp)</span>

Could a ‘meaning-blind’ person not behave similarly?

### <span class="fac">[7\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/7.webp)</span>

Where we say “I thought at the time that you meant…” he sometimes says “When you said that, the image of … floated before me,” and now he _draws_ the image. And of course, such an image can also serve to explain his behavior when hearing the words.

### <span class="fac">[8\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/8.webp)</span>

He would, for example, say: “At that time, this image floated before me” and draw it, and then: “It looks similar to this and that.” And now perhaps an explanation can easily be found as to why at this moment an image occurred to him that looks similar to… (Here, therefore, conscious would again be replaced by unconscious.)

### <span class="fac">[8\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/8.webp),[9\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/9.webp)</span>

“It is beautiful,” “ugly,” “good,” and “bad” are better propaganda than “Do this!” “Don’t do that!” “The modern person sits _like this_” (Loos). “That _one_ does not do!” “…is good” is _objective_. And what does that mean? What is the prototype of the objective? What does one compare the ethical statement to when one calls it “objective”?

### <span class="fac">[9\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/9.webp)</span>

“Well, ‘objective’ simply means: independent of us.” And what kind of dependence does one think of here? “‘That is good’ is good” is not a matter of taste. Certainly; but _in what_ lies the difference? Is the answer simply that good is objective? One wants to say: “Good” is like “red,” not like “pleasant.” Well, but in what respect is “good” like “red”? Because in _some_ respect, the concept ‘good’ is more similar to the concept ‘pleasant.’

### <span class="fac">[9\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/9.webp),[10\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/10.webp),[11\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/11.webp)</span>

Consider the two cases: 1) I give someone an ambiguous command (e.g., “go to the bank!”). He makes preparations to obey it in one sense; I challenge him; he now understands me and says, “I thought you meant….”

2) I tell someone something, and he says, “I first thought you were talking about….” It seems to me now that the words “I thought you meant…” in 1) do not have to be understood as a description of something experienced at that time, but the corresponding words in the second case do. So that the meaning-blind person could say the first, but not the second. Instead of the first, one could simply say: “Oh, you mean…, not…!”, or “Oh, you mean _that_ bank!” – and then does what is requested.

### <span class="fac">[11\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/11.webp)</span>

“When I said ‘He is an ass,’ I meant…” What kind of connection do those sounds have with that person? If asked “Who do you mean?”, I will name him, describe him, show his photograph, etc. Is there any other connection? One that existed in particular at the time of utterance? But during the entire sentence, or only during when I said “He”? No answer!

### <span class="fac">[11\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/11.webp),[12\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/12.webp)</span>

I would like to say that the experience during those words – naturally grows out of this explanation.

### <span class="fac">[12\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/12.webp)</span>

The fox's tail and the raised leg in a Busch sketch. I see it as it is meant. Of course, I cannot add the fox to the mental image.

### <span class="fac">[12\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/12.webp),[13\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/13.webp)</span>

But it is the case that: I will sometimes, in conversation, say “He is an ass,” and if I were asked “Would you have experienced something else with those words if we had talked about N instead of M,” I would have to admit that it might not have been the case. On the other hand, it sometimes seems to me as if I had an experience during the utterance that is unambiguously _related to him_. The experiences during speech seem to be clearly connected with _him_.

### <span class="fac">[13\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/13.webp)</span>

“Of course, I was thinking of him: I had him in front of me!” – but I didn’t recognize him from his picture.

### <span class="fac">[13\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/13.webp),[14\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/14.webp),[15\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/15.webp)</span>

I suddenly say, “He is an ass.” A: “Who did you mean?” I: “N.” A: Did you think of him while you were saying it, or only when you gave the explanation?” I could now answer that my words “He is…” were the end of a longer train of thought. I had been thinking of N all along. And could I now say that the words themselves were not connected to him by any particular experience, but the entire train of thought was? So, I could also have meant someone else with those words; to whom they referred was in what preceded them. But must I, in order to be able to say that I was talking about him, that I meant him, that I thought of him, really be able to remember something that is necessarily connected with him? Could it not be that it always seems to me as if _nothing_ happened during my words that could only be interpreted as referring to him? I therefore think that I am always _conscious_ that my mental images are ambiguous. However, I still say: “I meant him.” But is this not a contradictory assumption? No; that is really how it is. I say “I meant him”; _thus_ I continue.

### <span class="fac">[15\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/15.webp)</span>

11.08.1946

Religion would give me a certain modesty that I lack. For I attribute to myself, in everything that is more or less human in me, something that distinguishes me, as if it were a property.

### <span class="fac">[15\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/15.webp),[16\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/16.webp)</span>

I was talking to my neighbors about their doctor, and at the same time I have an image of this person in mind – but I have never seen him, I only know his name, and perhaps I form an image of him based on this. How can this image be characteristic of the fact that I am talking about _him_? – And yet that is how it seemed to me, until I remembered that I don't even know what this man looks like. So, his image represents him to me no better than his name.

### <span class="fac">[16\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/16.webp)</span>

If I compare the floating of meaning to a dream, then our speech is usually dreamless.

### <span class="fac">[16\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/16.webp)</span>

Remember the example of the _a_ that feels feminine and masculine (Maria as a female and male name). Usually, this ending is neither felt _this_ way nor _that_ way.

### <span class="fac">[17\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/17.webp)</span>

The “meaning-blind” person would therefore be one who always speaks in a dreamless way.

### <span class="fac">[17\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/17.webp)</span>

Just as I have a style, so every draftsman has a stroke.

### <span class="fac">[17\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/17.webp)</span>

And one can really say: What do his dreams have to do with me? Why should I be interested in what he dreams, or whether he dreams while he is talking to me, or listening to me? – Of course, this does not mean that these dreams can never interest me. But why should they be the most important thing in linguistic communication?

### <span class="fac">[17\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/17.webp)</span>

The use of the term ‘dream’ here is useful; but only if one sees that it contains an error.

### <span class="fac">[18\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/18.webp)</span>

“I thought all along that you were talking about…” – How was that? – Well, not any different from when he actually talked about that person. The fact that I later realize that I misunderstood him does not change what happened during the understanding. – So, if the sentence “I thought at the time that you meant…” is the report of a ‘dream’, then that means that I _always_ dream when I understand a sentence.

### <span class="fac">[18\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/18.webp)</span>

One also says: “I assumed that you were talking about…” and that sounds even less like the report of an experience.

### <span class="fac">[19\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/19.webp)</span>

“I thought you were talking about… and I wondered why you said… about him” – This wondering is again in a similar case: Here too, there is the feeling that one has only completed the rudimentary experience by uttering this wondering.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-131-19.2) <span class="fac">[19\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/19.webp)</span> {#192}

The thought that is working its way to the surface.

### <span class="fac">[19\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/19.webp),[20\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/20.webp)</span>

Well, it is true! For sometimes, when I say “I thought…”, I can say that when I thought, I actually said these words aloud or to myself; or that at the time I did not use these words, but other words, of which the present ones are a paraphrase. _This happens sometimes_! In contrast, there is the case in which my present expression is not a reproduction of something. For it is only a ‘reproduction’ if it is so according to objective rules.

### <span class="fac">[20\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/20.webp),[21\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/21.webp)</span>

The ability, or inclination, to say that one has understood an isolated word in one way or another does not seem particularly useful. One could imagine that one lacks this experience without losing more than someone who does not associate colors with vowels. On the other hand, this inclination now seems to be only a special case of something much more important. And I am not able to separate the important from the unimportant.

### <span class="fac">[21\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/21.webp)</span>

What has he lost who, when he hears the word in isolation, cannot say that he has heard it as a verb or conjunction? Surely not much. – Someone who would not be able to say: “but” can be a verb and a conjunction, or to form sentences in which it is one or the other, could not master certain primary school exercises. But that is not what is asked of a student: to understand the word in this way or that, or to report how he has understood it.

### <span class="fac">[22\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/22.webp)</span>

I would like to say: the conversation, the application and interpretation of the words flows along, and it is only in this flow that the word has its meaning. “He has left.” – “Why?” What did you mean when you said the word “why”? What were you thinking of? (And how?)

### <span class="fac">[22\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/22.webp)</span>

What would I give if I could express what is in my soul? The fear, the doubt, the longing, the anguish.

### <span class="fac">[22\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/22.webp),[23\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/23.webp)</span>

What did you mean when you asked, “why?” – I meant, “why did he leave?” – “Who did you mean by ‘he’?” – The one you were talking about. – And is that all you meant when you said the word “why”?

### <span class="fac">[23\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/23.webp)</span>

‘To mean him’ – ‘To hit him’

### <span class="fac">[23\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/23.webp)</span>

The comparison of the experience of meaning with a dream helps by illuminating the role of the past; but it harms by making this role seem too specific.

### <span class="fac">[23\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/23.webp)</span>

“I thought you meant _that_.” – Now, that doesn’t mean the same as “I think you meant that.” Don’t let the comparison with another use of the past mislead you!

### <span class="fac">[23\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/23.webp),[24\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/24.webp),[25\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/25.webp)</span>

We are playing this game: there are pictures there & words are spoken & we must point to the picture that corresponds to the word. Among the words are also ambiguous ones. When I hear the word…, first _one_ meaning comes to mind & I point to a picture, later only then another & I point to a different one. Will the meaning-blind person be able to do this? Of course. – But how is it _with this_: A word is named, one of its meanings comes to my mind. I don’t say it, but I look for the picture. Before I have found it, another meaning of the word comes to my mind; I say: “A second meaning has just occurred to me.” And then I explain, “First _this_ meaning came to my mind, then _that_.” Can _the_ meaning-blind person do that? – Can he not say that he knows the meaning of the word, but doesn’t say it? Or can he not say that it has just occurred to him, but he doesn’t say it? – It seems to me that he can say both of these things. But then also: “When you said the word, _this_ meaning occurred to me: …” And why not: “When I said the word, I first meant it in _this_ meaning”?

### <span class="fac">[25\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/25.webp)</span>

The meaning-blind person will in any case not be inclined to see the meaning of a word as an experience when hearing or speaking the word.

### <span class="fac">[25\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/25.webp)</span>

Think here of the use of the signal “Now I can do it” & the statement: “When I made that sign to you, I could do it.”

### <span class="fac">[26\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/26.webp)</span>

So the meaning-blind person should be able to say that first one meaning and then a second meaning of the word occurred to him, – without him actually saying the meanings.

### <span class="fac">[28\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/28.webp),[29\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/29.webp)</span>

It is as if the word that I understand has a certain light aroma that corresponds to the understanding. As if two words that are well known to me differ not only in their sound or appearance, but also, even if I do not _imagine_ anything about them, in their atmosphere. – But remember how the names of famous poets & composers seem to have absorbed a meaning of their own. ### <span class="fac">[29\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/29.webp),[30\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/30.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[30\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/30.webp),[31\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/31.webp)</span>

_Now_ I know what this expression means; _now_ I know how to sing this melody – and now follows a longer explanation. And how could I have known all this in a single moment? Was it all present to me? _This_ way of speaking is only confusing! _That_ is what one cannot put together.

### <span class="fac">[31\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/31.webp)</span>

I said that the words “Now I can do it!” do not express an _experience_. Well, just as little as the words: “Now I will raise my arm”. – But why do they not express an experience, a feeling? – How are they used? Both, for example, as an introduction to an action. The fact that a statement refers to a point in time, and that nothing happens in the outside world that it speaks of, does not prove that it spoke of an experience.

### <span class="fac">[32\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/32.webp)</span>

And _what_ must have happened in him? – Nothing. But it is important that he usually gives an answer when he has pointed; and that is the criterion for him understanding the pointing.

### <span class="fac">[32\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/32.webp),[33\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/33.webp)</span>

13.08.1946

It is difficult to understand oneself & one’s work. Hardy, for example, would like to be regarded as an artist because he does not understand himself & his work, & _I_ do not understand myself either. Perhaps one can only understand oneself in a sleepwalking manner. That is, when one opens one’s eyes, one no longer understands oneself. Or, understanding is then a struggle against vanity. It is a similar case here as when one does not understand one’s position in a society. Should one try to dress like them, should one not care how one is dressed, should one be proud not to be dressed like the others; should one join in their conversation, and what role should one play in it? If one becomes ‘self-conscious’, it is difficult. What is the scientist? Is he a seeker of truth, or a benefactor of humanity, or an artist, or is he a craftsman? For we naturally have feelings of movement, and we can also reproduce them. Especially when we repeat a movement under the same conditions in short intervals. Sensations are also localized, but almost never in the joints, mostly in the skin. (Blow out your cheeks! Where do you do it, and where do you feel it?)

### <span class="fac">[211\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/211.webp),[212\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/212.webp)</span>

“If I understand the word ‘Maria’ as a man’s name, I interpret it as I would Eliah or…”. This is certainly an analysis of my sensation. But did I think of such a man’s name beginning with “ah” when pronouncing the name “Maria”? No. It is a correct analysis of the sensation, and not a scientific, i.e., hypothetical analysis. That is, my statement that this is the analysis is sufficient here as evidence. [Cause – motive]. One could, in fact, compare the growth of the analysis with the growth of a germ. And in this case, to say “It was all already in the sensation,” or “It grew out of it as from a germ” amounts to the same thing.

### <span class="fac">[212\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/212.webp),[213\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/213.webp)</span>

How much truth is there in the fact that one sometimes repeats an arm movement, for example, based on the visual image, but not based on a kinesthetic image? [Because that’s what I once said].

### <span class="fac">[213\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/213.webp),[214\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/214.webp)</span>

08.09.1946

Does one sometimes really move one’s arm according to a mental image of it? I can only say: If I didn’t _see_ that my arm had moved after I, with my face turned away, believed I had moved it, I would be confused & would probably trust my _eyes_. Seeing can, in any case, teach me whether I have executed the intended movement precisely, e.g., whether I have reached the position that I wanted to reach; feeling could not do that. I certainly feel that I am moving, & can also judge approximately, according to feeling, _how_ – but I simply _know_ what movement I have made, without it being possible to speak of a sense-datum of this movement, of an immediate inner image of the movement. And when I say “I simply _know_…”, then “knowing” here means something like “being able to say”, & is not again a kind of inner image.

### <span class="fac">[214\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/214.webp),[215\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/215.webp),[216\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/216.webp)</span>

Could one also say it this way: In order to be able to say that the feeling teaches me where my arm is now, or how far I move it, one would have to be able to relate feelings & movements to each other. One would have to be able to say: ‘If I have this feeling ‥‥, then my arm is, according to experience, _there_’. Or also: One would have to have a criterion for the identity of feelings, in addition to that of the executed movement. But is this condition fulfilled for _seeing_, if it has any sense at all? Well, one can represent a visual image, for example, graphically. But one cannot give oneself, or someone else, the feeling that is supposed to be characteristic of bending the arm by 30˚, without actually bending the arm. Bend your arm a little! What do you feel? – A tension, or something like it here & there, & mainly the rubbing of my sleeve. Do it again! Was the feeling the same? _Approximately_. Approximately at the same places. Does this feeling always accompany this bending, can you say? No. And yet something about this argument still doesn't quite fit for me.

### <span class="fac">[216\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/216.webp),[217\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/217.webp)</span>

Imagine that certain movements produce sounds, & one says that we now recognize how far we have moved our arm by the sound that is produced. That would be possible. But what conditions would have to be met for this? For example, it would not be enough that sounds accompany the movements; nor would it be enough that they are often similar for similar movements. It would also not be enough to say: the sound _must_ indeed have _one_ and the same quality for equal movements, since it is the only sense-datum by which we can recognize the magnitude of the movement.

### <span class="fac">[217\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/217.webp)</span>

But isn’t there, after all, a kind of private ostensive definition for movements and the like? For example, I bend a finger and remember the sensation. Someone says to me, “I will produce certain sensations in your finger in this way, but without it moving; tell me when it is the one you have when you bend your finger.” Could I not, for my own use, call this sensation “E”, use my memory as a criterion for the identity, and now say “Yes, that is E again,” etc.?

### <span class="fac">[218\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/218.webp)</span>

It would then also be conceivable that I recognize the sensation and that it occurs without the conviction that the movement has taken place, without the sense of the movement.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-131-218.2) <span class="fac">[218\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/218.webp)</span> {#2182}

My ‘achievement’ is very similar to that of a mathematician who invents a calculus.

### <span class="fac">[218\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/218.webp),[219\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/219.webp)</span>

I can certainly, for example, raise my knee several times in a row and say that I had the same sensation each time: not as if I had this sensation every time I raise my knee, nor as if it were characteristic of this type of movement, but simply: I had the same sensation, caused by the movement, three times in this series of knee movements.

### <span class="fac">[219\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/219.webp)</span>

We don’t even notice most of the movements we make in everyday life.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-131-219.4) <span class="fac">[219\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/219.webp)</span> {#2194}

### <span class="fac">[219\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/219.webp),[220\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/220.webp)</span>

“I have had the same sensation three times,” that describes an event in my private world. But how does the other person know what I mean? What does “same” mean here? Well, does he rely on the fact that I use the word here as I always do? But what is, in this case, the usual, _analogous_ use? No, this difficulty is not contrived; he really does not know, cannot know, what are the same objects in this case.

### <span class="fac">[220\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/220.webp),[221\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/221.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[221\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/221.webp),[222\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/222.webp)</span>

09.09.1946

The example of the motor roller with the motor in the roller is really much better and deeper than I explained. Because, when someone presented the design to me, I saw at once that it could not work, since you could roll the roller from the outside, even if the ‘motor’ was not in operation; but I did not see that it was a rigid construction and not a machine at all. And here there is a close analogy to the case of the private ostensive definition. Because even there there is, as it were, a direct and an indirect way to see the impossibility.

### <span class="fac">[222\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/222.webp)</span>

My strength is actually to see analogies where one usually does not see them.

### <span class="fac">[222\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/222.webp),[223\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/223.webp)</span>

I named this sensation (associated with) movement “E.” For the other person, it is now the one that I had during this movement. But for me? Does “E” now mean something different? – Well, for me it means _this_ sensation. – But which one is this? Because a minute ago, I pointed to my sensation – how can I now point to _it_ again?

### <span class="fac">[223\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/223.webp),[224\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/224.webp)</span>

But consider the case where someone makes a series of arm movements & says, “The sensation I am now having in my leg, I call ‘E<sub>1</sub>’,” and so on. Later, on various occasions, he says, “Now I have E<sub>3</sub>.” And so on. – Such utterances could be important; if, for example, we observe certain physiological correlates to the sensations & can therefore draw conclusions from his utterances.

### <span class="fac">[224\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-131/224.webp)</span>

So we would say: “On the occasions A, B, C, D, he always has the same sensation in this part of the body; namely, the one he had under those & those circumstances when lifting his arm.” Consider the word – the name of the sensation – as an instrument! That is, always ask yourself how this thing is used. What it stands for only indicates how it is used – & how it is used is the expression for: it stands for ….
