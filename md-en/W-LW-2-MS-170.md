### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#1r.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/1r.webp)</span> {#ms-170-1r1}

People who do not have the concept of ‘tomorrow’. They might still have a fairly developed language: various commands, questions, descriptions. Could we communicate with them? – But could we _describe_ to them how people use the word “tomorrow” without teaching it to them? _What purpose_ could the description serve? ‘Tomorrow’ plays such a large role because the change from day & night is so important to us. Wouldn’t it be important to them too…?

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#1v.1) <span class="fac">[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/1v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-1v1}

If one wanted to give a casual description of the game with “tomorrow,” analogous to a casual description of differential calculus, it would have to be much more primitive, & it would be difficult to think of a purpose for it. Consider, however, what concept people have of curved space.

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#1v.2+2r.1) <span class="fac">[1v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/1v.webp),[2r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/2r.webp)</span> {#ms-170-1v22r1}

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#2r.2) <span class="fac">[2r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/2r.webp)</span> {#ms-170-2r2}

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#2r.3+2v.1) <span class="fac">[2r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/2v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-2r32v1}

Operating with concepts permeates our lives. I see some analogy with a very general use of keys. If one always had to open a lock in order to move something.

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#2v.2+3r.1+3v.1) <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/2v.webp),[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/3v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-2v23r13v1}

Can the psychologist teach us what seeing is? He does not teach us the use of the word “to see.” Is “to see” a technical term in psychology? Is “dog” a technical term in zoology? – The psychologist may discover differences between people that are not noticed in ordinary life & that only become apparent under the conditions of an experiment. But blindness is not something that the psychologist discovers. If seeing were something that the psychologist had discovered, then the word “to see” here could only mean a form of behaviour, an ability to act in such and such a way.

So, if the psychologist taught “There are people who _see_,” he would now have to be able to describe the behaviour of these seeing people. But this would not have taught us the use of the form “I see something red and round,” for example, and not even to the person who sees.

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#3v.2) <span class="fac">[3v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/3v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-3v2}

Could not a person who sees do without the word “to see” altogether? He says something like “There it is…” A normal child could do without the word “to see” for _a long_ time, but not, for example, without the words “red,” “yellow,” “round.”

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#3v.3+4r.1) <span class="fac">[3v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/3v.webp),[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/4r.webp)</span> {#ms-170-3v34r1}

If I observe the course of my pains, what sensory impressions should I have had if I had _not_ observed them? Would I have felt nothing? Or just not noticed it?

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#4r.2+4v.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/4v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-4r24v1}

“I would not have seen it if I had not observed it.” What do the words “it” refer to? To the same thing? “I would not have felt the pain if I had not observed the pain.” But one can also say “Observe your pain” & _not_ “Feel pain!”.

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#4v.3) <span class="fac">[4v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/4v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-4v3}

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#4v.4+5r.1) <span class="fac">[4v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/4v.webp),[5r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/5r.webp)</span> {#ms-170-4v45r1}

### [Ms-170](/ms-170/#5r.2+5v.1) <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/5r.webp),[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-170/5v.webp)</span> {#ms-170-5r25v1}

We say “Without any doubt, it is so,” & do not know how much this certainty determines our concepts. We would answer the question “Did the earth really exist before your birth?” half angrily & half embarrassed, “Yes, of course!” & be conscious of the fact that on the one hand we are not able to give reasons for it, because there seem to be too many of them, & on the other hand, that doubt is impossible, & one cannot answer the questioner with _a_ particular instruction, but by gradually teaching him a picture of our world.
