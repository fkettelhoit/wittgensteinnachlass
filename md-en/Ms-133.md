### <span class="fac">[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/1v.webp)</span>

October 22, 1946

Doesn’t one say that one knows something when one can describe it accurately? And can one say this about one’s own thoughts?

### <span class="fac">[1v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/1v.webp)</span>

If someone wants to call the words the “expression” of the thought instead of the “description” of the thought, let them ask themselves how one learns to describe a table, and how one learns to describe one’s own thoughts. And this simply means: let them observe how the description of a table, and the description of thoughts, are judged to be correct or incorrect; let them, therefore, consider these language-games in all their manifestations.

### <span class="fac">[1v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/1v.webp),[2r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/2r.webp)</span>

October 23, 1946

Philosophical doubts are like flowers. One person has only a weak, stunted specimen of _this_ kind, while another has a beautifully developed, vigorous one. A philosopher cultivates many kinds, and to a particularly large and beautiful size.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-133-2r.2+2v.1) <span class="fac">[2r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/2v.webp)</span> {#2r-2--2v-1}

One must not regard insanity as a disease. Why not as a sudden—more or _less_ sudden—change in character? Every person is (or most people are) mistrustful, and perhaps more so towards relatives than towards others. Does this mistrust have a reason? Yes and no. One can give reasons for it, but they are not conclusive. Why shouldn’t a person suddenly become _much_ more mistrustful of people? Why not _much_ more reserved? or devoid of affection? Don’t people also become this way in the ordinary course of events?—Where is the boundary here between wanting and being able? Do I _want_ to communicate with no one anymore, or _can_ I not? If so much can lose its appeal, why not everything? If people are also cunning in ordinary life, why shouldn’t they—and perhaps suddenly—become _much_ more cunning? And _much_ more inaccessible.

### <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/2v.webp)</span>

“The fact is that people only know their own thoughts.” (“The fact is that only I know my own thoughts.”) “And even I don’t” one could say.

### <span class="fac">[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/3r.webp)</span>

“The fact is that people only know their own thoughts; that they are something that belongs to them alone, hidden from others.” Is this the case, just as only I feel my heartbeat? Is it comparable to the fact that only I feel it when I have a stomach ache, or is the statement like: “Everyone only feels their own pain”?

### <span class="fac">[3r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/3r.webp)</span>

The sentence seems to say: “By nature, we have a way of keeping things secret, a closet into which no one can penetrate, and in this way our thinking, for example, and our feeling take place.”

### <span class="fac">[3r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/3v.webp)</span>

‘Same’ in a figurative sense. There is no boundary between a non-figurative and a figurative meaning.

### <span class="fac">[3v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/3v.webp)</span>

“Nature has given people the ability to think in secret.” Imagine someone saying: “Nature has given people the ability to speak audibly, but also to speak inaudibly in their minds.” This means that they can, in a sense, do the same thing in two ways. (As if they could digest visibly and invisibly.) The only difference is that when speaking in one’s mind, the speaking is more hidden than a process in the body can be. But what if I spoke when everyone else was deaf? Wouldn’t my speaking be just as hidden? “Something is happening in the deepest secret of the mind.”

### <span class="fac">[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/4r.webp)</span>

If someone _tells_ me what he has thought—has he really told me what he _thought_? Didn’t the actual mental event remain undescribed? Wasn’t _he_ the secret, of which I only give an image in speech to another?

### <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/4r.webp)</span>

No one sees, no one hears, no one perceives what I think. Thus, it is a process deeply hidden. But not embedded in matter, but removed into another _realm_. (And thus truly inaccessible to all perception.)—And how do you know what it is, what is happening, and which words describe it?

### <span class="fac">[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/4v.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[4v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/4v.webp),[5r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5r.webp)</span>

“Man is capable of conversing with himself in complete isolation; in an isolation that is far more perfect than any physical isolation.” How do I know that this is given to N.? – Because he says so & is reliable? – And yet we say: “I would like to know what he is thinking right now”; just as we could say: “I would like to know what he is writing in his notebook right now.” Yes, one could indeed say _that_ & take it, as it were, for granted that he is thinking what he writes in his notebook.

### <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5r.webp)</span>

Now, would people who could ‘read’ someone’s thoughts _regularly_ – perhaps by observing a person’s larynx – also be inclined to speak of the complete isolation of the mind in itself? – Or: Would they also be inclined to use _the image_ of ‘complete isolation’?

### <span class="fac">[5r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5r.webp)</span>

“I would like to know what he is thinking!” But now pose the – seemingly irrelevant – question: “What is so interesting about what is ‘in him,’ in his mind – assuming that something is going on?” (The devil take what is going on in him!)

### <span class="fac">[5r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5r.webp),[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5v.webp)</span>

The comparison of thinking with a process in concealment is misleading.

_So_ misleading, approximately, as the comparison of searching for the appropriate expression with a counterfeiting of the thought.

### <span class="fac">[5v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5v.webp)</span>

What confuses us is that knowing the thoughts of another is, in one sense, a logical impossibility, but in another sense, it is a psychological one.

### <span class="fac">[5v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/5v.webp)</span>

Is it correct to say that these two ‘impossibilities’ are so related to each other that the psychological impossibility (here) provides the image that then becomes the sign of the concept of ‘thinking’?

### <span class="fac">[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/6r.webp)</span>

One cannot say that writing in a notebook, or monological speaking, is ‘_similar_’ to silent thinking; however, one process can replace the other for certain purposes (e.g., mental arithmetic can replace arithmetic on paper).

### <span class="fac">[6r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/6r.webp)</span>

Could there be people who always mutter to themselves while thinking, so that their thinking is accessible to others? – “Yes, but we couldn’t know whether they are also thinking silently to themselves!” – But couldn’t it also be that this possibility of assuming it is just as senseless as assuming that the hair of these people is thinking, or that a stone is thinking?

### <span class="fac">[6v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/7r.webp)</span>

Is it therefore misleading to speak of the soul of a person, or of his mind? _So_ little that it is quite understandable when I say: “My soul is tired, not just my mind.” But don’t you still say that everything that can be expressed by the word “soul” can somehow also be expressed by words for the physical? I do not say that. But even if it were so, what would that say? The words, as well as what we point to in their explanation, are only the instruments, & now it depends on their use.

### <span class="fac">[7r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/7r.webp),[7v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/7v.webp),[8r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/8r.webp)</span>

October 25, 1946

I do not have the courage and the strength & clarity to face the facts of my life head-on. – B. has a _pre_-disposition towards me. Something that cannot last. _How_ this will fade, I naturally do not know. How _something_ of it could be preserved, alive, not pressed into a book as a memento, I also do not know. It is infinitely unlikely that this love, if it is pressured by another, or other circumstances, will still have enough resilience not to break. This is now a terrible difficulty in my life. I do not know whether and how I will be able to endure, to continue nurturing _this_ relationship with _this_ prospect. But I do not have the clarity and the strength to break it off. If I imagine that I had broken it off, then I am afraid of the loneliness; of having to tell myself that I have broken a bond that was a great & extraordinarily odd gift from heaven, _and that I did not know how to use for good_. It will seem to me that, when I never expected it, an opportunity was offered to me, and I, instead of _probably_ using it, have thrown it away. That is _difficult_: if I want to use this inclination, I can only do it by suffering a great deal. – And whether it will _then_ work, I do not know either, nor whether I can endure this pain _can_. Demons have woven this bond and hold it in their hands. They can tear it apart, or let it last.

### <span class="fac">[8r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/8r.webp),[8v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/8v.webp),[9r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/9r.webp)</span>

October 26, 1946

Love is a happiness. Perhaps a happiness with pain, but a happiness. If the happiness is missing, or shrinks to a brief flash, then the love is missing. – In love, I must be able to _rest_ securely. – But can you reject a warm heart? Is it a heart that is warm for _me_? – ‘I'd rather do anything than hurt the soul of friendship.’ – I must know: he won't hurt _our friendship_. A person cannot get out of his own skin. I cannot give up a demand that lies deep within me, anchored in my whole life. For love is connected with nature; and if I were to be unnatural, then love would have to stop. – Can I say: “I will be reasonable, & no longer demand that.”? In some cases, it works. It may work for most _for a while_. But only as a means to another end, not as an end in itself. I can say: let him be, – it will be different one day. – Love is the pearl of great value that one holds close to one's heart, for which one wants _nothing_ in exchange, which one values as the most precious thing. It _shows_ one, if one has it, what great value _is_. One learns what it _means_: to recognize value. One learns what it means: to separate a precious metal from all others. The immense preference for it teaches us the idea of unique value. The immense _preference_ leads us to see: it is our _duty_ to defend it. The preference leads us to seriousness. The passion, to seriousness. – If the preference does not do this, then it is not love. The terrible thing is the uncertainty. And in the uncertainty, my mind is always occupied with imagining possibilities, and almost always bad ones. That is sometimes right, but mostly bad. “Trust in God.” But I am far from trusting in God. From where I am, there is a _long_ way to trusting in God. Joyful hope and fear are siblings. I cannot have one without the other bordering on it.

I say, “Let me think!”, I think, then I speak. What happened when I was thinking? – I thought. (Of course, this is not an answer.) But I didn’t do absolutely _nothing_ during that time! If “doing nothing” means mental emptiness, then I did something, and not nothing. – Imagine someone said, “My mind was not in _repose_, but in motion.” – But what is “repose,” and what is “motion” in this context? – We must descend to the concept of “describing” – of “reporting” or “telling” a _process_. – But what about the process that I experience?! – Are you sure that you experience a ‘process’? And if you say you are sure, – what is that certainty worth? What is its value in practice? (“Black, a visual sensation, or the lack of a visual sensation?”)

### <span class="fac">[13v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/13v.webp)</span>

“I intend to go there”: Is this a _description_ of a state of mind, or an _utterance_? – If one imagines a model of the mind, then the sentence could be a description of the model in its present state. The human being looks at his mind & says: “…” Is it a good or a bad model – how would one decide? The question is: How would it be _used_ as a sign?

### <span class="fac">[13v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/13v.webp),[14r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/14r.webp)</span>

Thus, one could use a verb. Roughly corresponding to the expression “I am acting with the intention of…”

### <span class="fac">[14r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/14r.webp)</span>

Description of my states of mind: of the change from fear and hope, for example. “In the morning I was full of hope, then…. Everyone would call this a description. But it is characteristic of the fact that there could be a parallel description of my behaviour.

### <span class="fac">[14r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/14r.webp)</span>

Compare the expression of fear & hope with that of ‘belief,’ that this and that will happen. – One therefore also calls hope and fear “emotions,” but not belief (or the believing).

### <span class="fac">[14r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/14r.webp),[14v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/14v.webp)</span>

When I say: “The intention to do it became stronger from hour to hour” – this one would call a description. But then also _this_: “I intended it all along….” Now compare “I believed in the law of gravity all along” with “I believed all along that I was hearing a faint whisper.” In the first case, “believing” is used similarly to “knowing” (‘_Had_ one asked me, I _would have_ said…’) – in the second case, we have an activity, a guessing, listening, doubting, etc. And even if “believing” does not denote this activity, it is nevertheless it that makes us say that we are _describing_ here a state of mind or a mental activity. – We could also put it this way: We are forming a picture of the person who believes all along that he is hearing a faint noise. But not of the person who believes in the correctness of the law of gravity.

### <span class="fac">[15r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/15r.webp)</span>

I intend (one could say) does not mean: “I am in the process of intending” or “I am in the act of intending” (as one says I am in the act of reading the newspaper). But rather: “I am in the process of planning my trip,” etc. We do not have a single verb, but we could have it (and perhaps it actually exists in a little-known language) that expresses: “acting with the intention of doing this and that.”

### <span class="fac">[15r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/15r.webp)</span>

“I intend…” is never a description, but under certain circumstances, descriptions can be assigned to this utterance.

### <span class="fac">[15v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/15v.webp)</span>

Talking to oneself. “What is happening here?” A wrong question! Not only can one not say what is happening—not even: one does not know what is happening—not even: one knows only this and that about it! But even this is wrong, to say: it is simply a specific process that cannot be described in any other way than with these words. – The concepts of ‘description’ and ‘report’. One says: someone reports that he said to himself…. In what respect is this comparable to the ‘report’ that he said, e.g.,…? Let us realize that _describing_ is a very specific _language-game_. – – – [Description of a dream, _memory_.] We must dig up these underlying, hardened concepts.

I could also have said: the distorted in music. In the sense in which one speaks of features distorted by grammar. When Grillparzer says that Mozart only allowed the “beautiful” in music, that means, I believe, that he did not allow the distorted, the hideous, that in his music nothing can be found that corresponds to _this_. Whether this is entirely true, I will not say, but let us assume that it is, so it is a prejudice of Grillparzer that things should not be otherwise. That the music after Mozart (particularly, of course, through Beethoven) has expanded its area of language is neither to be praised nor regretted, but: _that_ is how it is. In Grillparzer’s behaviour, there is a kind of ingratitude. Did he _still_ want a Mozart? Could he imagine something that such a one would now compose? Could he have imagined Mozart if he had not known him? Here, too, the concept of “the beautiful” has done much mischief.

### <span class="fac">[27v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/27v.webp),[28r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/28r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[28r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/28r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[28r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/28r.webp),[28v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/28v.webp)</span>

We judge the motive of an action according to what the person who committed it tells us, according to the account of eyewitnesses, according to the background. Thus, we _judge_ the motives of a person. But it does not occur to us very much that there is such a thing as ‘judging motives.’ That this is a quite peculiar language-game – that the table and the stone do not have motives. That there is also the question: “Is this a reliable way to judge a person’s motives?”, but we must already know what “judging motives” means at all. There must already be a technique that we call this, before we can talk about a modification of it that we call a more reliable judgment of a motive.

### <span class="fac">[29r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/29r.webp),[29v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/29v.webp)</span>

One judges the length of a rod, & one can search for & find a method to judge it more accurately, more correctly. So – you say – what we judge here is independent of the method of judging; one cannot explain what length _is_ with the help of the method of judgment. But, whoever thinks like that makes a mistake. What kind of mistake? – How strange it would be to say: “The height of the Himalayas depends on how one climbs it.” “Measuring the length ever more accurately,” one wants to compare that to getting closer & closer to an object. But it is not clear in all cases what it means to “get closer & closer to the length of the rod.” And one cannot say: “You know what the length of a rod is; and you know what ‘determining it’ means; _therefore_ you know what it means to ‘determine the length ever more accurately’.” What it means to seek a more accurate determination of the length of the rod is clear under certain circumstances, & unclear under certain circumstances and requires a new determination. Imagine that you have to make a decision, namely, by pressing one of a number of buttons. The decision you make by doing so is indicated by a word that is on the button. It is then, of course, completely irrelevant what you experience when you see this word. If the word is, for example, “switch,” then you can mean it as an adjective, noun, or verb; the decision is not changed by that. And similarly, if you _pronounce_ the word as a decision. It communicates the same thing to the other person, who is waiting for the decision.

### <span class="fac">[47v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/47v.webp)</span>

But what if the decision is capable of two interpretations, and the person who hears it gives it _one_ of them? He can do this either through his actions, or, as it were, in thought. If one were not to act immediately on the decision, he could also hear it and, for the time being, not interpret it at all. On the other hand, he could answer a _question_ with an interpretation. I give someone a command with the word “switch” in the sense of a railroad switch, but I quietly add the word “eggs.” In what sense have I uttered the word? Or, in a conversation, sighing, I say, “Time flies!” and quietly continue: “if you've got nothing better to do.” It always seems as if one could not make the mutually exclusive thought processes. If one means the first words as a sigh, then the others stand there without any connection. And if one imagines oneself in a situation in which the words are a command, and speaks them in the tone of a sigh, then it is not the sigh “Time flies!”

It is possible to utter the words in accordance with a certain situation and thus in that and that sense, and yet to think of a different sense. So that the words have a meaning of their own, for me, but unconsciously for the other person.

### <span class="fac">[48v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/48v.webp)</span>

If asked, I will perhaps explain this meaning, and yet the explanation was not what I had in mind. What, then, would my state of mind, when I uttered the ambiguous word, have to do with the words of the explanation? In what way can these words correspond to it? ### <span class="fac">[48v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/48v.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[48v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/48v.webp),[49r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/49r.webp)</span>

Ask yourself again: what interests us about how someone meant this word, this sentence? Is it really a kind of organic accompaniment of speaking that interests us? & why? – Because we can draw empirical conclusions from this accompaniment?

### <span class="fac">[49v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/49v.webp)</span>

Imagine that people have the habit of scribbling while speaking – why should what they produce in this way with certain words be less interesting than accompanying processes in their mind, & why should the interest in _these_ be of a different kind?

Why _seems_ one of these processes to give the speech its own _life_?

Depending on whether he meant the word _this_ way or _that_ way, he expressed the one or the other intention. He had the one or the other intention. “Give him this command & mean by it…!” “Tell him that & mean by it…!” That would be a strange command, one that one would not usually give. Or I say to someone, “Convey this message,” & then I ask him, “Did you also mean it this way?”

### <span class="fac">[52v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/52v.webp),[53r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/53r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[53r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/53r.webp),[53v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/53v.webp)</span>

I mean: the criteria for what happened in the past are different here than, for example, for the appearance of a picture.

### <span class="fac">[53v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/53v.webp)</span>

How am I supposed to describe this psychological phenomenon? That one can mean a word in such and such a way on command? That one imagines meaning it this way or that way? ### <span class="fac">[53v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/53v.webp),[54r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/54r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[54v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/54v.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[54v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/54v.webp),[55r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/55r.webp)</span>

The special experience of meaning is characterized by the fact that we react with an explanation & the past tense: just as if we were explaining the meaning of a word for practical purposes.

### <span class="fac">[55r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/55r.webp)</span>

Imagine someone says a sentence, gives a command, means it in such and such a way, then he suddenly thinks better of it & means it differently. A change in his image of himself, which perhaps illustrates the change in meaning. Now one would like to say that the change in the mental image & the change in opinion are of a different kind. This could be expressed as follows: If you had asked me first what I meant, I would have given you this explanation; after that change, however, the other. The intention had changed & at the same time a content of experience had also changed, but the intention was not an experience.

### <span class="fac">[55r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/55r.webp),[55v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/55v.webp),[56r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/56r.webp)</span>

It is possible that one should introduce a new nomenclature here – a step in philosophy that is only rarely advisable. I mean a nomenclature for psychological categories. Thus, one could call the understanding of a word a ‘capacity’. But ‘intending’ would not be a capacity, & to mean a word in such & such a way is an intention. – But what about the sudden flash – “Now I know it”, “Now I can do it”, etc.? Where does it belong? Should I classify it among the sensations, because the sudden intake of breath, etc., does produce sensations? (James might have done that.) Well, sensations are sensations, but the flash is not a sensation, & the phenomenon that interests us here is not the flash either – which I can reproduce arbitrarily at any time – but rather the one that occurs under quite special circumstances. Should I now say that something special happens _during_ this flash? Well, how am I supposed to test this?! Can I, from the memory of such cases, say that something happens here, & what it is? No.

### <span class="fac">[56r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/56r.webp)</span>

One of the principles of observation must be that I do not disturb the phenomenon I am observing through my observation. That is to say, my observation must be applicable to the cases in which no observation was made.

### <span class="fac">[56r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/56r.webp),[56v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/56v.webp)</span>

So, does this flash correspond to a special experience? No. – Imagine someone who always says “Now I get it!” when he hadn't, – what should we say about him? What experience did he have? It is not the special ‘content of experience’ during the flash that gives him his special interest, & if someone says that he understood everything at that moment, that is not a description of the content of experience. – But why not? – I want to distinguish between a statement like: “I saw the formula in that moment” & one like “I grasped the method at that moment.” But not as if I wanted to say – “because one cannot grasp a method in a moment.” Of course, one can, it often happens. – I want to say: “‘Now I understand’ is a signal, not a description.” And what is achieved by saying this? Well, attention is directed to the origin of such a signal; the question “How does one learn the words ‘Now I understand’ & how, for example, the description of an image?” comes to the fore. Because the word “signal” points to a process that is being signaled.

### <span class="fac">[57r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57r.webp)</span>

But does it belong to psychology that I utter certain words under certain circumstances? Even if these words are not a description of the content of experience? That is precisely what it is: that they do not have to be such a description.

### <span class="fac">[57r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57r.webp)</span>

But is it not essential for psychological statements in the first person present that they must be ‘undeniable’? And is this not precisely the criterion for the description of the content of experience? Because who, for example, says that he decided on this course of action at that moment – can one ask him whether he also knows that he made this decision?

### <span class="fac">[57r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57r.webp),[57v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57v.webp)</span>

It is, of course, the undeniability that favors the image: as if something is being described here that only we see & the other does not see, which is therefore close to us & always accessible, but hidden from the other, something that is in us & we become aware of it by looking into ourselves. And psychology is now the science of this inner world.

### <span class="fac">[57v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57v.webp)</span>

If, therefore, I want to say that our ‘utterances,’ with which psychology deals, are by no means all descriptions of the content of experience, then I must say that what one calls descriptions of the content of experience is only a small group of those ‘undeniable’ utterances. But by what grammatical characteristics is this group characterized?

### <span class="fac">[57v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/57v.webp),[58r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-133/58r.webp)</span>

One would, for example, want to call pain a content of experience. But why? And why not sorrow & love? – One thing is that one cannot talk about a second-long love, etc. – What about the feeling of tiredness or freshness?
