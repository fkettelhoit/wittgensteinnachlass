# Ms-178b

### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/1.webp)</span>

“I understand it & I believe he understands it too.” (I have pains after the vaccination & I believe he has them too.)

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/1.webp)</span>

“He says he understands it, & I believe him.”

### <span class="fac">[1\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/1.webp),[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/2.webp)</span>

“He behaves as if he understood it.” The question now is whether this sentence is opposed to another: “he understands it”, or whether one can always only say the former? I mean: how have we arranged it? – Does one always only say “there seems to be an armchair standing there” or also in one case: “there really is an armchair standing there”? Or does this expression not exist at all in my language, but only this one: “the hypothesis that there is an armchair here is thereby made more probable.” That would be like fx having _no_ value for x = 0 but having approximate values when we approach zero. But here the word “approximate value” is misleading, as if these values were approaching a value & that is precisely not the case, but only x approaches zero. Thus here probability does not approach any certainty which we perhaps cannot attain only through human weakness. Rather, in our game we have provided for no such certainty (just as we have provided for no such value f(0)).

### <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/2.webp)</span>

We say: “understanding is something inner & we can only infer from indications that it is understood.” Then it is as if one inferred from noises & other effects that there is a person in the next room whom we cannot see. But _here_ there is a possibility of describing what it would be like if I could enter the next room and see him.

### <span class="fac">[2\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/2.webp)</span>

The impossibility of _knowing_ is a grammatical matter. It is meant to say: we do not want to speak here of “knowledge”.

The person who says that, strictly speaking, we can never _know_ whether there really is an armchair standing here determines a grammatical game. (And does not establish a fact about our human capacities.) We could ask him: “what do you call ‘knowing that there is an armchair standing there’ at all?”

### <span class="fac">[3\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/3.webp)</span>

And we can hold up our normal way of expressing ourselves to him & say “but I do know that there is an armchair here”. And this way of expressing ourselves is no less precise than his. And “I know it” of course means: “I cannot be mistaken about it”, for it would be a contradiction to say “I knew it, but I was nevertheless mistaken”. And this impossibility of error is of course once again a grammatical convention.

### <span class="fac">[3\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/3.webp)</span>

One then approaches certainty just as little through probability as one approaches an eastern pole of the earth by travelling east, although one can say that one is getting further and further east. (This example could clarify various things.)

### <span class="fac">[3\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/3.webp)</span>

“I believe he is in the room” could also be said like this: “I believe that what I am now going to record is the case”. But if one says: I believe he understands me, there is not here something that one would call the direct view of understanding.

### <span class="fac">[3\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/3.webp),[4\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/4.webp)</span>

Thus, in the case of the person in the next room, one speaks roughly of direct & indirect evidence, whereas in the case of understanding there is only one. (But these are different games, stipulations, not different experiences that we have in the two cases. As when in one case there is a goal in a race which the runners have to reach, but in another no such goal is stipulated, while the number of steps per second is measured & the runners’ ranking determined by it.)

### <span class="fac">[4\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/4.webp)</span>

I always want to say, mistakenly: “when I see this, I say: ‘he has understood me’”. But that is not at all the form of a rule.

### <span class="fac">[4\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/4.webp),[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/5.webp),[6\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/6.webp)</span>

He gives, for example, at my request, the ostensive definition of the word ‘red’ & I say: “he understands the word”. I could now be asked about the rules for the use of the word “understand”, by being asked: “Do you now know for certain that he understands the word ‘red’, or is it merely a supposition?” What will you say if he should now not be able to point to a red object, or if he … We easily make a certain mistake. Language is, after all, the phenomenon of people speaking with one another (or to themselves) & in this sense it consists of the sentences & words that everyone has spoken, written, read, etc., at some time. In another sense, too, it consists of the existing books & writings of every kind, and finally, in yet another sense, of the speeches & writings concerning the rules of language, of dictionaries & grammars. We can speak about all these phenomena as a part of natural history, but also in scientific explanations. But none of this is of interest to _us_. We are interested only in _one_ particular aspect of the matter. (As someone might say: botany deals with all these objects & connections, but I am interested only in whether one can train them on a trellis.) The aspect of the calculus according to rules. I am seeking to dispel certain disquiet. But now I fall into the error of pretending that language is through & through only a calculus according to rules, & that it is fully described once one has represented it as such. Whereas it is only incumbent on us to compare it, in all its parts, with calculi. This is connected with the fact that in philosophy we do not deal with general & particular problems.

### <span class="fac">[6\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/6.webp)</span>

Realism says that what we say in ordinary language is correctly expressed. Idealism claims that it is essentially wrongly expressed & that one ought really to say something different. We must first, broadly speaking, grant realism its due, but then eliminate the nonsense of the conception of language which is the real reason for the idealist objections.

### <span class="fac">[6\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/6.webp)</span>

We use the word “understand” in some respects similarly to the designation of a physical process or also of a physical body. Someone who does not know under what circumstances one says that there is an armchair standing here certainly does not understand the word “armchair”. Thus, for example, someone who does not know what an armchair looks like. But it is also true that there is no fixed number of views of an armchair, laid down, say, by drawings, which would be the views of an armchair. The grammar of the word “understand” is therefore in some respects analogous to that of a “physical process”. (In general we can only practise comparative grammar.)

### <span class="fac">[7\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/7.webp),[8\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/8.webp)</span>

It is infinitely strange, the problem (or the disquiet) which arises in us when looking at any spatial object, when we try to become conscious of what this body actually is, since we see only one side and hence, in a certain sense, do not see it. And now the point is to find the analogy according to which we (here) form the false question. For if it is senseless, how did I come to ask it, what was before my mind when I asked it, where does it (from what analogy) derive its apparent sense? It is a _linguistic_ trap in which we are caught. We no longer understand the “logic of language” of object-words. In particular, it is a confusion of the grammar of the object-word with that of a designation of the visual image of the object. For one believes that the thing-word has the grammar which the designation of the appearance of the object has. (This is then expressed most starkly in idealism, which claims that the object is the mental image, although idealism does not simply believe itself to be stating a grammatical equation, but something like the result of an analysis (analogous, then, to the sentence: water is H<sub>2</sub>O).

### <span class="fac">[8\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/8.webp)</span>

I hear noises in the next room & say that there is probably a person in it. I infer it from those indications. Now someone could ask me: & what is it, then, that you infer, what is it like when a person really is in the room? One could then, as an answer, point to a person in that room. This would be the answer to the question: “under what circumstances do you say that there is a person in the room?” And these circumstances could also be called the criteria. In contrast, that is, to a symptom such as the noise which I had heard from the next room.

### <span class="fac">[8\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/8.webp)</span>

One could say: my visual image agrees with the hypothesis that there is an armchair standing there, & one could lay down clear stipulations about when one calls a visual image that of an armchair. (Let us imagine something similar with a cube & the stipulations made, say, by looking through a glass plate, etc.) Those words can of course also be replaced by simpler ones, e.g.: there seems to be an armchair standing there.

### <span class="fac">[9\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/9.webp)</span>

Let us imagine the following test of understanding: I draw a view of an armchair & ask whether he understands what kind of body I mean by this drawing. To test whether he has understood it, I require him to draw a particular other view of this body.

### <span class="fac">[9\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/9.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[9\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/9.webp)</span>

“We regard both as signs of understanding.” This is analogous in one sense to: “we regard both this and this visual image as a sign that there is an armchair standing there.”

### <span class="fac">[9\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178b/9.webp)</span>

“Both we regard …” can of course mean: both are, according to experience, a sign (indication) of understanding; & we regard understanding as having occurred when certain utterances take place.
