### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-1r.2) <span class="fac">[1r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1r.webp)</span> {#1r-2}

April 25, 1951

“I cannot be mistaken in this” is a common sentence used to indicate the degree of certainty of a statement. And it is justified only in its everyday use.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-1r.3) <span class="fac">[1r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1r.webp)</span> {#1r-3}

But what on earth is it good for if I can – admittedly – be mistaken in it, and thus also in the sentence that it is supposed to support?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-1r.4) <span class="fac">[1r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1r.webp)</span> {#1r-4}

Or should I say, the sentence excludes a certain _kind_ of error.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-1r.5+1v.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1r.webp),[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1v.webp)</span> {#1r-5--1v-1}

“He told me that today; I cannot be mistaken in this.” – But what if it turns out to be false?! – Shouldn’t one make a distinction in the way something ‘turns out to be false’? – How can it be _proven_ that my statement was false? Here, evidence is set against evidence, and it must be _decided_ which one should yield.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-1v.2) <span class="fac">[1v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/1v.webp)</span> {#1v-2}

But if one raises the objection: What if I suddenly, as it were, wake up and say, “Now I imagined that I am called L.W.” – who says that I will not wake up again and then explain _this_ as a strange delusion, and so on?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2r.1) <span class="fac">[2r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2r.webp)</span> {#2r-1}

One can, of course, imagine a case, and there are cases in which, after ‘waking up,’ one never doubts again what was an illusion and what was reality. But such a case, or its possibility, does not discredit the sentence “I cannot be mistaken in this.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2r.2) <span class="fac">[2r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2r.webp)</span> {#2r-2}

Otherwise, wouldn’t it discredit all assertions?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2r.3) <span class="fac">[2r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2r.webp)</span> {#2r-3}

I cannot be mistaken in this, – but I may, rightly or wrongly, decide that I was not in a fit state to judge.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2v.1) <span class="fac">[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2v.webp)</span> {#2v-1}

If this were to happen always or often, it would indeed completely change the character of the language-game.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2v.2) <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2v.webp)</span> {#2v-2}

There is a difference between an error for which, as it were, a place is reserved in the game, and a complete violation of the rules that occurs exceptionally.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-2v.3+3r.1+3v.1) <span class="fac">[2v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/2v.webp),[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/3v.webp)</span> {#2v-3--3r-1--3v-1}

I can also convince others that I cannot be ‘mistaken about it’. I say to someone: “So & so was here this _morning_ & told me this & that.” If it is surprising, he might ask me: “You cannot be mistaken about that?” This might mean: “Is it also certain that it happened this _morning_?” or rather: “Did you also understand him correctly?” – It is easy to see how, through certain explanations, I could show that I was not mistaken about the time, & also, that I did not misunderstand the story. But all of this cannot show that I did not dream the whole thing, or that I imagined it in a dreamlike way. It can also not show that I did not perhaps slip up _throughout_. (Such a thing does happen.)

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-3v.2) <span class="fac">[3v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/3v.webp)</span> {#3v-2}

(I once told someone – in English – that the shape of a certain branch was characteristic of the branch of an elm, which the other person disputed. We then came across ashes, and I said, “You see, here are the branches I was talking about.” To which he replied: “But that’s an ash” – and I: “I always meant ash when I said elm.”)

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-3v.3+4r.1) <span class="fac">[3v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/3v.webp),[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/4r.webp)</span> {#3v-3--4r-1}

That is, the possibility of an _error_ can be eliminated in certain (and frequent) cases. – This is also how calculation errors are eliminated. For if a calculation has been checked many times, one can no longer say: “Its correctness is only _very probable_ – because an error may still have crept in.” For suppose it now seems that an error has been discovered – why should we not suspect an error _here_?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-4r.2+4v.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/4v.webp)</span> {#4r-2--4v-1}

I cannot be mistaken in that 12 × 12 = 144. And one cannot set the _mathematical_ certainty against the relative uncertainty of sentences of experience. For the mathematical sentence was obtained through a series of actions that do not differ in any way from actions in the rest of life & which are equally subject to forgetting, oversight, and deception.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-4v.2) <span class="fac">[4v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/4v.webp)</span> {#4v-2}

Can I now prophesy that people will never overturn today’s calculations, never say that now they know how it really is? But would that justify a doubt on our part?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-5r.1) <span class="fac">[5r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/5r.webp)</span> {#5r-1}

If the sentence 12 × 12 = 144 is exempt from doubt, then non-mathematical sentences must also be.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-5r.2+5v.1) <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/5r.webp),[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/5v.webp)</span> {#5r-2--5v-1}

26.04.1951

But one can raise several objections to this. – Firstly, “12 × 12 etc.” is indeed a _mathematical_ sentence, and one can infer from this that only such sentences are in a position to be so. And if this inference is not justified, then there should be an equally certain sentence that deals with the process of calculation but is not mathematical. – I am thinking of a sentence of this kind: “The calculation ‘12 × 12’, when performed by arithmeticians, will, in the vast majority of cases, yield ‘144’.” No one would dispute this sentence; no one would dispute it, and it is, of course, not a mathematical sentence. But does it have the certainty of the mathematical?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-5v.2) <span class="fac">[5v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/5v.webp)</span> {#5v-2}

The mathematical sentence, as it were, has officially had the stamp of indisputability affixed to it. That is: “Argue about other things; _this_ is certain, it is an anchor around which your dispute can revolve.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-5v.3) <span class="fac">[5v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/5v.webp)</span> {#5v-3}

And one cannot say of a _sentence_ that _I_ am called L.W. Nor of the sentence that the such-&-such people calculated the such-&-such calculation correctly.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-6r.1) <span class="fac">[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/6r.webp)</span> {#6r-1}

One could say that the sentences of mathematics are petrified. – The sentence “I am called…” is not. But those who, like me, have the overwhelming evidence also regard it as _incontrovertible_. And not out of thoughtlessness. For the fact that the evidence is overwhelming consists precisely in the fact that we do not _have_ to yield to any opposing evidence. We therefore have here a safeguard similar to the one that makes the sentences of mathematics incontrovertible.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-6r.2+6v.1) <span class="fac">[6r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/6v.webp)</span> {#6r-2--6v-1}

The question ‘But could you not now be in a delusion, and perhaps later find out that you were?’ could also be raised for each sentence of the multiplication table.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-6v.2) <span class="fac">[6v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/6v.webp)</span> {#6v-2}

“I cannot be mistaken that I have just had lunch.” Yes, if I say to someone, “I have just had lunch,” he may believe that I am lying, or not in my right mind, but he will not believe that I am _mistaken_. Yes, the assumption that I could be mistaken makes no sense here.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-6v.3+7r.1) <span class="fac">[6v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7r.webp)</span> {#6v-3--7r-1}

But that is not true. I could, for example, immediately after lunch, without knowing it, doze off and sleep for an hour, and then believe that I have just eaten. But I am at least distinguishing between different kinds of error here.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-7r.2) <span class="fac">[7r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7r.webp)</span> {#7r-2}

I could ask: “_How_ could I be mistaken that I am called L.W.?” And I can say: I do not see how it would be possible.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-7r.3) <span class="fac">[7r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7r.webp)</span> {#7r-3}

How could I be mistaken in the assumption that I have never been to the moon?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-7r.4+7v.1) <span class="fac">[7r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7r.webp),[7v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7v.webp)</span> {#7r-4--7v-1}

If I said, “I have not been to the moon – but I could be mistaken,” that would be absurd. For even the thought that I could, by unknown means, have been transported there in my sleep, _would not give me the right_ to speak of a possible mistake here. I am playing the game _wrongly_ if I do so.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-7v.2) <span class="fac">[7v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7v.webp)</span> {#7v-2}

I have a right to say “I cannot be mistaken here,” even if I am mistaken.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-7v.3+8r.1) <span class="fac">[7v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/7v.webp),[8r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8r.webp)</span> {#7v-3--8r-1}

There is a difference between learning in school what is right and wrong in mathematics, and my explaining myself that I cannot be mistaken in a sentence.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-8r.2) <span class="fac">[8r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8r.webp)</span> {#8r-2}

I am adding something special to what is generally established.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-8r.3) <span class="fac">[8r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8r.webp)</span> {#8r-3}

But what about, for example, anatomy (or a large part of it)? Is not what it describes also beyond all doubt?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-8r.4) <span class="fac">[8r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8r.webp)</span> {#8r-4}

Even if I came to a people who believe that people are transported to the moon in their dreams, I could not say to them: “I have never been to the moon. – Of course, I could be mistaken.” And to their question “Can you not be mistaken?” I would have to answer: No.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-8v.1) <span class="fac">[8v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8v.webp)</span> {#8v-1}

What practical consequences does it have if I make a statement and add that I cannot be mistaken in it? (Instead, I could also add: “I can be mistaken in this no more than in the fact that I am called L.W.”) The other person may still doubt my statement. But not only will he, if he trusts me, allow himself to be instructed by me, but he will also draw certain conclusions about my behavior from my conviction.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-8v.2+9r.1) <span class="fac">[8v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/8v.webp),[9r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/9r.webp)</span> {#8v-2--9r-1}

The sentence “I cannot be mistaken in this” will certainly be used in practice. One might, however, doubt whether it is to be understood in a strictly logical sense, or whether it is rather an exaggeration, perhaps used only for the purpose of persuasion. 27.04.1951

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-9r.2) <span class="fac">[9r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/9r.webp)</span> {#9r-2}

One could speak of fundamental principles of human research.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-9r.3+9v.1+10r.1) <span class="fac">[9r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/9r.webp),[9v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/9v.webp),[10r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10r.webp)</span> {#9r-3--9v-1--10r-1}

I fly from here to a continent where people have only vague, or even no, knowledge of the possibility of flying. I tell them that I have just flown from … to them. They ask me if I could be mistaken. – They apparently have a false image of how things are. (If I were packed into a box, it would be possible for me to be mistaken about the method of transport. If I simply tell them that I cannot be mistaken, this might not convince them; but if I describe the process to them, they will certainly not question the possibility of an _error_. However, they might – even if they trust me – believe that I dreamed it, or that some _magic_ made me imagine it.)

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-10r.2) <span class="fac">[10r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10r.webp)</span> {#10r-2}

‘If I don’t trust _the_ evidence, why should I trust any evidence at all?’

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-10r.3) <span class="fac">[10r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10r.webp)</span> {#10r-3}

Is it not difficult to distinguish between the cases in which I am _not_ – & those in which I can _hardly_ be mistaken? Is it always clear to which type a case belongs? I don't think so.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-10r.4+10v.1) <span class="fac">[10r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10r.webp),[10v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10v.webp)</span> {#10r-4--10v-1}

Now, however, there are certain typical cases in which I can rightly say that I cannot be mistaken, and Moore has given some examples of such cases. I can enumerate various typical cases, but I cannot give a general characteristic. (N.N. cannot be mistaken in that he flew from America to England a few days ago. Only if he is foolish can he consider anything else possible.)

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-10v.2) <span class="fac">[10v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10v.webp)</span> {#10v-2}

If someone believes that he flew from America to England a few days ago, then I believe that he cannot be _mistaken_ in this. Likewise, if someone says that he is now sitting at the table and writing.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-177-10v.3+11r.1) <span class="fac">[10v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/10v.webp),[11r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-177/11r.webp)</span> {#10v-3--11r-1}

“But if I cannot be mistaken even in these cases, is it not possible that I am in a state of anesthesia?” If I am in a state of anesthesia and if the anesthesia deprives me of consciousness, then I am not really talking and thinking now. I cannot seriously assume that I am dreaming now. Whoever says “I am dreaming” while dreaming, even if he is talking audibly, is as wrong as if he says “It is raining” in his dream, while it is actually raining. Even if his dream is actually related to the sound of rain.
