### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#28.3) <span class="fac">[28\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/28.webp)</span> {#ms-126-283}

<span class="series-number">1</span> It is, of course, clear that the mathematician, insofar as he really ‘plays a game,’ does not _draw conclusions_. For ‘playing’ here must mean: to _act_ in accordance with certain rules. And even that would be stepping outside the mere game; if he were to draw the conclusion that, in accordance with the general rule, he is allowed to _act_ in this way.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#30.4+31.1) <span class="fac">[30\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/30.webp),[31\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/31.webp)</span> {#ms-126-304311}

I want to say: it is essential to mathematics that its signs are also used _in everyday life_. It is the use outside of mathematics, i.e. the _meaning_ of the signs, that makes the game of signs into mathematics.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#33.4+34.1) <span class="fac">[33\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/33.webp),[34\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/34.webp)</span> {#ms-126-334341}

One could imagine this being completely idiotic in other respects.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#36.3+37.1) <span class="fac">[36\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/36.webp),[37\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/37.webp)</span> {#ms-126-363371}

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#37.2+38.1) <span class="fac">[37\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/37.webp),[38\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/38.webp)</span> {#ms-126-372381}

To say that mathematics is a game means: when conducting a proof, we need not appeal anywhere to the meaning of the symbols, that is, to their non-mathematical application. But what does it even mean to appeal to them? How can such an appeal bear fruit?

Does this mean stepping out of mathematics and returning to it, or does it mean moving from _one_ mathematical mode of reasoning to another?

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#39.1) <span class="fac">[39\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/39.webp)</span> {#ms-126-391}

<span class="series-number">5</span> November 1, 1942

If the intended application of mathematics is essential, what then of those parts of mathematics whose application—at least what mathematicians took to be an application—is entirely fanciful? So that, as in set theory, one pursues a branch of mathematics of whose application one has a completely false conception. Is one not, after all, _still_ doing mathematics?

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#39.2+40.1) <span class="fac">[39\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/39.webp),[40\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/40.webp)</span> {#ms-126-392401}

November 2, 1942

If the arithmetic operations served only to construct a cipher, their use would of course be fundamentally different from ours. But would these operations even be mathematical operations?

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#40.2+41.1) <span class="fac">[40\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/40.webp),[41\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/41.webp)</span> {#ms-126-402411}

Can one say of the person who makes the application of a deciphering rule evident that he is performing mathematical operations? And yet his transformations can be understood in this way. For he could indeed say that he is calculating what must result from the deciphering of the sign … according to this or that rule. And the statement that the symbols … decoded according to this rule … result is a mathematical one. Just as the statement that in a game of chess one can move from _this_ position to that one.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#41.2) <span class="fac">[41\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/41.webp)</span> {#ms-126-412}

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#45.3+46.1) <span class="fac">[45\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/45.webp),[46\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/46.webp)</span> {#ms-126-453461}

“Whether this applies to a _real_ spherical surface—it applies to the mathematical one”—this gives the impression that the mathematical proposition differs from an empirical proposition particularly in that, whereas the truth of the empirical proposition is fluctuating and approximate, the mathematical proposition describes _its_ object as exactly and absolutely true. As if the ‘mathematical sphere’ were actually a sphere. And one might ask, for example, whether there is only _one_ such sphere, or whether there are several (a Fregean question).

And are these _calculations_ any less valid for that reason? In _one_ respect, of course, his understanding stands on shaky ground; but he will certainly draw his conclusions, and his calculus will stand on _solid_ ground.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#50.3+51.1) <span class="fac">[50\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/50.webp),[51\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/51.webp)</span> {#ms-126-503511}

November 5, 1942

‘Ideal object’. “The sign ‘a’ denotes an ideal object” is apparently meant to make a statement about the meaning, that is, the use of ‘a’. But it is interesting what the expression ‘ideal object’ makes of this fact.

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#51.3+52.1) <span class="fac">[51\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/51.webp),[52\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/52.webp)</span> {#ms-126-513521}

### [Ms-126](/ms-126/#58.4+59.1) <span class="fac">[58\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/58.webp),[59\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-126/59.webp)</span> {#ms-126-584591}
