_**Preface.**_

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

Perhaps the only person who will understand this book is someone who has themselves already thought the thoughts expressed in it – or at least similar thoughts. – So it is not a textbook. – Its purpose would be achieved if it gave pleasure to someone who reads it with understanding.

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

The book deals with philosophical problems and shows – I believe – that the way these problems are posed rests on a misunderstanding of the logic of our language. – One might sum up the whole sense of the book roughly in these words: Whatever can be said at all can be said clearly; and what one cannot talk about, one must keep silent about.

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

The book thus aims to draw a limit to thinking, or rather – not to thinking, but to the expression of thoughts: for in order to draw a limit to thinking, we would have to be able to think both sides of that limit (so we would have to be able to think what cannot be thought).

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

So the limit can be drawn only in language, and what lies beyond the limit will simply be nonsense.

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

I do not wish to judge how far my endeavours coincide with those of other philosophers. Indeed, what I have written here makes no claim to novelty in its details; and that is why I give no sources, since it makes no difference to me whether, before I did, someone else had already thought what I have thought.

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp)</span>

I will mention only that I owe much of the impetus for my thoughts to Frege’s magnificent works and to the writings of my friend Mr Bertrand Russell.

### <span class="fac">[Ir\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/Ir.webp),[IIr\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/IIr.webp)</span>

If this work has any value, it is twofold. First, thoughts are expressed in it, and this value will be the greater, the better the thoughts are expressed. The more squarely the nail is hit on the head. – Here I am conscious of having fallen far short of what is possible. Simply because my powers are not equal to the task. – May others come and do it better.

### <span class="fac">[IIr\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/IIr.webp)</span>

On the other hand, the _truth_ of the thoughts communicated here seems to me unassailable and definitive. I am therefore of the opinion that I have solved the problems once and for all in their essentials. And if I am not mistaken about this, the value of this work consists, secondly, in its showing how little is achieved by the fact that these problems have been solved.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-1r.11) <span class="fac">[1r\[11\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/1r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">2.012</span> In logic nothing is accidental: if a thing _can_ occur in a state of affairs, then the possibility of that state of affairs must already be settled in advance in the thing.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-1r.12) <span class="fac">[1r\[12\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/1r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">2.0121</span> It would seem, as it were, accidental if a situation were to fit, after the fact, a thing that could exist on its own.

If things can occur in states of affairs, this must already lie in the things themselves.

(Something logical cannot be merely-possible. Logic deals with every possibility, and all possibilities are its facts.)

Just as we cannot conceive of spatial objects outside space at all, or of temporal objects outside time, so there is _no_ object we can conceive of outside the possibility of its combination with others.

If I can conceive of an object as part of a state of affairs, I cannot conceive of it outside the _possibility_ of its being so combined.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-1r.13) <span class="fac">[1r\[13\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/1r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">2.0122</span> A thing is independent in so far as it can occur in all _possible_ situations, but this form of independence is a form of connection with the state of affairs, a form of dependence. (It is impossible for words to occur in two different ways, on their own and in a sentence.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-1r.14) <span class="fac">[1r\[14\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/1r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">2.0123</span> If I know an object, then I also know every possibility of its occurring in states of affairs.

(Each such possibility must lie in the nature of the object.)

A new possibility cannot be found afterwards.

The condensation of the symbol for a complex into a simple symbol can be expressed by a definition.

An expression marks out a form and a content.

I call such a variable a “sentence variable”.

How the sentences are described is inessential.

<span class="series-number">3.322</span> It can never indicate the feature common to two objects that we designate them with the same sign but by two different _modes of designation_. For the sign is arbitrary, after all. One could therefore choose two different signs instead, and where would that leave what is common in the designation?

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-9r.6) <span class="fac">[9r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/9r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.323</span> In ordinary language it happens exceedingly often that the same word designates in different ways – and so belongs to different symbols – or that two words that designate in different ways are outwardly applied in the same way in a sentence.

Thus the word “is” appears as a copula, as an equality sign and as an expression of existence; “exist” as an intransitive verb like “walk”; “identical” as an adjective; we speak of _Something_ but also of _something_ happening.

(In the sentence “Grün ist grün” – where the first word is a person’s name and the last an adjective – these words do not merely have different meanings but are _different symbols_.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-9r.7) <span class="fac">[9r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/9r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.324</span> In this way the most fundamental confusions readily arise (and the whole of philosophy is full of them).

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-9r.8) <span class="fac">[9r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/9r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.325</span> To avoid these errors we must use a sign-language that rules them out by not using the same sign in different symbols and by not using signs that designate in different ways outwardly in the same way. A sign-language, then, that obeys _logical_ grammar – logical syntax.

(Frege’s and Russell’s concept-script is such a language, though it still does not rule out all errors.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-10r.6) <span class="fac">[10r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/10r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.332</span> No sentence can say anything about itself, because the sentence-sign cannot be contained in itself (that is the whole “Theory of types”).

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-10r.7) <span class="fac">[10r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/10r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.333</span> A function cannot be its own argument because the function sign already contains the prototype of its argument and cannot contain itself.

For suppose the function F (fx) could be its own argument; then there would be a sentence “F(F(fx))”, and in it the outer function F and the inner function F must have different meanings, for the inner has the form φ(fx), the outer the form ψ(φ(fx)). The only thing the two functions have in common is the letter “F”, which by itself designates nothing.

This is immediately clear if instead of “F(F(u))” we write “(∃φ):F(φ u)․φu = Fu”.

This disposes of Russell’s paradox.

<span class="series-number">4.061</span> If one overlooks the fact that the sentence has a sense independent of the facts, one can easily come to believe that true and false are relations of equal standing between a sign and what it designates.

One might then say, for example, that “p” designates in the true way what “~p” designates in the false way, etc.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-14r.8+15r.1) <span class="fac">[14r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/14r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.062</span> Can one not communicate with false sentences just as one has hitherto done with true ones? So long as one knows they are meant to be false. No! For a sentence is true if things are as we say they are by means of it; and if by “p” we mean ~p and things are as we mean them to be, then “p” on this new understanding is true and not false.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-15r.2) <span class="fac">[14r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/14r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.0621</span> But it is important that the signs “p” and “~p” can say the same thing. For it shows that nothing in reality corresponds to the sign “~”.

The mere occurrence of negation in a sentence is not yet a mark of its sense. (~~p = p).

The sentences “p” and “~p” have opposite senses, but one and the same reality corresponds to them.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-15r.3) <span class="fac">[14r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/14r.webp),[15r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/15r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.063</span> A picture to explain the concept of truth: A black patch on white paper; one can describe the shape of the patch by specifying for each point on the surface whether it is white or black. To the fact that a point is black there corresponds a positive fact – to the fact that a point is white (not black), a negative fact. If I designate a point on the surface (a Fregean truth-value), this corresponds to the assumption put forward for judgement, etc. etc. But to be able to say that a point is black or white, I must first know when one calls a point black and when one calls it white; to be able to say “p” is true (or false), I must have determined under what circumstances I call “p” true, and in doing so I determine the sense of the sentence.

Now the point at which the simile breaks down is this: We can point to a point on the paper even without knowing what white and black are; but nothing at all corresponds to a sentence without sense, for it designates no thing (truth-value) whose properties might be called “false” or “true”; the verb of a sentence is not “is true” or “is false” – as Frege believed – but what “is true” must already contain the verb.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-15r.4) <span class="fac">[15r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/15r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.064</span> Every sentence must _already_ have a sense; affirmation cannot give it one, for it is precisely the sense that is affirmed. And the same holds for negation, etc.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-15r.5) <span class="fac">[15r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/15r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.0641</span> One might say: Negation already refers to the logical place determined by the negated sentence.

The negating sentence determines a _different_ logical place from the negated one.

The negating sentence determines a logical place with the aid of the logical place of the negated sentence, by describing the former as lying outside the latter.

The fact that one can negate the negated sentence again already shows that what is negated is already a sentence and not merely the preparation for one.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-16r.10) <span class="fac">[16r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/16r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.12</span> A sentence can represent the whole of reality, but it cannot represent what it must have in common with reality in order to represent it – logical form.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-16r.11) <span class="fac">[16r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/16r.webp)</span>

What expresses _itself_ in language, _we_ cannot express by means of it.

The sentence _shows_ the logical form of reality.

It exhibits it.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-16r.12) <span class="fac">[16r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/16r.webp)</span>

If two sentences contradict one another, their structure shows it; likewise if one follows from the other. And so on.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.1) <span class="fac">[16r\[10\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/16r.webp)</span>

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.2) <span class="fac">[16r\[11\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/16r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1213</span> Now we also understand our feeling that we have a correct logical conception if only everything in our sign-language is in order.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.4) <span class="fac">[17r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.122</span> In a certain sense we can speak of formal properties of objects and states of affairs, or of properties of the structure of facts, and in the same sense of formal relations and relations between structures.

(Instead of ‘property of the structure’ I also say “internal property”; instead of ‘relation between structures’, “internal relation”.

I introduce these expressions to show the reason for the confusion, very widespread among philosophers, between internal relations and relations proper (external relations).)

But the obtaining of such internal properties and relations cannot be asserted by sentences; rather, it shows itself in the sentences that represent those states of affairs and are about those objects.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.5) <span class="fac">[17r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1221</span> We can also call an internal property of a fact a feature of that fact. (In the sense in which we speak, for instance, of facial features.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.6) <span class="fac">[17r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.123</span> A property is internal if it is unthinkable that its object should not possess it.

(This shade of blue and that one stand in the internal relation of lighter to darker eo ipso. It is unthinkable that _these_ two objects should not stand in this relation.)

(Here the fluctuating use of the word “object” corresponds to the fluctuating use of the words “property” and “relation”.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.7) <span class="fac">[17r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.124</span> The obtaining of an internal property of a possible situation is not expressed by a sentence, but expresses itself in the sentence representing that situation, through an internal property of that sentence.

It would be just as nonsensical to ascribe a formal property to a sentence as to deny it that property.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-17r.8) <span class="fac">[17r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1241</span> One cannot distinguish forms from one another by saying that one has this property but the other has that one; for this presupposes that it makes sense to predicate both properties of both forms.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-18r.1) <span class="fac">[17r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.125</span> The obtaining of an internal relation between possible situations expresses itself in language through an internal relation between the sentences representing them.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-18r.2) <span class="fac">[17r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1251</span> This settles the disputed question “whether all relations are internal or external”.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-18r.3) <span class="fac">[17r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/17r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1252</span> I call series ordered by _internal_ relations series of forms.

The series of numbers is ordered not by an external relation but by an internal one.

Likewise the series of sentences “aRb”, “(∃x):aRx.xRb”, “(∃x,y):aRx.xRy.yRb”, and so forth.

(If b stands in one of these relations to a, then I call b a successor of a.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-18r.4+19r.1) <span class="fac">[18r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.126</span> In the sense in which we speak of formal properties, we can now also speak of formal concepts.

(I introduce this expression to clarify the reason for the confusion between formal concepts and concepts proper that runs through the whole of the old logic.)

That something falls under a formal concept as an object of that concept cannot be expressed by a sentence. Rather, it shows itself in the sign for this object itself. (The name shows that it designates an object, the numeral that it designates a number, etc.)

Formal concepts cannot, after all, be represented by a function in the way that concepts proper can.

For their marks, the formal properties, are not expressed by functions.

The expression of the formal property is a feature of certain symbols. Thus the sign of the marks of a formal concept is a characteristic feature of all symbols whose meanings fall under the concept.

The expression of the formal concept, then, a propositional variable in which only this characteristic feature is constant.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.2) <span class="fac">[18r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.127</span> The propositional variable designates the formal concept, and its values designate the objects that fall under this concept.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.3) <span class="fac">[18r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1271</span> Every variable is the sign of a formal concept.

For every variable represents a constant form that all its values possess, and that can be understood as a formal property of those values.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.4) <span class="fac">[18r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18r.webp),[18av\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18av.webp),[19r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1272</span> Thus the variable name “x” is the proper sign for the pseudo-concept _object_.

Wherever the word “object” (“thing”, “item”, etc.) is used correctly, it is expressed in concept-script by the variable name.

For example, in the sentence “there are 2 objects which …” by “(∃x,y)…”.

Wherever it is used otherwise, that is, as a concept-word proper, nonsensical pseudo-sentences arise.

Thus one cannot, for example, say “There are objects.” as one might say “There are books.”. Nor can one say “There are 100 objects.” or “There are <img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/4e318f3951cc8018.svg" alt="{ℵ}&#95;{0}" style="width:1.100em;height:0.894em;vertical-align:-0.164em"> objects.”

And it is nonsensical to speak of the _number of all objects_. The same holds for the words “complex”, “fact”, “function”, “number”, etc.

They all designate formal concepts and are represented in concept-script by variables, not by functions or classes. (As Frege and Russell believed.)

Expressions like “1 is a number”, “there is only one zero” and all similar expressions are nonsensical.

(It is just as nonsensical to say “there is only one 1” as it would be to say: 2 + 2 equals 4 at 3 o’clock.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.5) <span class="fac">[18av\[1\]-relocated](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/18av.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.12721</span> The formal concept is already given with an object that falls under it. One cannot, therefore, introduce objects of a formal concept _and_ the formal concept itself as primitive concepts. One cannot, for example, introduce both the concept of function and particular functions (as Russell does) as primitive concepts; or the concept of number and particular numbers.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.6) <span class="fac">[19r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1273</span> If we want to express the general proposition “b is a successor of a” in concept-script, we need an expression for the general term of the series of forms: aRb, (∃x):aRx.xRb, (∃x,y):aRx.xRy.yRb, ..... . One can express the general term of a series of forms only by a variable, for the concept term of this series of forms is a formal concept. (Frege and Russell overlooked this; the way in which they want to express general propositions like the one above is therefore wrong; it contains a circulus vitiosus.)

We can determine the general term of the series of forms by specifying its first term and the general form of the operation that generates the next term from the preceding sentence.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19r.7) <span class="fac">[19r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.1274</span> The question of the existence of a formal concept is nonsensical. For no proposition can answer such a question.

(Thus one cannot, for example, ask: “Are there unanalysable subject–predicate sentences?”)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-19ar.8+20r.1) <span class="fac">[19ar\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19ar.webp)</span>

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-20r.2) <span class="fac">[19ar\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19ar.webp)</span>

(If I introduce a new sign “b” by means of an equation by stipulating that it is to replace an already familiar sign “a”, I write the equation – definition – (as Russell does) in the form “a = b Def”. The definition is a rule for signs.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-20r.3) <span class="fac">[19ar\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19ar.webp)</span>

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-20r.4) <span class="fac">[19ar\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/19ar.webp),[20r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/20r.webp)</span>

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-20r.5) <span class="fac">[20r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/20r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.3</span> The truth-possibilities of the elementary propositions signify the possibilities of the obtaining and non-obtaining of states of affairs.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-21r.8) <span class="fac">[21r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/21r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.43</span> We can express agreement with the truth-possibilities by assigning them, say, the mark “W” (true) in the schema.

The absence of this mark signifies disagreement.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-21r.9+22r.1) <span class="fac">[21r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/21r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.431</span> The expression of agreement and disagreement with the truth-possibilities of the elementary propositions expresses the truth-conditions of the proposition.

A proposition is the expression of its truth-conditions.

(Frege was therefore quite right to set them out at the start to explain the signs of his concept-script. It is just that Frege’s explanation of the concept of truth is wrong: if “the True” and “the False” really were objects and were the arguments in ~p etc., then on Frege’s definition the sense of “~p” would by no means be determined.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-22r.2) <span class="fac">[21r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/21r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.44</span> The sign that results from correlating those “W” marks with the truth-possibilities is a propositional sign.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-22r.3) <span class="fac">[21r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/21r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.441</span> It is clear that no object (or complex of objects) corresponds to the complex of signs “F” and “W”, any more than to the horizontal and vertical lines or to the brackets. – There are no “logical objects”.

An analogous point of course holds for all signs that express the same thing as the schemata of “W”s and “F”s.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-22r.4+23r.1) <span class="fac">[21r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/21r.webp),[22r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/22r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.442</span> For example:

<img class="math math-block" src="../math/4bacfbc99dc0abe4.svg" alt="\text{„}\begin{array}{c|c||c} p &amp; q &amp; \\ \hline\hline W &amp; W &amp; W \\ \hline F &amp; W &amp; W \\ \hline W &amp; F &amp;  \\ \hline F &amp; F &amp; W \\ \hline \end{array}\text{”}" style="display:block;width:7.317em;height:7.223em">

is a propositional sign. (Frege’s “judgement stroke” “⊢” has no logical significance at all; in Frege (and Russell) it indicates only that these authors take the propositions so marked to be true. “⊢” is therefore no more a part of the structure of the proposition than, say, the proposition’s number. A proposition cannot possibly say of itself that it is true.)

If the order of the truth-possibilities in the schema is fixed once and for all by a rule of combination, then the last column by itself already expresses the truth-conditions. If we write this column as a row, the propositional sign becomes:

“(W W – W) (p, q)” or, more clearly, “(W W F W) (p, q)”

(The number of places in the left-hand brackets is determined by the number of terms in the right-hand ones.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.2) <span class="fac">[22r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/22r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.45</span> For n elementary propositions there are L<sub>n</sub> possible groups of truth-conditions.

The groups of truth-conditions that belong to the truth-possibilities of a given number of elementary propositions can be arranged in a series.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.3) <span class="fac">[22r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/22r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.46</span> Among the possible groups of truth-conditions there are two extreme cases.

In the one case the proposition is true for all the truth-possibilities of the elementary propositions. We say the truth-conditions are _tautological_.

In the second case the proposition is false for all the truth-possibilities: the truth-conditions are _contradictory_.

In the first case we call the proposition a tautology, in the second a contradiction.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.4) <span class="fac">[22r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/22r.webp),[23r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.461</span> A proposition shows what it says; tautology and contradiction show that they say nothing.

A tautology has no truth-conditions, for it is unconditionally true: and a contradiction is true under no condition.

Tautology and contradiction are senseless.

(Like the point from which two arrows diverge in opposite directions.)

(For example, I know nothing about the weather if I know that it is raining or not raining.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.5) <span class="fac">[23r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.4611</span> But tautology and contradiction are not nonsensical; they belong to the symbolism, rather as “0” belongs to the symbolism of arithmetic.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.6) <span class="fac">[23r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.462</span> Tautology and contradiction are not pictures of reality. They depict no possible situation. For the former allows _every_ possible situation, the latter _none_.

In a tautology the conditions of agreement with the world – the relations of representation – cancel one another out, so that it stands in no representational relation to reality.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.7+23r.8+24r.1) <span class="fac">[23r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.463</span> The truth-conditions determine how much room the proposition leaves the facts.

(In a negative sense the proposition, the picture, the model are like a solid body that limits the freedom of movement of other bodies; in a positive sense, like the space bounded by solid matter in which a body has room.)

A tautology leaves the whole – infinite – logical space to reality; a contradiction fills the whole of logical space and leaves reality not a single point. Neither can therefore determine reality in any way.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-23r.8-relocated) <span class="fac">[23r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.464</span> The truth of a tautology is certain, that of a proposition possible, that of a contradiction impossible.

(Certain, possible, impossible: here we have an indication of the gradation we need in probability theory.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-24r.2) <span class="fac">[23r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.465</span> The logical product of a tautology and a proposition says the same as the proposition. So that product is identical with the proposition. For one cannot change what is essential to the symbol without changing its sense.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-24r.3) <span class="fac">[23r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23r.webp),[23av\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23av.webp),[24r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/24r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.466</span> A determinate logical combination of signs corresponds to a determinate logical combination of their meanings; a combination of _any kind whatever_ corresponds only to uncombined signs.

That is, propositions that are true for every situation cannot be combinations of signs at all, for otherwise only determinate combinations of objects could correspond to them. (And no combination of objects corresponds to no logical combination.)

Tautology and contradiction are the limiting cases of the combination of signs, namely its dissolution.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-24r.4) <span class="fac">[23av\[1\]-relocated](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/23av.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.4661</span> Admittedly, even in tautology and contradiction the signs are still combined, i.e. they stand in relations to one another, but these relations are devoid of meaning, inessential to the _symbol_.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-24r.5) <span class="fac">[24r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/24r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.5</span> It now seems possible to specify the most general sentential form: that is, to describe the sentences of _any_ sign-language in such a way that every possible sense can be expressed by a symbol that fits the description, and that every symbol that fits the description can express a sense if the meanings of the names are chosen accordingly.

It is clear that in describing the most general sentential form _only_ what is essential to it may be described – for otherwise it would not be the most general.

That there is a general sentential form is proved by the fact that there cannot be a sentence whose form one could not have foreseen (i.e. constructed). The general form of the sentence is: Things stand thus and so.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-24r.6) <span class="fac">[24r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/24r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.51</span> Suppose I were given _all_ the elementary propositions: then one may simply ask: which propositions can I form from them? And those are _all_ the propositions, and _that_ is how they are delimited.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-25r.4) <span class="fac">[24r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/24r.webp),[25r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/25r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.02</span> It is easy to confuse the arguments of functions with the indices of names. For both the argument and the index tell me the meaning of the sign in which they occur. In Russell’s “<img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/471beaa05c623a4b.svg" alt="{+}&#95;{c}" style="width:1.131em;height:0.708em;vertical-align:-0.158em">”, for example, “c” is an index indicating that the whole sign is the sign for addition of cardinal numbers. For Frege the propositions of logic were names, and their arguments the indices of these names.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-27r.4) <span class="fac">[26r\[12\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/26r.webp),[27r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/27r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.4711</span> To specify the essence of the proposition is to specify the essence of all description, hence the essence of the world.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-33r.8) <span class="fac">[33r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.472</span> The description of the most general sentential form is the description of the one and only general primitive sign of logic.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-33r.9+34r.1) <span class="fac">[33r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.473</span> Logic must look after itself.

A _possible_ sign must also be capable of designating. Everything that is possible in logic is also permissible. (“Socrates is identical” means nothing because there is no property called “identical”. The sentence is nonsensical because we have not made an arbitrary stipulation, not because the symbol is in and of itself inadmissible.)

In a certain sense we cannot go wrong in logic.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.2) <span class="fac">[33r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.4731</span> The self-evidence of which Russell spoke so much can become dispensable in logic only through language itself preventing every logical error. – For logic to be a priori is for it to be impossible to think _illogically_.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.3) <span class="fac">[33r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.4732</span> We cannot give a sign the wrong sense.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.4) <span class="fac">[33r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.47321</span> Ockham’s maxim is of course not an arbitrary rule, nor one justified by its practical success: it says that _unnecessary_ sign-units mean nothing.

Signs that serve _one_ purpose are logically equivalent, signs that serve _no_ purpose logically meaningless.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.5) <span class="fac">[33r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/33r.webp),[34r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.4733</span> Frege says: Every legitimately formed sentence must have a sense; and I say: Every possible sentence is legitimately formed, and if it has no sense, that can only be because we have given some of its constituents no _meaning_.

(Even if we believe we have done so.) Thus “Socrates is identical” says nothing because we have given the word “identical” _no meaning as an adjective_. For when it occurs as an equality sign, it symbolizes in an altogether different way – the designating relation is a different one – so the symbol too is altogether different in the two cases; it is only by chance that the two symbols have the sign in common.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.6) <span class="fac">[34r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.474</span> The number of basic operations needed depends _only_ on our notation.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-34r.7) <span class="fac">[34r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.475</span> All that matters is to form a system of signs with a determinate number of dimensions – with a determinate mathematical multiplicity.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.1) <span class="fac">[34r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.476</span> It is clear that what is at issue here is not a _number of basic concepts_ that have to be designated, but the expression of a rule.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.2) <span class="fac">[34r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5</span> Every truth-function is a result of the successive application of the operation (-----W) (ξ, ....) to elementary propositions.

This operation negates all the propositions in the right-hand brackets, and I call it the negation of these propositions.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.3) <span class="fac">[34r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.501</span> I indicate a bracketed expression whose terms are sentences – when the order of the terms in the brackets does not matter – by a sign of the form „<img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/f038c2b607c599c6.svg" alt="(\overline{\xi})" style="width:1.388em;height:1.277em;vertical-align:-0.226em">”. “ξ” is a variable whose values are the terms of the bracketed expression; and the stroke over the variable indicates that it stands for all its values within the brackets.

(Thus if ξ has, say, the 3 values P, Q, R, then <img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/0747aa540e4b703b.svg" alt="(\overline{\xi})=(P,\;Q,\;R)" style="width:6.997em;height:1.277em;vertical-align:-0.226em">.)

The values of the variable are specified.

The specification is the description of the sentences for which the variable stands.

How the terms of the bracketed expression are described is inessential.

We _can_ distinguish three kinds of description: 1.) Direct enumeration. In this case we can simply put the constant values of the variable in its place. 2.) Specification of a function fx whose values for all values of x are the sentences to be described. 3.) Specification of a formal law according to which those sentences are formed. In this case the terms of the bracketed expression are all the terms of a series of forms.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.4) <span class="fac">[34r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/34r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.502</span> Thus instead of “(-----W)(ξ, ....)” I write “<img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/a651cfb90ab95fec.svg" alt="N(\overline{\xi})" style="width:2.192em;height:1.277em;vertical-align:-0.226em">”.

<img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/a651cfb90ab95fec.svg" alt="N(\overline{\xi})" style="width:2.192em;height:1.277em;vertical-align:-0.226em"> is the negation of all the values of the sentence variable ξ.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.5) <span class="fac">[35r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.503</span> Since it is evidently easy to express how propositions can be formed with this operation and how they cannot be formed with it, it must also be possible to give exact expression to this.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.6) <span class="fac">[35r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.51</span> If ξ has only one value, then <img class="math math-inline math-stacked" src="../math/26c6a6c49dc3743e.svg" alt="N(\overline{\xi})={\sim}p" style="width:4.766em;height:1.327em;vertical-align:-0.276em"> (not p); if it has two values, then <img class="math math-inline" src="../math/1fbc1f0e442c84c6.svg" alt="N(\overline{\xi})={\sim}p\cdot{\sim}q" style="width:6.711em;height:1.327em;vertical-align:-0.276em"> (neither p nor q).

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.7) <span class="fac">[35r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.511</span> How can the all-encompassing logic that mirrors the world make use of such particular hooks and manipulations? Only by all of these linking up into an infinitely fine network to form the great mirror.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-35r.8+36r.1) <span class="fac">[35r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.512</span> “~p” is true if “p” is false. So in the true sentence “~p”, “p” is a false sentence. How, then, can the stroke “~” bring it into agreement with reality?

But what negates in “~p” is not the “~”, but what is common to all the signs in this notation that negate p.

That is, the common rule by which “~p”, “~~~p”, “~p⌵~p”, “~p.~p”, etc. etc. (ad inf.) are formed. And what they have in common mirrors negation.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.2) <span class="fac">[35r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.513</span> One might say: What all symbols that affirm both p and q have in common is the proposition “p.q”. What all symbols that affirm either p or q have in common is the proposition “p⌵q”.

And so one can say: Two propositions are opposed to one another if they have nothing in common, and: Each proposition has only one negative, because there is only one proposition that lies wholly outside it.

Thus it also shows itself in Russell’s notation that “q:p⌵~p” says the same as “q”. That “p⌵~p” says nothing.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.3) <span class="fac">[35r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.514</span> Once a notation is fixed, there is in it a rule by which all sentences negating p are formed, a rule by which all sentences affirming p are formed, a rule by which all sentences affirming p or q are formed, and so on. These rules are equivalent to the symbols, and the sense of the symbols is mirrored in them.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.4) <span class="fac">[35r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.515</span> It must be apparent from our symbols that what is linked together by “⌵”, “.”, etc. must be sentences.

And this is indeed so, for the symbols “p” and “q” themselves presuppose “⌵”, “~”, etc. If the sign “p” in “p⌵q” does not stand for a complex sign, then on its own it cannot have sense; but then the signs “p⌵p”, “p.p”, etc., which have the same sense as “p”, cannot have sense either. But if “p⌵p” has no sense, then “p⌵q” cannot have sense either.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.5) <span class="fac">[35r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/35r.webp),[36r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5151</span> Must the sign of the negative sentence be formed using the sign of the positive one? Why should one not be able to express the negative sentence by means of a negative fact? (For instance: If “a” does not stand in a particular relation to “b”, this could express that aRb is not the case.)

But here too, of course, the negative sentence is formed indirectly by means of the positive one.

The positive _sentence_ must presuppose the existence of the negative _sentence_, and vice versa.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.6) <span class="fac">[36r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.52</span> If the values of ξ are all the values of a function fx for all values of x, then we get <img class="math math-inline" src="../math/54a76de029a1287b.svg" alt="N(\overline{\xi})={\sim}(\exists x)\cdot fx" style="width:7.891em;height:1.327em;vertical-align:-0.276em">.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.7) <span class="fac">[36r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.521</span> I separate the concept _All_ from the truth-function.

Frege and Russell introduced generality in connection with the logical product or the logical sum. This made it difficult to understand the sentences “(∃x)․fx” and “(x)․fx”, in which both ideas are contained.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.8) <span class="fac">[36r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.522</span> What is distinctive about the designation of generality is, first, that it points to a logical prototype and, second, that it singles out constants.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-36r.9) <span class="fac">[36r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.523</span> The designation of generality occurs as an argument.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.1) <span class="fac">[36r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.524</span> If the objects are given, then _all_ objects are already given to us with them.

If the elementary propositions are given, then _all_ elementary propositions are given with them too.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.2) <span class="fac">[36r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.525</span> It is incorrect to render the sentence “(∃x)․fx” – as Russell does – in words as “fx is _possible_”.

The certainty, possibility, or impossibility of a situation is expressed not by a sentence, but by an expression’s being a tautology, a sentence with sense, or a contradiction.

That precedent to which one would always like to appeal must already lie in the symbol itself.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.3) <span class="fac">[36r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.526</span> One can describe the world completely by means of fully generalized sentences, that is, without assigning any name to a particular object in advance.

To arrive at the ordinary way of expressing oneself, one then simply has to say, after an expression “there is one and only one x such that…”: And this x is a.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.4) <span class="fac">[36r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/36r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5261</span> A fully generalized sentence is composite, like any other sentence. (This shows itself in the fact that in “(∃x,φ)․φx” we have to mention “φ” and “x” separately.) The two stand independently in designating relations to the world, as in the ungeneralized sentence.

A mark of a composite symbol: It has something in common with _other_ symbols.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.6) <span class="fac">[37r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5262</span> After all, the truth or falsity of _every_ proposition alters something in the general structure of the world. And the latitude that the totality of elementary propositions leaves to its structure is precisely what the fully general propositions delimit.

(If an elementary proposition is true, then at any rate one _more_ elementary proposition is true.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.7) <span class="fac">[37r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.53</span> I express sameness of object by sameness of sign, and not with the aid of an equality sign. Difference of objects by difference of signs.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.8) <span class="fac">[37r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5301</span> It is evident that identity is not a relation between objects. This becomes quite clear if one considers, for example, the proposition “(x):fx․⊃․x = a”. What this proposition says is simply that _only_ a satisfies the function f, not that only things that stand in a certain relation to a satisfy the function f.

To be sure, one might now say that _only_ a stands in this relation to a, but to express this we would need the equality sign itself.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.9) <span class="fac">[37r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5302</span> Russell’s definition of “ = ” is not adequate, because on that definition one cannot say that two objects have all their properties in common. (Even if this proposition is never true, it still has _sense_.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.10) <span class="fac">[37r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5303</span> In passing: to say of _two_ things that they are identical is nonsense, and to say of _one_ thing that it is identical with itself is to say nothing at all.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-37r.11+38r.1) <span class="fac">[37r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.531</span> So I write not “f(a,b)․a = b” but “f(a,a)” (or “f(b,b)”). And not “f(a,b)․~a = b” but “f(a,b)”.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.2) <span class="fac">[37r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.532</span> And analogously: Not “(∃x,y)․f(x,y)․x = y” but “(∃x).f(x,x)”; and: Not “(∃x,y)․f(x,y)․~x = y” but “(∃x,y)․f(x,y)”.

(So instead of Russell’s “(∃x,y)․f(x,y)”: “(∃x,y)․f(x,y).⌵.(∃x).f(x,x)”.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.3) <span class="fac">[37r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5321</span> So, for example, instead of “(x):fx⊃x = a” we write “fa: ~(∃x,y)․fx.fy”.

And the sentence “_only_ one x satisfies f( )” reads: “(∃x)․fx: ~(∃x,y)․fx.fy”.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.4) <span class="fac">[37r\[10\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.533</span> So the equality sign is not an essential part of a concept-script.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.5) <span class="fac">[37r\[11\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.534</span> And now we see that pseudo-sentences such as “a = a”, “a = b.b = c.⊃.a = c”, “(x).x = x”, “(∃x).x = a”, etc. cannot be written down at all in a correct concept-script.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.6) <span class="fac">[37r\[12\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/37r.webp),[38r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.535</span> This also disposes of all the problems that were bound up with such pseudo-sentences.

All the problems that Russell’s “Axiom of infinity” brings with it are to be resolved here already. What the Axiom of infinity is supposed to say would be expressed in language by there being infinitely many names with different meanings.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.7) <span class="fac">[38r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5351</span> There are certain cases in which one is tempted to use expressions of the form “a = a” or “p⊃p” and the like. This happens when one wants to speak of the prototype: sentence, thing, etc. Thus in the “Principles of Mathematics” Russell rendered the nonsense “p is a sentence” in symbols as “p⊃p” and placed it as a hypothesis before certain sentences so that their argument places could be occupied only by sentences.

(It is nonsense to put the hypothesis p⊃p before a sentence to ensure that it has arguments of the right form, if only because with a non-sentence as argument the hypothesis becomes not false but nonsensical, and because arguments of the wrong kind make the sentence itself nonsensical, so that it guards itself against the wrong arguments just as well, or just as badly, as the senseless hypothesis attached for this purpose.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-38r.8) <span class="fac">[38r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5352</span> Similarly, one wanted to express “There are no _things_” by “~(∃x)․ x = x”. But even if this were a sentence, would it not also be true if “there were things”, but they were not identical with themselves?

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.1) <span class="fac">[38r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.54</span> In the general sentential form, a proposition occurs in a proposition only as the basis of truth-operations.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.2) <span class="fac">[38r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.541</span> At first glance it seems as though a sentence could also occur in another in a different way.

Especially in certain sentential forms of psychology, such as “A believes that p is the case” or “A thinks p”, etc.

For on the surface it seems here as though the sentence p stands in a kind of relation to an object A.

(And in modern epistemology (Russell, Moore etc.) those sentences have indeed been understood in this way.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.3) <span class="fac">[38r\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.542</span> But it is clear that “A believes that p”, “A thinks p”, “A says p” are of the form “‘p’ says p”: And what we have here is not a correlation between a fact and an object, but a correlation of facts by correlating their objects.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.4) <span class="fac">[38r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5421</span> This also shows that the mind – the subject, etc. – as conceived in today’s superficial psychology, is an absurdity.

For a composite mind would no longer be a mind.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.5) <span class="fac">[38r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/38r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5422</span> The correct explanation of the form of the sentence “A judges p” must show that it is impossible to judge nonsense. (Russell’s theory does not meet this condition.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.6) <span class="fac">[39r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/39r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.5423</span> To perceive a complex is to perceive that its constituents stand in such-and-such relations to one another.

This presumably also explains why one can see the figure

![](../graphics/202,39r.svg)

as a _cube_ in two ways; and all similar phenomena. For we really do see two different facts.

(If I look first at corner a and only glance at b, a appears at the front; and vice versa.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.7) <span class="fac">[39r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/39r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.55</span> We must now answer a priori the question of all the possible forms of elementary propositions.

An elementary proposition consists of names. But since we cannot specify the number of names with different meanings, neither can we specify the composition of the elementary proposition.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.8) <span class="fac">[39r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/39r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.551</span> Our principle is that every question that can be decided by logic at all must be decidable without further ado.

(And if we find ourselves having to answer such a problem by looking at the world, this shows that we are altogether on the wrong track.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-39r.9) <span class="fac">[39r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/39r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.552</span> The “experience” we need to understand logic is not that something is thus and so, but that something _is_; but that is precisely _not_ an experience.

<span class="series-number">6.341</span> Newtonian mechanics, for example, brings the description of the world into a uniform form. Let us imagine a white surface with irregular black patches on it. Now we say: whatever picture results from this, I can always come as close as I like to a description of it by covering the surface with a suitably fine square grid and then saying of each square that it is white or black. In this way I will have brought the description of the surface into a uniform form. This form is arbitrary, for I could have used a grid with triangular or hexagonal meshes with the same success. It may be that the description would have been simpler with a triangular grid; that is, that we could describe the surface more accurately with a coarser triangular grid than with a finer square one (or vice versa) and so on. Mechanics determines a form for describing the world by saying: all the sentences describing the world must be obtained in a given way from a number of given sentences – the axioms of mechanics. What makes it non-contingent cannot lie _in_ the world, for then it would itself be contingent.

It must lie outside the world.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-51r.6) <span class="fac">[51r\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.42</span> That is also why there can be no sentences of ethics.

Sentences cannot express anything higher.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-51r.7) <span class="fac">[51r\[8\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.421</span> It is clear that ethics cannot be put into words.

Ethics is transcendental.

(Ethics and aesthetics are one.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-51r.8) <span class="fac">[51r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51r.webp),[51av\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51av.webp),[51av\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51av.webp),[51av\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51av.webp),[52r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.422</span> The first thought when an ethical law of the form ‘thou shalt …’ is laid down is: And what then, if I do not do it? But it is clear that ethics has nothing to do with punishment and reward in the ordinary sense. So this question about the _consequences_ of an action must be beside the point. – At the very least, these consequences must not be events. For there must be something right about asking the question in that way. There must, to be sure, be a kind of ethical reward and ethical punishment, but they must lie in the action itself.

(And it is also clear that the reward must be something pleasant and the punishment something unpleasant.)

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-51r.9+52r.1) <span class="fac">[51av\[1\]-relocated](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/51av.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.423</span> The will as the bearer of the ethical cannot be spoken of.

And the will as a phenomenon is of interest only to psychology.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-52r.2) <span class="fac">[52r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.43</span> If good or evil willing changes the world, then it can change only the limits of the world, not the facts; not what can be expressed by language.

In short, the world must thereby become a different world altogether. It must, so to speak, wane or wax as a whole.

The world of the happy is different from that of the unhappy.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-52r.3) <span class="fac">[52r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.431</span> Just as in death, too, the world does not change but comes to an end.

<span class="series-number">6.4311</span> Death is not an event in life. One does not experience death.

If by eternity one means not infinite duration but timelessness, then whoever lives in the present lives eternally.

Our life is as endless as our visual field is boundless.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-52r.5) <span class="fac">[52r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.4312</span> Not only is the immortality in time of the human soul, that is, its living on for ever even after death, in no way guaranteed, but above all this assumption does not at all accomplish what one has always wanted it to accomplish. Is a riddle solved by my living on for ever? Is this eternal life, then, not just as riddling as the present one? The solution to the riddle of life in space and time lies _outside_ space and time.

(Not problems of natural science are to be solved.)

<span class="series-number">6.432</span> _How_ the world is is of no concern whatsoever to what is higher. God does not reveal himself _in_ the world.

<span class="series-number">6.45</span> Contemplating the world sub specie aeterni is contemplating it as a – bounded – whole.

The feeling of the world as a bounded whole is the mystical.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-52r.10) <span class="fac">[52r\[9\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.5</span> If one cannot put an answer into words, one cannot put the question into words either.

There is no such thing as _**the** riddle_.

If a question can be asked at all, it _can_ be answered.

### [TLP](/w-tlp/#ts-204-52r.11) <span class="fac">[52r\[10\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-202/52r.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.51</span> Scepticism is _not_ irrefutable, but obviously nonsensical when it seeks to doubt where no question can be asked.

For doubt can exist only where there is a question; a question only where there is an answer, and an answer only where something _can_ be _said_.

<span class="series-number">6.53</span> The right method of philosophy would really be this: to say nothing except what can be said, that is, sentences of the natural sciences – that is, something that has nothing to do with philosophy – and then, whenever someone else wanted to say something metaphysical, to show them that they had given no meaning to certain signs in their sentences. This method would be unsatisfying to the other person – they would not feel that we were teaching them philosophy – but _it_ would be the only strictly correct one.
