### <span class="fac">[1r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/1r.webp),[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/1v.webp)</span>

20.11.1937

I am troubled by the matter with Anna Rebni. (When I think about the entries in my diary, I often wonder whether it will also reflect well on me.) On a whim, I went to Anna Rebni & brought her some cakes. I told her that I wanted to be at peace with her; & some other things. We parted – on good terms. – I do not feel capable of working. ### <span class="fac">[1v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/1v.webp),[2r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/2v.webp)</span>

“_Red_ is, after all, this image. –” (While saying this, I look at something red.) But doesn’t it surprise you that you can give it a name? _What_ is the purpose of uttering sounds while you have the image? What is it useful for? And it doesn’t seem to us that we have assigned a name to something, but rather that we have simply said what we see. (Namely) as if the word ‘red’ & the color are _one._ And that means, we react with the word ‘red’. “But to the red color!” Yes, I could say: indifferent to what color, as long as it appears red to me. But what does it mean here, that a color appears red to me? I don’t _have_ any other samples of this color; so it seems to me that it _is_ called ‘red’. I cannot say: it appears to me as it appears to me. And yet, it seems to me that I could say: “Whether I am mistaken or not – this color appears _red_ to me.” It is as if I am stating a certain characteristic of the color of the object, the red characteristic. As if I were repeatedly applying the redness that I see to something else, as if I were repeatedly associating it with the color. (As if it made sense to say about Beethoven “he is a _Beethoven_!”, Watson drew my attention to a passage in Omar Khayyam: “and this … men call ‘the sky’”.)

### <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/2v.webp),[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/3v.webp),[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/4r.webp)</span>

“This image is _‘red’_” as if I could, by means of a single act during the definition of ‘red’, distinguish whether I mean the image or the object, or its color, by “red.” Of course, there is also: to _imbibe_ the color & other things; but how does that help me in defining it? In what way have I given the name to the _color_, if I directed my attention to the color when defining it? The definition is meant for _future_ use, & how is that mental act useful in it? One could say: What you _mean_ when defining, if this meaning is supposed to be a mental act, is completely irrelevant to the use & therefore to the meaning of the definition. What matters is what you do with this definition. _What_ you have named “red” is shown by the calculus with this word. I understand, of course, that the different ways of showing & applying attention when defining can be useful; _e.g._ for memorizing the definition. But it is a superstition that, by means of a certain mental act when defining, one gives the name not to the cheek but to the sensation “toothache.” As if one were thereby assigning the name to this & not that object. As if the one would receive it & not the other. As if one could, by means of an act of ‘meaning’ when defining, attach the name to this & not that. – This is not to be confused with: I can establish this associative connection & not another by means of a mental act. But: whether I have succeeded in establishing this & that connection is shown by the _consequences_. That is, whether I have memorized something through this way of looking at it is shown when my memory is put to the test.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-120-4r.2+4v.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/4v.webp)</span> {#4r-2--4v-1}

In religion, it would have to be the case that each level of religiousness corresponds to a type of expression that does not make sense at a lower level. For someone who is currently at the lower level, this teaching, which has meaning at the higher level, is null & void; it can only be understood _incorrectly_, & therefore these words do not apply to this person. The teaching, for example, of predestination, in Paul, is at my level of irreligiosity, an ugly nonsense. Therefore, it does not belong to me, because I can only apply the image offered to me incorrectly. If it is a pious & good image, then it is for a completely different level, at which it must be applied in life in a completely different way than I could apply it.

### <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/5r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[5r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/5r.webp),[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/5v.webp),[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/7r.webp)</span>

He gives _himself_ an ostensive definition; _we assume_ that he then remembers it – but this is not to say that he remembers it ‘correctly’, but that he has an experience of remembering; but how does that definition come into this? Perhaps something like this: that he _says_ “I remember that I called it that.” But it must also be possible without words! Fine, then say _how_! One could probably say: it seems to him as if he had given this definition, but that then has nothing to do with that actual definition. One could express it something like this: he gives a definition again later, & it is memory-laden. (This word merely avoids the reference to an actual earlier event.) So: he has given a definition; later he gives definitions again & they are memory-laden, or contain themselves a reference (through the language, for example) to an earlier event. – Firstly: shall we call this language, if its use, judged by other criteria, is without rules? – But can we not speak of an inner language? We can; but we now have no reason to apply the picture of ‘language’ to his case. It is a hypothesis that serves no purpose. – Secondly: shall we still say that he has an experience of remembering, if, _that_ is his behaviour? And we cannot say: _he_ must of course know whether it is one, or God must know it, if not he. That is to say, God would then know that he had the experience which one usually calls “experience of remembering”. Now, however, there is no experience which one calls, without qualification, I mean, without reference to other facts, an experience of remembering. There is – for example – a certain experience, the recalling of a name, after a brief pause: I mean _the_ case when one says: “What was it called? – – Oh yes: – ‘Kobalt blue’.” This could be called an experience of remembering. Would we call it that, however, if someone then always ‘recalled’ quite wrong words or even nonsensical sequences of sounds? And suppose someone wanted to say: “Yes, those could also be genuine experiences of remembering” – would the answer not be: One can of course call it that – for example, the gesture “gesture of remembering” – but it has now lost everything that interests us about remembering. One now sees that it was only an addition to what was essential to remembering.

### <span class="fac">[7r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/7r.webp),[7v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/7v.webp)</span>

“I must know whether I am in pain” – Does this mean: I must know that what I have ‘is’ pain? No – one might say – it means, I must know that what I have is pain. Compare: I must know that that is a chair. One can know all sorts of things about it: that one can sit on it, that it can be unfolded into a chair, etc., etc. You really mean: “I must know what I have.” Yes, perhaps – but what do you have? You can only answer this in words or gestures. The only correct thing to say is: it means nothing to ask: “Are you sure that you are in pain?” But not because the other person is obviously sure. The phrase: “I must know – – –” is, of course, used (hence the confusion), but consider on what occasions one might use it. (The doctor says: “But it doesn’t hurt that much!”)

### <span class="fac">[7v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/7v.webp),[8r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/8r.webp)</span>

I am now counting the days of my stay. A little less than 3 weeks. Whether I will experience them. – I am afraid of everything; of the stay, of the journey through Germany; even a little bit of Vienna, & of the future. Yes, I am, in a way, in a mess right now. I feel lost. God help me! To ‘find one’s place in the world’ – that is what matters. Not to ‘demand’ something from it. But to find one’s place in it, as it is. That is to say: not to create a fantasy for oneself, & then be surprised & offended that the world does not correspond to it. But I am, in fact, doing this. I feel rather miserable.

### <span class="fac">[8r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/8r.webp),[8v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/8v.webp)</span>

Under the concept of memory-experience, one primarily imagines a memory-image. Now, of course, there are memory-images – I can easily conjure some up in my mind. But _how_ do I conjure it up in my mind? And if I now fix it, _is it_ the memory? One says (for example): I see myself taking a walk with a friend there & there. But – how do I know that _it’s_ me & my friend? Is the portrait so well-captured? Of course not. But I _say_ that it’s me with my friend. I make this transition from the image to words, or from this image to certain others; etc.

### <span class="fac">[9v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/9v.webp),[10r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/10r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[11r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/11r.webp),[11v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/11v.webp),[12r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/12r.webp),[12v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/12v.webp)</span>

“We cannot know it – but he either has this experience or he does not.” What does this “he has it, or he does not” mean here? We leave everything open – & yet we assert something. And indeed, we offer a choice of two images. We say: it must look like this or that. We, as it were, sketch these images for you. Here is the situation, now choose! This is, as it were, a conjurer’s trick, for it takes the problematic for granted & then asserts something that everyone would admit anyway: “He who sees the whole series sees whether this digit is in it or not.” “He who looks into the mind of that person sees there either that this image is there, or not.” – But it is not about something being _in_ something, hidden in a mind (or the like), & seen by God, perhaps. In this picture already lies the misinterpretation of our grammar. One speaks of “turning one’s gaze inward”; one often does this by closing one’s eyes. And we feel sadness & sometimes also joy inside. The ‘inner’ voice of conscience sometimes appears to us as speaking from within, sometimes from without. One could also say that the word “inside” & “inner” used in this way stands for a physical gesture that seems to be caused in some way by a feeling in the chest (mainly). We press our hand to our heart & make various movements, with our head & shoulders, as if we were feeling something there. But if, on the other hand, I see or hear something, as happens almost continuously during my waking life, does the designation ‘_inner_ experience’ apply here?

### <span class="fac">[12v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/12v.webp),[13r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/13r.webp),[13v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/13v.webp)</span>

If I say: I have a certain mental image, so could the other also have it – to whom am I saying that? Does it make sense to say to myself: “I have…”? If I say it now, it seems similar to the statement: If a clock can lie in this drawer, then it can also lie in that one. The clock in this drawer shows us (then) how a clock lies in a drawer, how we are supposed to imagine it. And it is thus presented as if my pains show me how to imagine that the other person has pains. But if I were to imagine the pains of the _other_ after my pains, it would be, for example, pains that, as I would then have to express it, I have in the other’s body. Because I have experienced that a pain has moved from one place to another. But do I experience that I _have_ them? But what does that mean? To another I say: “I have pains,” for myself I only groan. And the groaning corresponds to the word “pains” but there is no talk of ‘I’. My groaning can also indicate to the other that _I_ have pains, namely by the fact that I groan. But if, when I have pains, _I_ (i.e., _my_ body) groan & not the other, it does not happen because I chose _my_ mouth to groan. That is, I do not want to express _thereby_ that it is precisely _I_ & not another who groans.

### <span class="fac">[13v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/13v.webp)</span>

I see a red spot & say: “That is _me_, who sees it.” – But above all, what does “that” mean here? (And it does not mean: what I mean by it.) But if I leave the meaning of “that” in abeyance, what meaning does the sentence still have?

### <span class="fac">[13v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/13v.webp),[14r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/14r.webp),[14v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/14v.webp)</span>

“If I can have pain, so can another. Just as I can have it, so can another.” – But I don't know at all the something that can pass from me to him. This sentence actually means: You must understand what it means for another to be in pain, if you know what it is to be in pain yourself! Like: “You must understand what it means for there to be a wick in that lamp, if I show you that there is one in this lamp.” But what if I can't make the transition? Or rather: what does it mean to make it? If it is an ‘immediate’ understanding, then it is on the same level as a _gesture_, where I, for example, make a painful face & point emphatically at the other. (Think of how one says to a small child that his doll has ‘owie owie’). But I could make _different_ transitions, & that shows that one cannot say: this & that is what one means by ‘pain’ & if you know what ‘I have,’ ‘you have’ etc. means, then you know what it means: ‘_he has pain_’.

### <span class="fac">[14v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/14v.webp)</span>

If you explain the word “to have” to someone & then, on one occasion, when he is in pain, you point to the painful spot & say: “Isn’t it true that you have something here? & what you have, another also has” – then he will probably not understand you.

### <span class="fac">[15r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/15r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[17r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/17r.webp),[17v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/17v.webp),[18r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/18r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[18r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/18r.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[18r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/18r.webp),[18v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/18v.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[146v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/146v.webp),[BCr\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-120/BCr.webp)</span>

26.04.1938

“The finite cannot grasp the infinite.” – One might say: “I look at your calculations – you are not doing anything infinite; they are just ordinary finite operations!” – But how! What else could one have expected? What does a finite operation look like? You speak as if the mathematician had set out to perform infinite operations & you only find finite ones. Don't you rather want to say: “Everything you do can also be understood in a quite simple finite way”? Because the mathematician did not set out to use an infinite number of signs (or the like), but rather the _word_ “infinitely many”. In his signs – one could say – he made a gesture pointing towards infinity. – (And what is a ‘gesture pointing towards infinity?’ one might ask.) The infinite would be something immensely large & I don't find in your considerations that anything like that is being discussed. The absolute use of ‘small’ & ‘large’. Compare: “The larger x becomes, the smaller y becomes” – & “When x becomes enormously large, y becomes infinitesimally small”.
