### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/1.webp)</span>

09.09.1946

If it is true that we do not judge the type & size of a limb’s movement by _feeling_, how would a person differ from us for whom this would be the case? Now, one could easily imagine that someone might have different degrees or different types of sensations of pain with different movements. He would, for example, say: “I feel this stabbing sensation when I bend my arm by about 90˚.”

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/1.webp),[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/2.webp)</span>

Imagine someone who can determine the depth of a spring using a divining rod, and according to the force it exerts. He learned to do this: he went over springs of various depths & remembered the force. (This could have been determined with a spring scale.) He associated the force with the depth & now infers the depth from the force. It could happen that he states the force – for example, in kg – & then moves on to the depth (perhaps even according to a table). But it could also be that he knows no other expression of the force than the depth. After some practice, he can correctly state the depth. If one does not move the rod through water, but rather lowers it through weights, he will now also say: “It pulls, like so many meters.”

### <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/2.webp),[3\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/3.webp)</span>

But it could still be the case that he would be able to correctly state the depth of a spring by means of the force of the rod, but not to correctly estimate the force of the rod. I mean this: it could be that water at different depths exerts the same force under different circumstances; & this diviner now says, for example: “This spring is deeper than the previous one; it pulls less” – & he is right, the spring is indeed deeper, but the force, measured with the spring scale, was the same & he did not remember _it_ correctly. – Should one in this case say that he judges the depth according to the force?

### <span class="fac">[3\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/3.webp),[4\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/4.webp)</span>

Well, he might say: “This force is that of a spring at a depth of ‥‥․” by studying this force – as one weighs a weight in one’s hand. Perhaps, however – “I cannot judge the force – the water is at a depth of ‥‥․.” In this (latter) case, one would not say that he judges the depth according to the force. (At least not ‘consciously’.)

### <span class="fac">[4\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/4.webp)</span>

Suppose someone says that he notices how far he has bent his arm by the strength of a pressure sensation in his elbow. This means: when it reaches a certain strength, he recognizes that the arm is bent to _that_ degree. Or what else should it mean: that he judges the degree of flexion according to the pressure sensation?

### <span class="fac">[4\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/4.webp),[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/5.webp)</span>

I mean: how does one know that one is judging something according to a feeling? – Is it enough for this that, when judging, one directs his attention to the feeling?

### <span class="fac">[5\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/5.webp)</span>

If you now say that it is necessary for this that someone can say: “If the pressure is _so_ strong, then my arm is bent 90˚” – then this ‘_So_’ must be expressible in terms of strength. Otherwise, it means that one judges the flexion according to the pressure sensation only that one cannot judge the flexion if one feels _no_ (or only a very weak) pressure sensation. (For example, if one is anesthetized.)

### <span class="fac">[5\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/5.webp),[6\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/6.webp)</span>

There are therefore different cases. It may be the case that someone _says_ he judges the flexion according to the pressure or pain sensation, & in a sense listens to this sensation; but otherwise cannot state the degree of the sensation in any way. – Or there may be two independent statements of the degree of the sensation & the flexion.

### <span class="fac">[11\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/11.webp)</span>

If a child draws a locomotive in a primitive way. Of course, the child does not consider the drawing to be a locomotive – but should I say that it _sees_ the drawing as a locomotive?? That is, do we have _here_ the phenomenon which one later refers to as “seeing something as something”?

### <span class="fac">[11\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/11.webp),[12\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/12.webp)</span>

When children play with trains – should I say that a child imitating the locomotive is seen as a locomotive by another? It is _understood_ as a locomotive in the game. I have seen a child build this shape

![](../graphics/132,11.svg)

with building blocks & called it “locomotive”.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-132-12.2) <span class="fac">[12\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/12.webp)</span> {#122}

In bullfighting, the bull is the hero of a tragedy. First made furious by pain, it dies a long & terrible death.

### <span class="fac">[12\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/12.webp),[13\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/13.webp)</span>

Imagine I showed that shape to an adult & asked “What does it remind you of,” & he answered “A locomotive” – does that mean he has _seen_ it as a locomotive? I take this to be the typical case of “seeing something as something” when someone says: “Now I see it as this, now as that”. When he knows different aspects & that apart from any use of the object being looked at. So I would like to say: I do not regard any _use_ of the picture as evidence that it is seen in this way or that.

### <span class="fac">[13\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/13.webp)</span>

‘And since then, face suffering with courage.’ I am generally in a crisis, so the misfortune that may befall me should not upset me. Why should something terrible not happen to me during this time of horrors?

### <span class="fac">[13\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/13.webp),[14\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/14.webp)</span>

So, if I said to someone, show me somewhere the shape

![](../graphics/132,13.svg)

& he pointed to a corner of a room – would that not be evidence that he has seen the picture in three dimensions?? And if he points, conversely, not to a corner, but to the point of an arrow – does that not mean that he sees the drawing in two dimensions?

### <span class="fac">[14\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/14.webp),[15\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/15.webp)</span>

But couldn't I ask: “How do you know that he doesn't simply see the corner of the room, & the point of the arrow in three dimensions?” Is it different if he can play the game of seeing the drawing in one way, then in another? Of course, he doesn't have to use the expression “seeing something as…” He could, for example, make signs with his hand, so that it seems as if he is depicting what he sees; but sometimes he uses one object for the depiction, sometimes another. – But how does it show that he doesn't believe he is seeing a change in the picture, in some other sense? How does it show that he doesn't believe that the _picture_ has changed? Because isn't that essential? That he sees that only the aspect has changed.

### <span class="fac">[15\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/15.webp),[16\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/16.webp)</span>

13.09.1946

He will, for example, be less ‘puzzled’ than ‘amused’ by this change. But that is all still superficial. How will he show that he sees that the picture has not changed? – He can, of course, _say_ it. But how will he _show_ what he means by the words? Well, one can imagine that.

### <span class="fac">[16\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/16.webp),[17\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/17.webp)</span>

I am now inclined to say: The phenomenon I am talking about here does not occur when I usually _see_. That is, I certainly know that there is a table there, that this picture depicts a landscape with a foreground & background, etc.; but there is no “seeing something as something” superimposed on the seeing. Now, what I say here, as it stands, is nonsense. I would like to say: “Well, I have my eyes open, & I act & speak in such a way that it expresses that I see a table there, a picture there, etc. etc.”

### <span class="fac">[17\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/17.webp)</span>

So, if someone says: This seeing is not as simple as it seems, it can be analyzed & only then do you see what the person is doing, how much he has had to learn, for example, before he _sees_ in this way, – I reply: I am not interested in the _story_ here, & we are not talking about something _complicated_, something composite. It is only complicated, difficult, to understand our concepts. Because their connection is complicated.

### <span class="fac">[17\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/17.webp),[18\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/18.webp)</span>

Wouldn't it be conceivable that we don't notice the abrupt change in aspect? and that, for example, we talk about a picture sometimes as _this_, sometimes as _that_, without remembering the _change_. So that we always take ‘as _what_ we see it’ for granted?

### <span class="fac">[18\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/18.webp)</span>

Or like this: Does a child understand what it means to see the table as a ‘table’? It learns: “This is a table, this is a bench,” etc., & it perfectly masters a language-game without any indication that this is about an aspect.

### <span class="fac">[19\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/19.webp)</span>

“Yes, a child doesn't analyze what it does.” – Again: we are not talking about an analysis of what is happening. Just an analysis – & this word is very misleading – of our concepts. And our concepts are more complicated than those of a child; in that our words have a more complicated use than theirs.

### <span class="fac">[19\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/19.webp),[20\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/20.webp)</span>

If I see the picture there as a coastline receding into the distance, this is expressed by the fact that I react to it with words, with descriptive hand movements. But now I don't react constantly, & so the question arises: Do I see it _always_ like that? And again one wants to say: How? & the answer is again a movement, or something like that. The question is wrongly put. (Reminds one of: Does the thing only weigh a pound as long as it is being weighed?)

### <span class="fac">[20\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/20.webp),[21\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/21.webp)</span>

And one would like to say: “But I see it _like_ that even when I don't express it. This seeing is a _state_ that lasts; like the existence of the picture itself.” And what is the answer to that? “But I see it _like_ that, even while I'm not expressing it.” That would mean that what I see does not change when I express it. If one asked: “Does the body only have this weight as long as it is being weighed?” – then this would mean: “Does its weight change when we put it on the scales?” And that is of course not at all what we want to ask.

### <span class="fac">[132\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/132.webp),[133\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/133.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[133\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/133.webp),[134\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/134.webp)</span>

What is objectionable about the idea that we study the use of a word, uncover errors in the description of this use, etc.? Above all, one wonders: how could _this_ be so important to us? It depends on whether one calls a ‘false description’ one that does not correspond to the sanctioned linguistic usage, or the one that does not correspond to the practice of the describer. Only in the second case does a philosophical conflict arise.

### <span class="fac">[192\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/192.webp),[193\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/193.webp),[194\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/194.webp)</span>

Less objectionable is the idea: we make _a false picture_ of thinking, for example. Because here one says to oneself: we are dealing with thinking at least, not with the word “thinking.” So, we make a false picture of thinking. – But _of what_ do we make a false picture; how do I know, for example, that you are making a false picture of _that_ of which I also make a false picture? Let's assume that the picture of thinking is a person who rests his head in his hand and talks to himself. Our question is not “Is this a correct picture?”, but: “How is this picture used as a picture of _thinking_?” Not: “We have made a false picture” – but: “We are not familiar with the use of our picture, or our pictures”! And therefore not familiar with the use of our word.

### <span class="fac">[194\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/194.webp),[195\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/195.webp)</span>

Well, – but this word is only interesting insofar as it actually has a very specific use for us, i.e., it already refers to a certain phenomenon! – That is true. And that means: we are not dealing with an improvement of grammatical conventions. – But what does it mean: “We all know what phenomenon the word ‘thinking’ refers to”? Doesn't it just mean: we can all play the language-game with the word “thinking”? It only creates confusion to call thinking a ‘phenomenon’; and further confusion to say: “we make a false picture of this phenomenon” (one could say “a false concept” more easily).

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-132-205.2+206.1+207.1) <span class="fac">[205\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/205.webp),[206\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/206.webp),[207\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/207.webp)</span> {#205220612071}

I believe that Bacon was not a _sharp thinker_. He had great, one might say broad, visions. But whoever has only these must be magnificent in promise, insufficient in fulfillment. One can _invent_ a flying machine, without paying close attention to its details. ### <span class="fac">[209\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/209.webp),[210\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/210.webp),[211\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-132/211.webp)</span>

But isn't there a connection between the grammatical ‘privateness’ of thoughts & the fact that, in general, we cannot guess another person’s thoughts before they express them? There is, after all, a kind of guessing of thoughts in the sense that one says to me: “I know what you are thinking now” (or “what you were thinking now”), and I have to admit that he has correctly guessed my thoughts. And this actually happens very rarely. I often sit in my classroom for several minutes without speaking, and thoughts go through my head; but none of my students could probably guess what I was thinking. But it would also be possible for them to guess and write it down, as if I had expressed it. And if he only showed the written text, I would have to say, “Yes, that is exactly what I was thinking.” – And here, for example, the question would be undecidable: whether I am not also mistaken; whether I really had that thought, or only, influenced by his writing, I just firmly _imagine_ that I had that thought. And the word “undecidable” refers to the language-game.
