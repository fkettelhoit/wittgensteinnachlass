### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-168-1r.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/1r.webp)</span> {#1r-1}

16.01.1949

The recounting of a dream, a jumble of memories. Often forming a meaningful & enigmatic whole. Almost like a fragment that _strongly_ impresses us (_sometimes_), so that we search for an explanation, for connections. But why did _now these_ memories come? Who can say? – It may be connected with our present life, and thus also with our wishes, fears, etc. – “But are you saying that this phenomenon must be in a specific causal relationship?” – I want to say that it does not necessarily have to have sense, to speak of finding its cause.

### [VB](/w-vb/#ms-168-1r.2+1v.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/1r.webp),[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/1v.webp)</span> {#1r-2--1v-1}

1.49. Shakespeare & the dream. A dream is completely incorrect, absurd, composite, & yet completely correct: it produces an _impression_ in this curious composition. Why? I do not know. And if Shakespeare is great, as is claimed, then one must be able to say of him: it is all false, _it is not right_ – & yet it is completely correct according to its own law. One could also say this: if Shakespeare is great, he can only be so in the _mass_ of his dramas, which create their _own_ language & world. He is therefore completely unrealistic. (Like the dream.)

I believe that the education of people today is geared towards reducing their capacity for suffering. In England, a school is now considered good if the children have a good time. ### <span class="fac">[2r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/2v.webp)</span>

5.48. “The treachery of the object” – an unnecessary anthropomorphism. One could speak of a treachery of the _world_; it is easy to imagine that the devil created the world, or part of it. And it is not necessary to believe in the intervention of the demon in each individual case; everything can proceed ‘according to the laws of nature’; it is simply that the whole plan was designed for the worst from the beginning. But man finds himself in this world, in which things slip, break, and instigate misfortune. And his body is, of course, one of the things. – The ‘treachery’ of the object is a silly anthropomorphism. For the truth is much more serious than this fiction.

### <span class="fac">[4r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/4v.webp)</span>

00.12.1947

It seems to me that a religious belief can only be a passionate commitment to a frame of reference. Thus, although it is _belief_, it is a way of life, or a way of judging life. A passionate embracing of _this_ perspective. And instruction in a religious belief must therefore be the representation, the description of that frame of reference, & at the same time an appeal to conscience. And these two must ultimately bring about the fact that the instructed person himself, on his own, passionately grasps that frame of reference. – It would be as if someone showed me, on the one hand, my hopeless situation, and on the other hand, the saving refuge, until I, on my own – but certainly not guided by the _instructor_ – rushed to the lifebuoy & seized it.

### <span class="fac">[4v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/4v.webp),[5r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/5r.webp)</span>

Genius is the talent in which one’s character expresses itself. Therefore, I would say, Kraus had talent, an extraordinary talent, but not genius.

Of course, there are flashes of genius, in which one then, despite the great application of talent, doesn’t notice the talent. Example: “For even oxen and asses can do things, but…”. It is remarkable that this, for example, is so much greater than anything Kraus ever wrote. It is not a mere intellectual framework here, but a whole person. This is also the reason why the greatness of what one writes is dependent on everything else that one writes and does.

### <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/5r.webp),[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/5v.webp)</span>

Schiller writes in a letter about a ‘poetic mood’. I believe I know what he means; I believe I know it myself. It is the mood in which one is receptive to nature, and thoughts appear as vividly as nature. It is remarkable, however, that Schiller did not produce anything better (or so it seems to me), and therefore I am not at all convinced that what _I_ produce in such a mood is really of any value. It is quite possible that my thoughts only receive their brilliance from a light that stands _behind_ them. That they do not shine _themselves_.

### <span class="fac">[5v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/5v.webp),[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-168/6v.webp)</span>

If it is true, as I believe, that Mahler’s music is worthless, then the question is what he, in my opinion, should have done with his talent. For it is quite clear that a number of very rare talents were involved in creating this bad music. Should he, for example, have written & burned his symphonies? Or should he have done violence to himself & not written them? Should he have written them, & realized that they were worthless? But how could he have realized that? I can see it because I can compare his music with that of the great composers. But _he_ could not; for whoever came up with the idea may well be _suspicious_ of the value of the product, because he probably sees that he does not, so to speak, have the nature of the other great composers, – but he will not realize its worthlessness because of this, for he can always tell himself that although he is _different_ from the others (whom he admires), he is valuable in a different way. – One might perhaps say: If no one you admire is like you, then you probably only believe in your own value because you are _you_. – Even someone who fights against vanity, but is not entirely successful in doing so, will always be deluded about the value of his product. But it seems most dangerous when one puts one’s work in a position where it is, first by oneself & then by others, compared with the old great works. One should not even think of such a comparison. For if the circumstances today are really so different from those in the past that one cannot compare one’s work with the earlier works in terms of _kind_, then one cannot compare its _value_ with theirs either. I myself repeatedly make the mistake that is being discussed here.
