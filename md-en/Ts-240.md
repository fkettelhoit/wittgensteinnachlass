# Ts-240

### <span class="fac">[216\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/216.webp)</span>

A picture lies in a series of turns of our language; it is constantly uttered by us; no wonder that it has great power over us.

### <span class="fac">[216\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/216.webp)</span>

Someone is singing a melody that is familiar to him; we interrupt him and then ask: “Did you know how it continues; did you want to continue it _like this_, or _like that_?” He answers: “Of course, I knew how it continues, and I naturally wanted to continue it _like this_:…”. The image is forced upon us that the continuations of the melody already exist, _namely in us_, as if behind the opening of the mouth. This image is reinforced by the fact that after the interruption, we still hear a bit of the melody with our inner ear, and it is as if we still see a piece of the series of those tones that were ready to come to light. And this is again very similar to what happens when counting, or when writing a series with ‘dots’ that mean ‘and so on, ad infinitum’.

### <span class="fac">[216\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/216.webp),[217\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/217.webp)</span>

“But there is, after all, a decisive difference between a series that is intended to have a specific end, and those beginnings of a series that are _endless_, I mean, an (essential) difference in the way in which we _conceive_ of the written series. Endless – I would like to say – is really _endless_. And here the meaning cannot consist in the use, because the use is finite, and if you look at it, you end up with finitist thoughts! But if you look at the _meaning_, at what we _think_ when we hear the word, then you see what mathematics is talking about.” – First, when you say that the use of the word is _finite_, what does that mean? What does an infinite use look like? ‒ ‒ So one can probably not apply ‘finite’ and ‘infinite’ to the use of a word. – Is the use we make of “and so on, ad inf.” the same as the use we make of “and so on, ad 700”? Apparently not. Only the difference in the use of “and so on, ad 100” and “and so on, ad 700” is not of the same _kind_ as the difference between the use of one of these signs and the sign “and so on, ad inf.”

### <span class="fac">[217\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/217.webp)</span>

As (for example) the use of the commands “draw a circular segment with a radius of 25cm”, “draw a circular segment with a radius of 6cm” is not of the same kind as that of the command: draw a circular segment with a radius of α”. In the first & second case, we use a compass; in the third, a ruler.

### <span class="fac">[217\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/217.webp),[218\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/218.webp)</span>

There is nothing more to be objected to in the use of the word “endless” or “infinite” than the _spirit_ in which they are used. The hocus-pocus that, despite all apparent sobriety, lies in the words with which mathematicians accompany their calculi. Show us, instead of the _pictures_, the gestures that this word and these sentences evoke, their non-mathematical _use_!

### <span class="fac">[218\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-240/218.webp)</span>

If one now looks at the use of the sign “a, a2, a3… ad inf.”, one notices, of course, that the peculiarity of this use cannot consist in the fact that it is, in some sense, _more extended_ than that of the other signs. It does not differ in ‘_length_’ from that of the limited series.
