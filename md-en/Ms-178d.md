### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/1.webp)</span>

By logic, one understands essentially: not an axiomatic system, because it should not be based on assumptions. Or: there should not be logical axioms & logical methods of inference.

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/1.webp)</span>

‘From its contrary, a contradiction can be derived.’ – Well, perhaps he is not doing anything here.

### <span class="fac">[1\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/1.webp),[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/2.webp)</span>

Avoiding the contradiction is a mathematical method. It leads to usable formations, & “usable” is here almost as indeterminate as “having a point.” But if the function of a sentence is completely indeterminate, why shouldn’t it be a contradiction? Why should a mathematician, in principle, make the sign of the cross against _every_ contradiction? (One could say: don’t be afraid, it doesn’t bite!)

### <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/2.webp)</span>

Logic could very well be conducted with contradictions; we would then have learned to use the sentences of logic differently. And what if, when confronted with such a (true) contradiction, one asked: is it true or is it false? –

### <span class="fac">[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-178d/5.webp)</span>

The interest in a magician’s trick can be of very different kinds. Children are delighted by the strange phenomenon that coffee can be brewed in a hat, & the hat remains undamaged. But the same phenomenon could also have a very different interest: it could be very important to us to show people how easily & in what way such a deceptive appearance comes about; this could be extremely important to us, & therefore also the trick that demonstrates this.
