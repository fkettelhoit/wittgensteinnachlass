It is important, for example, that we are used to drawing with a pen, pencil or the like, & that therefore the elements of our representation are lines & dots (in the sense of 'dot'). I only say: If you believe that these concepts are the only correct ones, the only ones worthy of highly developed people, then imagine the nature being different from what it is, & quite different concept formations will no longer seem unnatural to you.

### <span class="fac">[76\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/76.webp),[77\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/77.webp)</span>

But concepts also seem natural to us that we are used to, for which we have no ‘explanation’. Does everything we do have a purpose? & if it does not have one, is it a mistake? Does only what has a purpose satisfy us?

### <span class="fac">[77\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/77.webp),[78\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/78.webp)</span>

But I do not say: If the facts of nature were different, then we would have different concepts. This is a hypothesis. I have no use for it, & it does not interest me. I only say: If you believe that our concepts are the right ones, appropriate for intelligent people, that others would not understand what we understand, then imagine certain general facts of nature differently than they are, & other concept formations than ours will seem _natural_ to you.

### <span class="fac">[78\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/78.webp)</span>

‘Natural’, not ‘necessary’. Is everything we do purposeful? Is everything that cannot be called purposeful, also purposeless?!

### <span class="fac">[78\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/78.webp)</span>

“This wall has a very specific character.” What character? I can't say. Now, which wall would have _no_ specific character? Perhaps one that did not have any specific, understandable, familiar features; one whose sight would only confuse me; one that I could not recognize even after a long time.

### <span class="fac">[78\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/78.webp),[79\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/79.webp)</span>

Arvid once said to me as we looked at flower beds of pansies with all sorts of different color combinations: “& each has a _face_”. But what does that mean? It does not mean that each one is pleasing, but rather something like: each one can be memorized (like a face). (Each one has its own “character”, each one has its own necessity, each one says something, etc.)

### <span class="fac">[79\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/79.webp)</span>

“A face is characteristic” does not mean that it is strange, nor that it has a character that I can name or describe.

### <span class="fac">[79\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/79.webp)</span>

I have torn off a piece of the hem of the dress of many a thought that has flown past my head.

### <span class="fac">[79\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/79.webp),[80\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/80.webp)</span>

I include this face in my gallery of models. So not: “this face is one from my collection of characters,” but: “I want to incorporate this face into my collection of characters.”

### <span class="fac">[80\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/80.webp)</span>

On what occasions does one say, “This ornament says something”? – One can communicate it to another, someone who is prepared for such utterances. Someone who has an ‘understanding’ for ornaments, a feeling for, for example, where they fit, and where they do not fit, etc.

### <span class="fac">[80\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/80.webp),[81\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/81.webp)</span>

“Garden flowers and wild flowers cannot be mixed; they have quite different characters.” Couldn’t I have said instead: “Don’t mix garden flowers and wild flowers; don’t you see that they don’t go together?” I could also have said something like: Garden flowers are more lush, so to speak, fatter, than wild flowers. The latter are leaner, tougher.

### <span class="fac">[81\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/81.webp)</span>

Someone who has been led to a contradiction by his rules can say: “I have given false rules.” [But what are false rules?] They are rules the consequences of which I disavow.

### <span class="fac">[81\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/81.webp)</span>

The king says to the executioner: “Don’t hang the one who guesses correctly what we are doing with our prisoners.” The prisoner now says to the executioner: “I will be hanged.” Etc. He surprised and confused the executioner with this answer. The executioner does not know how to carry out the king’s command.

### <span class="fac">[82\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/82.webp)</span>

However, this case would be similar: The king says: “Don’t hang the one who can prove his innocence.” The thief proves that 1000 = 1 and that he therefore stole only one coin, not 1000.

### <span class="fac">[82\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/82.webp)</span>

There are people who are _intrigued_ by a mathematical proof, although they see, or believe, that the proof is completely in order.

### <span class="fac">[82\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/82.webp)</span>

The result of a philosophical investigation is sometimes a new ‘filing system’.

### <span class="fac">[82\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/82.webp),[83\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/83.webp)</span>

One compares two jugs

![](../graphics/130,82.svg)

by comparing body with body, neck with neck, handle with handle. But it may sometimes lead to a clearer understanding if one says: The body of one corresponds to the neck of the other, which has only received a lid, etc. In other words: What at first glance appears to be homologous, we must not regard as homologous if we seek a deeper understanding. And we must be able to see homologies where they do not appear to the superficial eye. (This, I think, is also a method of mathematics.)

### <span class="fac">[83\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/83.webp),[84\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/84.webp)</span>

Do we understand the sentence “’Heteronomous’ is heteronomous”? And here is the curious phenomenon that it seems to us as if we can understand the sentence in two ways.

### <span class="fac">[84\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/84.webp)</span>

We are dealing with temptations in philosophy.

### <span class="fac">[84\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/84.webp)</span>

If I define: ~ξ(ξ) = F(ξ) then I must understand that the ‘F’ does not replace ‘~ξ’: so that ~ξ(η) = F(η) would be the case. – Where is that now? Well, I can explain it as I just did, and reinforce it through practice.

### <span class="fac">[84\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/84.webp),[85\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/85.webp)</span>

If I am given the sentence “f(f)”, how do I know that the two “f” have the same meaning? If I am assured that they have the same meaning, what does that mean? It would only be possible if one shows me a language-game with this expression that I recognize as analogous to a language-game with φ(a), φ(b). Because how can I give the sentence “Green is green” the meaning that “is” is the meaning of the copula and the first and last word is the meaning of the adjective “green”? Does that mean anything?

### <span class="fac">[85\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/85.webp),[86\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/86.webp)</span>

~ξ(“ξ”) ≝ H(“ξ”) With this definition, one cannot explain the expression H(“H”). For the definition only says that H(“H”) means as much as ~H(“H”). But the definition does indeed teach the meaning of “H”, and so I may now apply the signs! – The definition teaches us an application of “H” and explains, thereby teaches, the meaning of the use of ‘H(“ξ”)’ when the meaning of ‘ξ(“ξ”)’ is known. But if we substitute “H” for “ξ”, the rule (definition) also teaches us something: namely, that we may substitute ‘~H(“H”)’ for ‘H(“H”); but the rule does not say anything more.

### <span class="fac">[86\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/86.webp)</span>

In other words: Without that _definition_, one could not have written down the paradox.

### <span class="fac">[86\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/86.webp)</span>

Imagine that someone uses “p” as an abbreviation for “~p”!

### <span class="fac">[86\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/86.webp),[87\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/87.webp),[88\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/88.webp)</span>

But is it true that the paradox could not be written down without that definition? Why shouldn’t I write: “The property of a property, not to possess itself, possesses itself & does not possess itself”? This is, of course, strange German; but it is, at a pinch, a sentence. And what should I now say: “Since the two sentences which contradict each other here are of no use, the contradiction does no harm” – or: “The fact that these two sentences follow from each other & contradict each other shows that they are of no use, and therefore senseless”? Can one say: “If this sentence were to be used in a language-game, it would lead to a contradiction”? Or like this: “If this sentence were usable, then, of course, one would have reason to be upset about the contradiction; but since it is useless anyway, the contradiction does no harm.” Or also: “Since we do not know how this sentence, or what looks like a sentence, could be verified, we need not worry about the contradiction.”

### <span class="fac">[88\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/88.webp)</span>

But what if we were now to give this structure a use? Then we would have to prevent the contradiction; & how, that would give it meaning.

### <span class="fac">[88\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/88.webp),[89\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/89.webp)</span>

The familiarity of the physiognomy of words, the feeling that they are not arbitrary signs; but rather like pictures of their meanings, that they have, as it were, absorbed their meanings into themselves – there can be a language in which all this is foreign. And how do these ‘sensations’ manifest themselves in us? In the way we choose & value words.

### <span class="fac">[89\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/89.webp),[90\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/90.webp)</span>

The familiar face of a word; the feeling that it is like a picture of its meaning; that it has, as it were, absorbed its meaning into itself – there can be a language in which all this is foreign. And how do these sensations express themselves in us? In the way we choose words & value them. [Goethe on personal names] [If-feeling]

### <span class="fac">[90\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/90.webp)</span>

The cases in which we can rightly say that we _think_ what we see as this & that are easy to characterize.

### <span class="fac">[90\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/90.webp),[91\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/91.webp),[92\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/92.webp),[93\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/93.webp)</span>

When we interpret, we put forward a conjecture, express a hypothesis that may later prove to be false. If we say “I see this figure as an F,” this can be verified or falsified no more than the sentence “I see a bright red.” Here, therefore, there is a similarity in the uses of the word “see” in the one & the other context. (Not a similarity that introspection shows us.) At different points in a book, a physics textbook, for example, we see the illustration

![](../graphics/130,91.svg)<span class="post-block-punct">.</span>

In the accompanying text, sometimes a glass cube is mentioned, sometimes a wire frame, sometimes an overturned open box, sometimes three small boards that form a spatial corner. The text interprets the illustration each time.

But we can also say that we see the illustration once as this thing, once as that thing. – How strange, then, that we can use the words of _interpretation_ to describe the immediately perceived! We would first like to answer this as follows: This description of immediate experience by means of an _interpretation_ is only an indirect description. The truth is: we can give the figure once interpretation A, once interpretation B, once interpretation C; & there are now also three direct experiences – ways of seeing the figure – A', B', C', so that A' is favorable to interpretation A, B' to interpretation B, C' to interpretation C. Therefore, we use an interpretation A as a description of the type of seeing that is favorable to it.

### <span class="fac">[93\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/93.webp)</span>

But what does it mean to say that experience A' is favorable to interpretation A? What is experience A'? How does one identify it?

### <span class="fac">[93\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/93.webp),[94\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/94.webp)</span>

Let us assume that someone makes the following discovery. He investigates the processes in the retinas of people who see the figure sometimes as a glass cube, sometimes as a wire frame, etc., & he finds that these processes are similar to those he observes when the subject looks at a glass cube, then at a wire frame, etc. Such a discovery would be inclined to be regarded as proof that we really see the figure differently each time. But on what grounds? How can the experiment tell us anything about the nature of immediate experience? – It places it in a certain class of phenomena.

### <span class="fac">[94\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/94.webp),[95\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/95.webp)</span>

How does one identify experience A'? How is it that I am even aware of this experience? How does one teach someone the expression of this experience, “I now see the figure as a wire frame”? Many have learned the word “see” & never made any such use of it. If I now show the figure to someone like that & say to him, “Now try to see it as a wire frame” – must he understand me? How, if he says, “Do you mean something other than that I should follow the text of the book, which speaks of a wire frame, by looking at the figure?” And if he does not understand me, what can I do? And if he understands me, how does that manifest itself? Not precisely by him also saying, “Yes, now I see the figure as a wire frame!”?

### <span class="fac">[96\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/96.webp),[96\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/96.webp)</span>

It is therefore the tendency to use that word expression that is a characteristic manifestation of the experience. (And a manifestation is not a symptom.)

But are there not other manifestations of this experience? Would not the following process be conceivable: I show someone a wire frame, a glass cube, a box, etc., & ask him, “Which of these things represents the figure?” He replies, “The wire frame.”

### <span class="fac">[96\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/96.webp)</span>

Should we now say that he has seen the figure as a wire frame, even though he never had the experience of seeing it sometimes as one thing, sometimes as something else?

### <span class="fac">[96\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/96.webp),[97\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/97.webp),[98\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/98.webp)</span>

Let us suppose that someone asks: Do we all see a letter F in the same way? Well, one could carry out the following experiment: We show different people a letter F & ask the question: “Which way is the F looking, to the right, or to the left?” Or we ask: “If you were to compare an F with a face in profile, where would the front be, where the back?” Some would simply not understand these questions. They are analogous to questions of the kind “What color does the sound ‘a’ have for you?” or “Does ‘a’ seem yellow or white to you?” etc. If someone did not understand these questions, if he declared that they were nonsense, could we say that he does not understand German, or the meanings of the words “color”, “sound”, etc.? On the contrary: If he has learned to understand these words, then he can react to the question ‘with understanding’ or ‘without understanding’.

### <span class="fac">[98\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/98.webp)</span>

“Do we all see a letter F in the same way?” – That does not mean anything at all, as long as it is not established how we experience ‘in what way’ someone sees it. But if I now also say, “For me, an F looks to the right & a J to the left,” can I say that whenever I see an F, it looks in this direction – or in some direction? What reason could I have to say such a thing?!

### <span class="fac">[98\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/98.webp),[99\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/99.webp)</span>

Let us suppose that the question had never been asked: “In what direction does an F look?” – but only the question: “If you were to paint an eye & a nose on an F & a J, would it look to the right or to the left?” This, too, would be a psychological question. And in it, there would be no talk of seeing it in one way or another. But there would be talk of a tendency to do one thing or another.

### <span class="fac">[99\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/99.webp),[100\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/100.webp),[101\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/101.webp)</span>

One example of the use of the term “looking in this direction” is this: one says to an architect, “Arrange the windows of the facade so that they look in this direction!” “Yes, now they are looking in this direction.” The purpose of this expression is similar to that of the expression: [The frame of a bicycle looks bad if one sees it as an approximate rhombus, or as a trapezoid with one diagonal, but good if one sees it as a triangle with two supports towards the handlebars.]

![](../graphics/130,100.svg)

The question of whether this is seeing or interpreting arises because an interpretation becomes an expression of experience. And the interpretation is not an indirect description, but its primary expression.

### <span class="fac">[101\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/101.webp),[102\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/102.webp)</span>

But why don’t we see this immediately, but instead think that there must be a more immediate expression here, and that the phenomenon is just too intangible, not quite describable, and we must in any case resort to indirect depiction in order to achieve mutual understanding with others? We say to ourselves: it is impossible that we have an experience, without adding something to the figure in our imagination, that is essentially connected to things that are quite outside the sphere of immediate perception. One could, for example, say: “You claim that you see the figure as a wire frame. Do you also know whether it is copper wire or iron wire?” And why should it then be _wire_? This shows that the word wire does not necessarily belong to the description of the experience.

### <span class="fac">[102\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/102.webp),[103\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/103.webp),[104\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/104.webp)</span>

Let us now consider this kind of explanation: When one holds one’s nose while eating, the foods lose all their taste except for sweetness, bitterness, saltiness, and sourness. So, let us say that the particular taste of bread, for example, consists of this ‘taste’ in the narrower sense, and the aroma, which is lost when we do not breathe through our nose. Why shouldn’t something similar happen when seeing something as something; for example: The _eye_ does not distinguish the figure as a wire frame from the figure as a box, etc. This is, as it were, the aroma that the brain adds to what is seen. On the other hand, the eye also distinguishes different aspects: it, as it were, phrases the visual image; and a phrasing is an interpretation that is appropriate to the other. (Empirically) Think, for example, of interpretations of musical passages. Or, for example, how the sun shines while it is still raining. Now, let us say: this interpretation forces itself upon us. (This is, after all, an experience.) And the interpretation can be explained by certain purely musical relationships. – Yes, but we do not want to explain, but to describe.

### <span class="fac">[104\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/104.webp)</span>

![](../graphics/130,104.svg)

Look at the triangle so that c is the base and C is the apex; and now so that b is the base and B is the apex. What are you doing? – Above all: – do you know what you are doing? No. “Well, perhaps it is the gaze that first rests on the ‘base’ and then goes to the ‘apex.’” But can you say that in another context, the gaze could not move in exactly the same way, without you having seen the triangle in this way? Also, try this: Look at the triangle so that it (like an arrow tip) points in direction A, and then in direction B.

### <span class="fac">[105\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/105.webp),[106\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/106.webp)</span>

Of whom does one say that he sees the triangle as an arrow pointing to the right? Of the one who has simply learned to use it as such, and has always used it in that way? No. This does not mean, of course, that one says that such a person sees it _differently_, or that we do not know how he sees it. Here, it is not yet a question of _seeing_ it one way or another. – But what about in a case where I correct the other and say: “What is there is not an arrow pointing to the right, but one that points upwards,” and then I explain the practical consequence of this interpretation to him? He says now: “I have always understood the triangle as an arrow pointing to the right.” – Is this a case of seeing? No, because it could also mean “When I have encountered this sign, I have always followed it _in this way_.” Whoever says that would not even understand the question, “But have you _seen_ it as an arrow pointing to the right?”

### <span class="fac">[106\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/106.webp)</span>

We say of the one who sees the triangle sometimes this way, sometimes that way, that he says these words with the marks of understanding, or hears them; but also of the one who says, “Now the triangle points in this direction, before it pointed in the other direction,” and who, when asked whether the triangle has changed its shape or its position, answers that it has not. And so on.

### <span class="fac">[106\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/106.webp),[107\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/107.webp)</span>

Now, how is it when I take what I see to be this body, sometimes this, sometimes that? Does this also show that here there is this kind of seeing, sometimes this way, sometimes that way? The linguistic expression and the consequences are different here than in the case of the triangle.

### <span class="fac">[107\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/107.webp)</span>

When a philosophical question feels cold and uncomfortable, remember that the right question has not yet been asked.

### <span class="fac">[107\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/107.webp),[108\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/108.webp)</span>

If I say I _consider_ a drawing, for example, to be a cube – what does that mean? – I react to it in a certain way. One considers the painted fly to be a real fly & wants to swat it away. Something that is actually convex, one considers to be concave & wants to put something into its hollow. Should one not say that the person who does this _sees_ the object as concave? – If he is then informed about it, he could say: “I would still like to reach into the hollow & put something in, but I now know that there is no hollow there.” Here, we would _certainly_ say that he sees the object as concave, but knows the opposite.

### <span class="fac">[108\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/108.webp)</span>

Let us take the case of the wheels rotating in opposite directions. First, I can see the figure as this or that movement. Second, I can also take it to be this or that.

### <span class="fac">[109\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/109.webp)</span>

He went, and this one goes? I thought they were doing the same thing!

### <span class="fac">[109\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/109.webp),[110\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/110.webp)</span>

The _somewhat odd_ phenomenon of seeing something in one way or another only appears when one realizes that the visual image remains _the same_ in _one_ sense, & something else, which one might call “perception,” can change. If I consider the image to be this or that, let’s say two wheels running against each other, then this does not yet involve a division of the impression in the visual image & perception. – So is the separation the phenomenon that interests me? Or should we ask this way: Which _reaction_ interests me? The one that shows that someone takes a bowl for a bowl (and thus also that he takes a bowl for something else)? or the one that shows that he observes a change & at the same time (yet) that nothing has changed in the visual image.

### <span class="fac">[110\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-130/110.webp)</span>

It is also possible that I say: “I always took that to be a bowl, now I see that it is not” – without being conscious of a change of ‘aspect’. I simply mean: I now see something different, now have a different visual impression. Suppose someone shows me something and asks what it is.
