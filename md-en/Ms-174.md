A proof of God should actually be something through which one can be convinced of the existence of God. But I think that the _believers_ who provided such proofs wanted to analyze & justify their ‘belief’ with their intellect, although they themselves would never have come to believe through such proofs. Perhaps one could convince someone of the ‘existence of God’ through a kind of education, by shaping one's life in a certain way. And they do not show us God like a sense impression shows an object, nor do they let us _suspect_ him. Experiences, thoughts—life can impose this concept on us.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-34v.3) <span class="fac">[34v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/34v.webp)</span> {#34v3}

I have learned a great deal & accepted it on the authority of people, & then found much of it confirmed or refuted through my own experience.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35r.1) <span class="fac">[35r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35r.webp)</span> {#35r1}

What is stated in textbooks, for example, in geography, I generally consider to be true. Why? I say: All these facts have been confirmed a hundred times. But how do I know that? What is my evidence for it? I have a world-view. Is it true or false? It is primarily the substrate of all my research & assertions. Not all the sentences that describe it are subject to the same kind of scrutiny.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35r.2+35v.1) <span class="fac">[35r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35r.webp),[35v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35v.webp)</span> {#35r235v1}

Does anyone ever check whether this table here will remain in place if no one pays attention to it? We examine the history of Napoleon, but not whether all the accounts of him are based on sensory illusions, fraud, and the like. Yes, if we examine anything at all, we already assume something that is not examined. Should I then say that the experiment I perform to test a sentence presupposes the truth of the sentence, that there really is the apparatus here that I believe I see (and so on)?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35v.2) <span class="fac">[35v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35v.webp)</span> {#35v2}

Doesn't testing have an end?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35v.3) <span class="fac">[35v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35v.webp)</span> {#35v3}

A child could say to another, “I know that the Earth is already many hundreds of years old,” & this would mean: I have learned it.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35v.4) <span class="fac">[35v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35v.webp)</span> {#35v4}

The difficulty is to recognize the groundlessness of our belief.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-35v.5+36r.1) <span class="fac">[35v\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/35v.webp),[36r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/36r.webp)</span> {#35v536r1}

That our statements of experience do not all have the same status is clear, since one can define such a sentence & make it a rule for description instead of an empirical proposition. Think of chemical investigations. Lavoisier conducts experiments with substances in his laboratory & concludes that this & that happens during combustion. He does not say that things could happen differently at some other time. He adopts a certain world-view, yes, he naturally did not invent it, but learned it as a child. I say world-view & not hypothesis because it is the self-evident basis of his research & as such is not even stated.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-36r.2) <span class="fac">[36r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/36r.webp)</span> {#36r2}

But what role does the assumption play that a substance A always reacts in the same way to a substance B under the same conditions? Or does this belong to the definition of a substance?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-36v.1) <span class="fac">[36v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/36v.webp)</span> {#36v1}

One might think that there are sentences that state that chemistry is _possible_. And these would be sentences of a natural science. For what should they be based on, if not on experience?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-36v.2) <span class="fac">[36v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/36v.webp)</span> {#36v2}

I believe what people communicate to me in a certain way. Thus, I believe geographical, chemical, & historical facts, etc. This is how I _learn_ the sciences. Yes, learning is naturally based on belief. Whoever has learned that Mont Blanc is 4000 m high, whoever has looked it up on a map, now says that he _knows_ it. And can one say: We justify our trust in this because it has proven itself?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37r.1) <span class="fac">[37r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37r.webp)</span> {#37r1}

A main reason for M. to assume that he wasn’t on the moon is that no one was on the moon & could get there; & we believe that based on what we _learn_.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37r.2) <span class="fac">[37r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37r.webp)</span> {#37r2}

Perhaps one says “There must be a principle underlying this trust”; but what can such a principle achieve? Is it more than a natural law of ‘belief-holding’?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37r.3+37v.1) <span class="fac">[37r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37r.webp),[37v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp)</span> {#37r337v1}

Is it in my power what I believe? or what I believe unshakeably? I believe that there is a chair there. Can I not be mistaken? But can I believe that I am mistaken? Yes, can I even consider it? – & _could_ I not also hold on to my belief, no matter what I learn later?! But is my belief now _justified_?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37v.2) <span class="fac">[37v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp)</span> {#37v2}

I act with _full_ certainty. But this certainty is my own.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37v.3) <span class="fac">[37v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp)</span> {#37v3}

“I know it” I say to the other; & here there is a justification. But there is none for my belief.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37v.4) <span class="fac">[37v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp)</span> {#37v4}

Instead of “I know it,” one can say in some cases “It is so; rely on it.” In some cases, however: “I learned that years ago”; & sometimes: “I am sure that it is so.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37v.5) <span class="fac">[37v\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp)</span> {#37v5}

What I know, I believe.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-37v.6+38r.1) <span class="fac">[37v\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/37v.webp),[38r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38r.webp)</span> {#37v638r1}

The false use that M. makes of the sentence “I know…” lies in the fact that he regards it as an utterance that is as little to be doubted as, for example, “I am in pain.” And since “I know that it is so” implies “It is so,” this too cannot be doubted.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38r.2) <span class="fac">[38r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38r.webp)</span> {#38r2}

It would be correct to say: “I believe…” has subjective truth; but “I know…” does not.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38r.3) <span class="fac">[38r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38r.webp)</span> {#38r3}

Or also: “I believe…” is an utterance, but “I know…” is not.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38r.4) <span class="fac">[38r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38r.webp)</span> {#38r4}

How, if M. had said “I swear…” instead of “I know…”?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38r.5+38v.1) <span class="fac">[38r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38r.webp),[38v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38v.webp)</span> {#38r538v1}

The more primitive image is that the Earth _never_ had a beginning. No child has any reason to ask how long the _Earth_ has existed, because all change takes place _on_ it. If what one calls the Earth really did come into being at some point, which is already difficult enough to imagine, one naturally assumes the beginning to be in an unimaginable time.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38v.2) <span class="fac">[38v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38v.webp)</span> {#38v2}

“It is certain that Napoleon, after the Battle of Austerlitz… Well, then it is also certain that the Earth existed at that time.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38v.3) <span class="fac">[38v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38v.webp)</span> {#38v3}

“It is certain that we did not come down from another planet to this one 100 years ago.” Well, it is as certain as such things can be.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-38v.4+39r.1) <span class="fac">[38v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/38v.webp),[39r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39r.webp)</span> {#38v439r1}

It would seem ridiculous to me to want to doubt the existence of Napoleon; but if someone doubted the existence of the Earth 150 years ago, I would perhaps be ready to listen, because now he is doubting our entire system of evidence. It does not seem to me that this system is more secure than a certainty within it.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39r.2) <span class="fac">[39r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39r.webp)</span> {#39r2}

“I could assume that Napoleon never existed and is a fable, but not that the _Earth_ did not exist 180 years ago.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39r.3) <span class="fac">[39r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39r.webp)</span> {#39r3}

“Do _you_ know that the Earth existed at that time?” – “Of course I know it. I heard it from someone who knows exactly.”

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39r.4) <span class="fac">[39r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39r.webp)</span> {#39r4}

It seems to me that whoever doubts the existence of the Earth at that time must attack the essence of all historical evidence. And I cannot say that this is certainly _true_.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39r.5+39v.1) <span class="fac">[39r\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39r.webp),[39v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39v.webp)</span> {#39r539v1}

At some point, one must move from explanation to mere description.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39v.2) <span class="fac">[39v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39v.webp)</span> {#39v2}

What we call historical evidence suggests that the Earth existed long before my birth; – the opposite hypothesis has _nothing_ to support it.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-174-39v.3) <span class="fac">[39v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-174/39v.webp)</span> {#39v3}

If now everything speaks for a hypothesis, and nothing against it, – is it then certainly true? One can describe it that way. – But does it certainly correspond to reality, to the facts? – With this question, you are already moving in a circle.
