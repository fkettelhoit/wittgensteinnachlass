### <span class="fac">[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-179/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-179/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-179/7r.webp)</span>

Lightning-fast thinking is lightning-fast experience that _represents_ the thought, like a note.

“Now I understand it,” etc., is an exclamation in a certain situation. It is justified by later actions or not.

One cannot say, “When I said it, I could, but then I had forgotten it.” The unforeseen must not simply be the inability to do something.

One could imagine the case that this apparent ability occurs again & again. In fact, this intuition is often justified.

This consideration sheds light on the grammar of the word “thinking.” It shows that the grammar is fundamentally different from that of an activity (such as, for example, speaking). Private language for private experiences.

A diary about a sensation. Signs coupled with the natural expression of the sensation. Then the diary is equally understandable to everyone. But what if there is no natural expression of the sensation? How do I know when I have the same sensation?

But I can always think for myself, without any connection with other thoughts. This gives rise to the question of how one knows that the thought one has is a thought about this or that. Is it experience?

Let’s imagine people who communicate with each other through a sign language. They also produce sounds (word-like or song-like) with which they accompany their sign language. However, these sounds do not form a system of language; they do not have the regularity of a language.
