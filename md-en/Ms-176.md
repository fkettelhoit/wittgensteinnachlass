### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-1r.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/1r.webp)</span> {#1r-1}

A language-game: Reporting on whether a certain body is brighter or darker than another. – But now there is a related one: Making statements about the relationship of the brightnesses of certain shades of color. (This can be compared to: Determining the relationship of the lengths of two rods – & determining the relationship of two numbers.) – The form of the sentences in both language-games is the same: “x is brighter than y”. But in the first it is an external relation & the sentence is temporal, in the second it is an internal relation & the sentence is timeless.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-1r.2+1v.1) <span class="fac">[1r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/1r.webp),[1v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/1v.webp)</span> {#1r-2--1v-1}

In a picture, in which a piece of white paper gets its brightness from the blue sky, this is brighter than the white paper. And yet, in another sense, blue is the darker, white the brighter color. (Goethe.) On the palette, white is the brightest color.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-1v.2) <span class="fac">[1v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/1v.webp)</span> {#1v-2}

Lichtenberg says that only a few people have ever seen pure white. So, are most people using the word incorrectly? And how did _he_ learn the correct use? – He constructed an ideal one, based on the common use. And this does not mean a better one, but one that is refined in a certain direction, in which something is taken to the extreme.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-1v.3+2r.1) <span class="fac">[1v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/1v.webp),[2r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/2r.webp)</span> {#1v-3--2r-1}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-2r.2) <span class="fac">[2r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/2r.webp)</span> {#2r-2}

If I say of a piece of paper that it is pure white, & snow is held next to the paper & this now looks gray, I would still, in its normal environment, rightly call it white, not light gray. It could be that, in the laboratory for example, I use a refined concept of white (just as I might also use a refined concept of the precise determination of time).

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-2r.3+2v.1) <span class="fac">[2r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/2r.webp),[2v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/2v.webp)</span> {#2r-3--2v-1}

What can be said in favor of the fact that green is a primary color, not a mixed color of blue & yellow? Would it be correct to say: “One can only recognize this directly by looking at the colors?” But how do I know that I mean the same thing by the words “primary color” as someone else, who is also inclined to call green a primary color? No – language-games decide here.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-2v.2+3r.1) <span class="fac">[2v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/2v.webp),[3r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/3r.webp)</span> {#2v-2--3r-1}

[There is more or less bluish (or yellowish) green &] there is the task of mixing a less yellowish (or bluish) one with a given yellowish green (or bluish green), or of selecting one from a number of color samples. However, a less yellowish one is not a bluish green (and vice versa), & there is also the task of choosing, or mixing, a green that is neither yellowish nor bluish. I say “or to mix”, because a green does not thereby become both greenish & yellowish because it is produced by a kind of mixing of yellow & blue.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-3r.2) <span class="fac">[3r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/3r.webp)</span> {#3r-2}

People could have the concept of an intermediate color or mixed color even if they never produced colors by mixing (in whatever sense). It could always be a matter of their language-games only involving the searching, choosing of already existing intermediate or mixed colors.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-3r.3+3v.1) <span class="fac">[3r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/3r.webp),[3v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/3v.webp)</span> {#3r-3--3v-1}

If now green is not an intermediate color of yellow & blue, could there not be people for whom there is a bluish yellow, a reddish green? People, therefore, whose color concepts differ from ours – since the color concepts of colorblind people also differ from those of normal people. And not every deviation from the normal must be a blindness, a defect.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-3v.2+4r.1) <span class="fac">[3v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/3v.webp),[4r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/4r.webp)</span> {#3v-2--4r-1}

Whoever has learned to find or mix a more yellowish, whitish, reddish one with a given shade, etc., whoever, therefore, knows the concept of the intermediate color, should now be asked to show us a reddish green. He may simply not understand this request & react as if he had been asked to show a regular one-sided shape, pentagon, hexagon. But what if he, without hesitation, points to a color sample (perhaps to a brownish color, as we would describe it)?

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-4r.2+4v.1) <span class="fac">[4r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/4r.webp),[4v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/4v.webp)</span> {#4r-2--4v-1}

Whoever is familiar with reddish-green should be able to create a series of colors that begins with red, ends with green, and, perhaps even for us, forms a continuous transition between them. It would then become apparent that, where we always see the same shade, for example, brown, he would sometimes see brown, sometimes reddish-green. That he could, for example, distinguish between two chemical compounds that have the same color for us according to their color, and call one brown and the other reddish-green.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-4v.2) <span class="fac">[4v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/4v.webp)</span> {#4v-2}

Imagine that almost all people, with rare exceptions, were red-green colorblind. Or also the other case: all people were either red-green or blue-yellow colorblind.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-4v.3+5r.1) <span class="fac">[4v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/4v.webp),[5r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/5r.webp)</span> {#4v-3--5r-1}

Let us imagine a _people_ of colorblind individuals, & such a group could easily exist. They would not have the same color concepts as we do. For even if we assume they speak, for example, German, & therefore have all the German color words, they would still use them differently than we do, & _learn_ to use them differently. Or if they have a foreign language, it would be difficult for us to translate their color words into ours.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-5r.2) <span class="fac">[5r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/5r.webp)</span> {#5r-2}

If, however, there were also people for whom it would be natural to use the word “reddish-green” or “yellowish-blue” in a consistent way, and who might also reveal abilities that we do not have, we would still not be compelled to acknowledge that they _see colors_ that we do not see. After all, there is no generally accepted criterion for what a color is, unless it is one of our colors.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-5v.1) <span class="fac">[5v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/5v.webp)</span> {#5v-1}

In every serious philosophical problem, uncertainty extends down to the very roots. One must always be prepared to learn something _completely_ new.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-5v.2) <span class="fac">[5v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/5v.webp)</span> {#5v-2}

The description of the phenomena of color blindness belongs to psychology: does this also apply to the description of the phenomena of normal seeing? Psychology only describes the _deviations_ of color blindness from normal seeing.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-5v.3+6r.1) <span class="fac">[5v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/5v.webp),[6r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/6r.webp)</span> {#5v-3--6r-1}

Runge (in the letter that Goethe prints in the Theory of Colors) says that there are transparent and opaque colors. White is an opaque color. This shows the indeterminacy in the concept of color, or also of color identity.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-6r.2) <span class="fac">[6r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/6r.webp)</span> {#6r-2}

Can a transparent green piece of glass have the same color as an opaque piece of paper, or not? If such a piece of glass were depicted in a painting, the colors on the palette would not be transparent. If one wanted to say that the color of the glass is also transparent in the painting, one would have to call the complex of color patches that represents the glass its _color_.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-6r.3+6v.1) <span class="fac">[6r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/6r.webp),[6v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/6v.webp)</span> {#6r-3--6v-1}

How is it that something transparent can be green, but not white? Transparency & reflection only exist in the depth dimension of a visual image. The impression of the transparent medium is that something lies _behind_ the medium. Complete uniformity of color in the visual image cannot be transparent.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-6v.2+7r.1) <span class="fac">[6v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/6v.webp),[7r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7r.webp)</span> {#6v-2--7r-1}

Something white behind a colored transparent medium appears in the color of the medium, something black appears black. According to this rule, black on a white background must be seen through a ‘white transparent’ medium as if through a colorless one.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-7r.2) <span class="fac">[7r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7r.webp)</span> {#7r-2}

Runge: “If one wants to think of a bluish orange, a reddish green, or a yellowish violet, one feels as if one were experiencing a southwestern north wind… White as well as black are both opaque or physical… One cannot imagine white water, just as one cannot imagine clear milk.”

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-7r.3+7v.1) <span class="fac">[7r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7r.webp),[7v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7v.webp)</span> {#7r-3--7v-1}

We do not want to find a theory of colors (neither a physiological nor a psychological one), but the logic of color concepts. And this achieves what one has often wrongly expected from a theory.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-7v.2) <span class="fac">[7v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7v.webp)</span> {#7v-2}

“One cannot imagine white water, etc.” That is, one cannot describe (e.g., paint) what something white and clear would look like, and that means that one does not know what description, what representation these words demand of us.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-7v.3+8r.1) <span class="fac">[7v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/7v.webp),[8r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/8r.webp)</span> {#7v-3--8r-1}

It is not immediately clear which transparent glass one should say has the _same color_ as an opaque color sample. If I say “I am looking for a glass of _this_ color” (while pointing to a colored piece of paper), then that will roughly mean that something white, seen through the glass, should look like my sample. If the sample is pink, sky blue, or purple, then one will imagine the glass as _opaque_, but perhaps also clear and only faintly reddish, bluish, or purplish.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-8r.2+8v.1) <span class="fac">[8r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/8r.webp),[8v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/8v.webp)</span> {#8r-2--8v-1}

In the cinema, one can sometimes see the events in the film as if they were behind the surface of the screen, but the screen is transparent, like a glass panel. The glass would take away the color of the things and only allow white, gray, and black to pass through.

(Here we are not speaking in a physical sense, but rather considering white and black as colors just like green and red.) – One could therefore think that we are imagining a glass that would be called white and transparent. And yet, we are not inclined to call it that: Where does the analogy with a transparent green panel, for example, break down?

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-8v.2) <span class="fac">[8v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/8v.webp)</span> {#8v-2}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-9r.3) <span class="fac">[9r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/9r.webp)</span> {#9r-3}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-9v.1) <span class="fac">[9v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/9v.webp)</span> {#9v-1}

(For it is not a psychological concomitant – that is how one imagines ‘thought’ – but the use that distinguishes the logical sentence from the empirical proposition.)

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-10v.1) <span class="fac">[10v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/10v.webp)</span> {#10v-1}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-10v.2) <span class="fac">[10v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/10v.webp)</span> {#10v-2}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-10v.3) <span class="fac">[10v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/10v.webp)</span> {#10v-3}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-10v.4) <span class="fac">[10v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/10v.webp)</span> {#10v-4}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-11r.1) <span class="fac">[11r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/11r.webp)</span> {#11r-1}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-11r.4+11v.1) <span class="fac">[11r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/11r.webp),[11v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/11v.webp)</span> {#11r-4--11v-1}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-11v.2) <span class="fac">[11v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/11v.webp)</span> {#11v-2}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-11v.3+12r.1) <span class="fac">[11v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/11v.webp),[12r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12r.webp)</span> {#11v-3--12r-1}

It could be that we have two words for “green”: one only for green surfaces, the other for green transparent objects. So the question remains: why is there no color word corresponding to the word “white” for something transparent?

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-12r.2) <span class="fac">[12r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12r.webp)</span> {#12r-2}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-12r.3) <span class="fac">[12r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12r.webp)</span> {#12r-3}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-12r.4+12v.1) <span class="fac">[12r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12r.webp),[12v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12v.webp)</span> {#12r-4--12v-1}

From two places in my surroundings, which I, in one sense, _see_ as having the same color, I can, in another sense, see one as white, the other as gray. In one context, this color is white for me in poor lighting, in another, gray in good lighting. These are sentences about the concepts ‘white’ & ‘gray’.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-12v.2) <span class="fac">[12v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12v.webp)</span> {#12v-2}

The bucket standing in front of me is painted a glossy white; it would be absurd to call it “gray” or to say “I am actually seeing a light gray.” But it has a white highlight that is much brighter than the rest of the surface, and this is partly in the light, partly in the shadow, without appearing to be of a different color. (To _appear_, not just to _be_.)

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-12v.3+13r.1) <span class="fac">[12v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/12v.webp),[13r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13r.webp)</span> {#12v-3--13r-1}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-13r.2) <span class="fac">[13r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13r.webp)</span> {#13r-2}

White as a _material color_ (in the sense in which one says, snow is white) is brighter than any other material color; black is darker. _Here_ the color is a darkening, and if this darkening is removed from the material, then white remains, and that is why one can call it “colorless.”

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-13r.3) <span class="fac">[13r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13r.webp)</span> {#13r-3}

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-13r.4+13v.1) <span class="fac">[13r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13r.webp),[13v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13v.webp)</span> {#13r-4--13v-1}

It is easy to see that not all color concepts are logically of the same kind. For example, the difference between the concepts ‘color of gold’ or ‘color of silver’ and ‘yellow’ or ‘gray’.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-13v.4+14r.1) <span class="fac">[13v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/13v.webp),[14r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/14r.webp)</span> {#13v-4--14r-1}

_“I feel x”_

_“I observe x”_

In the first & second sentence, x does not stand for the same concept, even if perhaps for the same verbal expression, e.g. for “a pain”. For if one asks “What kind of pain?” then in the first case I could answer “This one” & perhaps prick the questioner with a needle. In the second case, I must answer the same question differently; e.g. “The pain in my foot”. Also, x in the second sentence could stand for “my pain”, but not in the first.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-14v.2+15r.1) <span class="fac">[14v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/14v.webp),[15r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/15r.webp)</span> {#14v-2--15r-1}

I paint the view from my window; a particular spot, defined by its location in the architecture of a house, I paint with ochre. I say that I see this spot in this color. This does not mean that I see the color ochre here, because this pigment may, when _so_ surrounded, appear lighter, darker, redder (etc.) than ochre. “I see this spot as I have painted it here with ochre, namely as a strong reddish yellow.” But what if I were asked to specify the exact shade that I see there? – How is it to be specified, & how is it to be determined? One could demand that I produce a color sample (a rectangular piece of paper of this color). I do not say that such a comparison would be without any interest, but it shows us that it is not clear from the outset how shades are to be compared & what “sameness of color” means.

### [ROC I](/w-roc-1/#ms-176-15r.2) <span class="fac">[15r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/15r.webp)</span> {#15r-2}

It is only in connection with the others that one sees it as a piece of blue sky, as a shadow, a gleam, as transparent or opaque, etc. – Do the individual stones show us the _actual colors_ of the places in the picture?

“I know that my room is on the second floor, that behind the door there is a short corridor leading to the stairs, etc.” One could imagine cases where I would make this utterance, but they would be rather rare. 15.04.1951

“Is the impossibility of knowing what is going on in another person a physical or a logical one? And if both, – how are they related?” For the time being: one could imagine possibilities of exploring the other person that do not actually exist. So there is a physical impossibility. The logical impossibility lies in the absence of exact rules of evidence. (Therefore, we sometimes express ourselves in this way: “We can always be wrong; we can never be certain; what we observe can always still be pretense.” Although pretense is only one of many possible causes of a false judgment.) – We can imagine an arithmetic in which tasks with small numbers can be solved with certainty, but the results become increasingly uncertain as the numbers get larger. So that the people who possess this art of calculation would say that one can never be completely certain of the product of two large numbers, and no limit can be set between small and large numbers. But it is of course not true that we are never certain of the mental processes in another person. We are in countless cases. And the question remains whether we would give up our language-game, which is based on ‘unreliable evidence’ and often leads to uncertainty, if we had the possibility of exchanging it for a more exact one that would have similar consequences on the whole. We could – for example – work with a mechanical “lie detector” and redefine a lie as something that produces a reaction on the lie detector. The question is therefore: Would we change our form of life if this and that were made available to us? – And how could I answer that?

Is it enough if I don't, as now, _without further ado_, without the interruption of any doubt, call something ‘black’, ‘green’, ‘red’ – but instead say “I am certain that it is red”, as one might say “I am certain that he will come today” (i.e., with the ‘feeling of certainty’)? The accompanying feeling is, of course, irrelevant to us, and we also have no need to worry about the words “I am certain that”. – What is important is whether a difference in the _practice_ of language is associated with this. One might ask whether it occurs in all cases where we, for example, assert something with certainty (in an experiment, for example, we look into a tube and report the color we observe through it), whether it says “I am certain” on this occasion. If it does, one would first be inclined to check its statement. But if it turns out that it is quite reliable, one would explain that its way of speaking is merely an idiosyncrasy that does not affect the matter. One might, for example, assume that it has read skeptical philosophers, has been convinced that one cannot know anything, and therefore has adopted this way of speaking. Once we are used to it, it makes no difference to the practice.

But does “I know that that is a tree” say something different from “That is a tree”?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-66r.4+66v.1) <span class="fac">[66r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/66r.webp),[66v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/66v.webp)</span> {#66r-4--66v-1}

Instead of “I know what that is,” one could say “I can say what that is.” And if one were to adopt this way of expressing oneself, what would become of “I know that that is…”?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-66v.2) <span class="fac">[66v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/66v.webp)</span> {#66v-2}

Back to the question of whether “I know that that is a…” says something different from “That is a…”. – The first sentence mentions a person, the second does not. But that does not show that they have different meanings. In any case, one often replaces the first form with the second & often gives the latter a special intonation. Because one speaks differently when making an undisputed statement, & when maintaining it against a contradiction.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-67r.1+67v.1) <span class="fac">[67r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/67r.webp),[67v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/67v.webp)</span> {#67r-1--67v-1}

But doesn’t saying “I know that…” mean that I am in a certain state, whereas the mere assertion “That is a…” doesn’t say this? And yet, one often answers such an assertion with “How do you know that?” – “But only because the fact that I make this assertion shows that I believe I know it.” – One could express it like this: In a zoo, the sign could read “This is a zebra”; but not “I know that this is a zebra.” “I know” only has sense when a person utters it. _Then_, however, it is irrelevant whether the utterance is “I know…”, or “That is…”.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-67v.2) <span class="fac">[67v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/67v.webp)</span> {#67v-2}

How does one learn to recognize the state of knowledge?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-67v.3) <span class="fac">[67v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/67v.webp)</span> {#67v-3}

One could more readily speak of recognizing a state where it says “I know what that is.” One can convince oneself here that one actually possesses this knowledge.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-67v.4+68r.1) <span class="fac">[67v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/67v.webp),[68r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/68r.webp)</span> {#67v-4--68r-1}

“I know what kind of tree that is. – It is a chestnut.” “I know what kind of tree that is. I know that it is a chestnut.” The first statement sounds more natural than the second. One will only say “I know” a second time if one wants to particularly emphasize the certainty; for example, in order to anticipate a contradiction. The first “I know” means approximately: I can say. In another case, however, one could begin with the statement “That is a…”, and then, in response to a contradiction, say: “I know what kind of tree that is” and thus emphasize the certainty.

“L. W.” is my name. And if anyone disputed it, I would immediately establish countless connections that would confirm it.

“But I can imagine a person who makes all these connections, none of which correspond to reality. Why shouldn’t I be in a similar situation?” If I imagine that person, I also imagine a reality, a world that surrounds him; and him, as he thinks (& speaks) against this world.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-69r.2+69v.1) <span class="fac">[69r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/69r.webp),[69v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/69v.webp)</span> {#69r-2--69v-1}

And there is a sense to the answers Yes and No. – Of course, even this answer is not infallible, i.e., it may turn out to be wrong, but that does not take away from the question “Can you be mistaken…?” and the answer “No” their sense.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-69v.2) <span class="fac">[69v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/69v.webp)</span> {#69v-2}

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-69v.3+70r.1) <span class="fac">[69v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/69v.webp),[70r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/70r.webp)</span> {#69v-3--70r-1}

For example, if it concerns the name of the person, the case could be that the person has never used this name, but remembers having read it on a document – and on the other hand, the answer could be: “I have used this name all my life, I have been called that by all people.” If _that_ is not equivalent to the answer “I cannot be mistaken about that,” then it has no sense at all. And quite clearly, this indicates a very important difference.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-70r.2+70v.1+71r.1) <span class="fac">[70r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/70r.webp),[70v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/70v.webp),[71r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/71r.webp)</span> {#70r-2--70v-1--71r-1}

One could, for example, describe the certainty of the sentence that water boils at approximately 100˚ C. It is not, for example, a sentence that I once heard, like this or that one, which I could name. I performed the experiment myself in school. The sentence is a very elementary one in our textbooks, which can be trusted in such matters because… – One can now oppose all of this with examples that show that people have considered this and that to be certain, which later, in our opinion, has proven to be false. But this argument is worthless. To say that in the end, we can only cite such reasons that _we_ consider to be reasons, says nothing. Can it not also happen that one believes today that one has recognized an error of earlier times, and later realizes that the first view was correct? etc. I believe that there is a misunderstanding of the essence of our language-games at the bottom of this.

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-71r.2) <span class="fac">[71r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/71r.webp)</span> {#71r-2}

What reason do I have to trust the textbooks of experimental physics? I have no reason not to trust them. And I trust them. I know how such books come about – or rather, I believe I know. I have some evidence, but it is not very far-reaching and is of a very scattered nature. I have heard, seen, and read things.

22.04.1951

There is always the danger of wanting to recognize the meaning by considering the expression and the mood in which one uses it, instead of always thinking about the practice. That is why one repeats the expression to oneself so often, because it is as if one had to see in it and in the feeling that one has what one is looking for.

23.04.1951

Should I say, “I believe in physics,” or “I know that physics is true”?

### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-71v.3+72r.1) <span class="fac">[71v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/71v.webp),[72r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/72r.webp)</span> {#71v-3--72r-1}

In the end, however, I rely on these experiences, or on reports of them, and I regulate my own actions without any hesitation. – But has this trust not also proven itself? ### [OC](/w-oc/#ms-176-74v.1+75r.1) <span class="fac">[74v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/74v.webp),[75r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-176/75r.webp)</span> {#74v-1--75r-1}

“I cannot be mistaken, etc.” assigns my assertion its place in the game. But it refers essentially to _me_, not to the game in general.

If I am mistaken in my assertion, that does not deprive the language-game of its use.
