Here, the hypothesis seems to add depth to the matter. And one can recall the explanation of the strange relationship between Siegfried & Brunhild in the more recent Nibelungenlied. Namely, that Siegfried seems to have seen Brunhilde earlier. It is now clear that what gives depth to this _use_ is its connection to the burning of a person. If it were customary at some festival for people (like in the horse-and-rider game, to ride on each other), we would see nothing in it but a form of carrying, which is reminiscent of a person riding on a horse; but if we knew that it was customary among many peoples to use slaves as riding animals and to celebrate certain festivals in this way, then we would now discover something deeper and less harmless in the harmless use of our time. The question is: does this – let’s say – darkness adhere to the use of the Beltane fire as it was practiced 100 years ago (in itself), or only if the hypothesis of its origin should prove to be true? I believe that it is obviously the inner nature of the modern use itself that seems dark to us, and the known facts of human sacrifice only point to the direction in which we should look at the use. When I speak of the inner nature of the use, I mean all the circumstances in which it is practiced and which are not included in the account of such a festival, since they consist not so much in the specific actions that characterize the festival as in what one might call the spirit of the festival, which would be described by, for example, describing the type of people who participate in it, their usual way of acting, i.e., their character; the type of games they usually play. And one would then see that the darkness lies in the character of these people themselves.

### [GB II](/w-gb-2/#ms-143-13.1+14.1+15.1+16.1) <span class="fac">[13\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/13.webp),[14\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/14.webp),[15\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/15.webp),[16\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/16.webp)</span> {#131141151161}

619

Here, something looks like the remnants of a lottery. And through this aspect, it suddenly gains depth. If we were to learn that the cake with the buttons was originally baked in a certain case, for example, to honor a button maker on his birthday, and that this use then became established in the area, then this use would actually lose all its “depth,” unless it lies in its present form. But in such a case, one often says: “this use is _obviously_ very old.” How does one know that? Is it only because one has historical testimony about such old customs? Or is there another reason, one that one gains through interpretation? But even if the prehistory of the use and the origin from a dark custom is historically proven, it is still possible that the use today has _absolutely nothing_ dark about it, that nothing of the prehistoric horror has remained in it. Perhaps it is now only practiced by children who compete in baking and decorating cakes with buttons. Then the depth lies only in the thought of that origin. But this can be very uncertain, and one would like to say: “why worry about such an uncertain matter” (like a backward-looking wise Alice). But these are not the worries. – Above all: where does the certainty come from that such a use must be very old (what is our data, what is the verification)? But do we have certainty, can we not be mistaken, and be historically proven to be wrong? Certainly, but there still remains something that we are sure of. We would then say: “Okay, in this _one_ case, the origin may be different, but in general, it is certain that it is prehistoric.” What gives us the _evidence_ for this must contain the depth of this assumption. And this evidence is again a non-hypothetical psychological one. Namely, if I say: the depth in this use lies in its origin _if_ it happened that way. So either the depth lies in the thought of such an origin, or the depth is itself only hypothetical, and one can only say: _If_ it happened that way, then it was a dark, deep story. I mean: the darkness, the depth does not lie in the fact that the history of _this_ use unfolded in this way, because perhaps it did not unfold that way at all; nor in the fact that it may or probably unfolded that way, but in what gives me reason to assume that. Yes, where does the depth and darkness in human sacrifice come from? Because are they only the sufferings of the victim that give us the _impression_? Diseases of all kinds that are associated with just as much suffering do not evoke this impression. No, this depth and darkness is not understood of itself if we only learn about the history of the external action, but _we_ bring it back from an experience in our inner selves. The fact that the lot is drawn through a cake has (also) something particularly terrible (almost like the betrayal through a kiss), and that this seems particularly terrible to us, has an essential meaning for the investigation of such customs [of such a custom]. It is, if I see such a custom, hear about it, as if I see a man who, on occasion, speaks sternly to another, and from the tone of voice and the face, I notice that this man can be terrible on a given occasion. The impression I receive here can be very deep and extraordinarily serious.

### [GB II](/w-gb-2/#ms-143-16.4+17.1) <span class="fac">[16\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/16.webp),[17\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/17.webp)</span> {#164171}

### [GB II](/w-gb-2/#ms-143-17.2+18.1+19.1+20.1) <span class="fac">[17\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/17.webp),[18\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/18.webp),[19\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/19.webp),[20\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-143/20.webp)</span> {#172181191201}

One could also say: if someone wanted to impress us with the account of the Beltane festival, they would certainly not need to express the hypothesis of its origin, but would only need to present us with the material (which leads to this hypothesis) and say nothing further about it. Now one might say: “Of course, because the listener or reader will draw the conclusion himself!” But must he draw this conclusion explicitly? Or draw it at all? And what is it, this conclusion? That this or that is _probably_ the case?! And if he can draw the conclusion himself, how is he to be impressed by it? What impresses him must be what _he_ has not done! Is he therefore impressed first by the expressed hypothesis (whether expressed by him or by others), or already by the material for it? But could I not just as well ask: if I see someone being killed, am I impressed simply by what I see, or only by the hypothesis that a person is being killed here? But it is not simply the thought of the possible origin of the Beltane festival that carries the impression with it, but rather what one might call the immense probability of this thought, as taken from the material. The Beltane festival, as it has come to us, is a spectacle, and similar to when children play robbers. But not quite. For even if it is predetermined that the party that saves the victim wins, what happens still has a certain element of temperament that the mere theatrical performance does not have. – But even if it were only a completely cool performance, would we not be uncomfortably asking ourselves: what is the purpose of this performance, what is its _sense_?! And it could, apart from any interpretation, disturb us through its peculiar senselessness. (Which shows what kind of thing can be the cause of such disturbance.) If a harmless interpretation were given: the lot is simply cast so that one has the pleasure of being able to threaten someone with being thrown into the fire, which is not pleasant, then the Beltane festival would indeed become much more like one of those amusements in which one of the company has to endure certain cruelties, and which, as they are, satisfy a need. And the Beltane festival would indeed lose all its mysteriousness through such an explanation, if it did not itself deviate in the action as in the mood from such ordinary robber games, etc. Similarly, if children burned a straw man on certain days, even if no explanation were given for it, it could disturb us. Strange that _a person_ should be festively burned by them! I mean: the solution is not more disturbing than the riddle. But why should it not really be (or at least partly) the _thought_ that gives me the impression? Are not images terrible? Can I not feel a shudder at the thought that the cake with the buttons was once used to draw lots for the sacrificial victim? Does not the _thought_ have something terrible about it? – Yes, but what I see in those stories, they gain it through the evidence, also through that which does not seem to be directly connected with it, through the thought of the human being and his past, through all the strange things that I have seen and heard in myself and in others.
