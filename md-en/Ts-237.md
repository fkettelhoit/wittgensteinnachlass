“Philosophy” could also be called what is possible _before_ all new discoveries and inventions. (If someone believes he has found the solution to the ‘problem of life’ and wants to say that now everything is very easy, he only needs to remember that there was a time when the solution had not been found; but even at _that_ time one had to be able to live and think.) And so it is in logic. If there were a ‘solution’ – such as a mathematical problem – to logical, i.e. philosophical, problems, then we would only have to remember that they were once not solved (and even then one had to be able to live and think).

### <span class="fac">[85bottom\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-237/85bottom.webp),[86\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-237/86.webp)</span>

It is true: a unit of measurement is well chosen if it expresses many of the lengths that one wants to measure with it in whole numbers. But dogmatism asserts that every length _must_ be an integral multiple of our unit of measurement. 129 At the time (Log. Phil. Abh.), I said that the ‘elementary sentence’ is a concatenation of names. For the names correspond to objects, and the sentence corresponds to a complex of them. The sentence, “The bottle is to the right of the glass,” if it is true, corresponds to the complex consisting of the bottle, the glass, and the relation right-left (or however one wants to designate it). – The use of the words “object” and “complex” that is contrary to language!! A complex of houses ‘consists’ of the houses, and not of them and their mutual locations! When I say that I see three objects on the table, I do not mean: the glass, the bottle, and their spatial relation. For example, under certain circumstances, one can speak of _combinations of colors with shapes_ (for example, the colors red and blue with the shapes square and circle) just as one speaks of combinations of different shapes or bodies. And this is the root of my awkward expression: that a fact is a complex of objects. To say that a red circle ‘consists of’ redness and circularity, that it is a complex of these components, is an abuse of these words, and misleading. (Related to this: the confusion of _color_ and _pigmentum_.) The fact that this circle is red ‘consists’ of nothing. (Frege, in conversation, once objected to my expression, saying: “The part is smaller than the whole.”)

### <span class="fac">[86\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-237/86.webp),[79bottom\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-237/79bottom.webp)</span>

But I am searching, searching desperately, for a system, for a _**unity**_ of all sentences. – And now I will become a prisoner of certain forms of expression of my language, I will remain caught in the net of language. – – – If, instead of the sentence “The bottle is blue,” we say: “The bottle has the property of being blue,” instead of “The bottle is to the right of the glass”: “The bottle is to the glass in the relation of right,” and so on, it may seem as if every sentence is a combination of names. For all words with, as it were, _material_ meaning appear scattered here … One knows no way out, because language seems to offer us no way out.
