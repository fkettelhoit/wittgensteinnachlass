### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-305/1.webp)</span>

Questions such as these: “does every even number have the Goldbach property?” are nonsensical, because they have not yet been correctly formulated. Mathematical sentences refer to the form of the number; the designation with “every” disappears in the perfect notation. Fundamentally, a question can only be asked when one already knows the answer, when the bridge between question & answer has already been built. What sense does the Euclidean proof, _that there are infinitely many prime numbers_, have if the underlined sentence itself has no sense? The proof in the one notation corresponds to _a particular form_ in the other notation; the whole proof designates this form, its individual members are incomplete symbols. That sentence is the final member (the assertion), and so does not mean nothing by itself.
