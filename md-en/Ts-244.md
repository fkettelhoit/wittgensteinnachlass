# Ts-244

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-186.1+187.1) <span class="fac">[186\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/186.webp),[187\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/187.webp)</span> {#18611871}

<span class="series-number">699</span> Let us consider what one can say about a phenomenon like this: to see the figure sometimes as an F, sometimes as the mirror image of an F. I want to ask: what does it consist in, to see the figure one way and then another? – Do I really see something different each time; or do I only _interpret_ what I see in different ways? – I am inclined to say the former. _But why?_ Well, interpreting is an action. It can consist, for example, in someone saying “This is supposed to be an F”; or in not saying it, but replacing the sign with an F when copying; or in thinking: “What could it be? It will be an F that the writer botched.” – Seeing is not an action, but a state. (A grammatical remark.) And if I never thought of the figure as anything other than an F, never wondered what it might be, then one will say that I _see_ it as an F; namely, because one knows that it can also be seen differently. How did one come up with the concept of ‘seeing this as that’ in the first place? In what situations does it manifest itself, is it a necessity? (Very often, when we talk about a work of art.) There, for example, where it is a matter of phrasing through the eye or the ear. We say “You have to hear these bars as an introduction,” “You have to listen for this key,” but also “I hear the French ‘ne…pas’ as a two-part negation, not as ‘not a step’,” etc. Is it really seeing or hearing? Well: that is what we call it; with these words we react in certain situations. And we react to these words again with certain actions.

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-187.2) <span class="fac">[187\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/187.webp)</span> {#1872}

<span class="series-number">670</span> Is introspection what teaches me whether I am dealing with genuine seeing or with interpretation? First of all, I must be clear about what I would call interpretation; what shows that something is an interpretation or a seeing. (Seeing in accordance with an _interpretation_.)

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-187.3) <span class="fac">[187\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/187.webp)</span> {#1873}

<span class="series-number">671</span> I would like to say: “I see the figure as the mirror image of an F” is only an indirect description of my experience. There is a direct one; namely: I see the figure _like this_ (where I interpret my visual impression for myself). Where does this temptation come from? – There is an important fact here, namely, that we are willing to accept a number of different descriptions of our visual impression; for example: “The figure _looks_ to the right now, to the left now.”

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-187.4) <span class="fac">[187\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/187.webp)</span> {#1874}

<span class="series-number">672</span> Suppose we ask someone: What is the similarity between this figure and an F? Now one answers “The figure is an inverted F,” another “It is an F with strokes that are too long.” Should we say “The two see the figure differently”?

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-187.5+188.1) <span class="fac">[187\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/187.webp),[188\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/188.webp)</span> {#18751881}

<span class="series-number">673</span> Do I not see the figure one way and then another, even if I do not react with words or through other signs? But “one way,” “another way” are words, and what right do I have to use them here? Can I prove my right to you, or to myself? (Unless through another reaction.) But I do know that there are two impressions, even if I don’t say it! But how do I know that what I then say is what I knew?

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-188.2) <span class="fac">[188\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/188.webp)</span> {#1882}

<span class="series-number">674</span> The familiar appearance of a word; the feeling that it is as if a picture of its meaning; it has as if absorbed its meaning within itself – there can be a language for which all of this is foreign. And how are these feelings expressed in us? In how we choose and value words.

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-188.3) <span class="fac">[188\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/188.webp)</span> {#1883}

<span class="series-number">675</span> The cases in which we can rightly say that we _interpret_ what we see as this or that are _easily_ characterized.

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-188.4+189.1) <span class="fac">[188\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/188.webp),[189\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/189.webp)</span> {#18841891}

<span class="series-number">676</span> When we interpret, we make an assumption, express a hypothesis that may subsequently prove to be false. If we say “I see this figure as an F,” this can be verified or falsified no more than the sentence “I see a bright red.” Thus, there is a similarity in the uses of the word “see” in these two contexts. (Not a similarity that introspection reveals.)

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-189.2) <span class="fac">[189\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/189.webp)</span> {#1892}

<span class="series-number">677</span> At various points in a book, for example, a physics textbook, we see the illustration. In the accompanying text, it speaks once of a glass cube, once of a wire frame, once of an inverted open box, once of three small boards that form a spatial corner. The text interprets the illustration each time. But we can also say that we _see_ the illustration once as this thing, once as that thing. – How remarkable that we can also use the words of the _interpretation_ to describe what is immediately perceived! We would first like to answer like this: That description of the immediate experience by means of an _interpretation_ is only an indirect description. The truth is: we can give the figure the interpretation A, the interpretation B, the interpretation C; and there are now also three direct experiences – ways of seeing the figure – A', B', C', so that A' is favorable to the interpretation A, B' to the interpretation B, C' to the interpretation C. Therefore, we use the interpretation A as a description of the way of seeing that is favorable to it.

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-189.3) <span class="fac">[189\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/189.webp)</span> {#1893}

<span class="series-number">678</span> But what does it mean to say that experience A' is favorable to interpretation A? What is experience A'? How does one identify it?

### [RPP I](/w-rpp-1/#ts-244-189.4) <span class="fac">[189\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/189.webp)</span> {#1894}

<span class="series-number">679</span> Let us assume that someone makes the following discovery. He investigates the processes in the retinas of people who see the figure once as a glass cube, once as a wire frame, etc., and he finds that these processes are similar to those he observes when the subject looks at a glass cube, a wire frame, etc.

### <span class="fac">[190\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/190.webp)</span>

One would be inclined to regard such a discovery as proof that we really see the figure differently each time. But on what grounds? How can the experiment say anything about the nature of immediate experience? – It places it in a certain class of phenomena.

### <span class="fac">[190\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/190.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">680</span> How does one identify experience A'? How is it that I even know about this experience? How does one teach someone the expression of this experience “I now see the figure as a wire frame”? Many have learned the word “see” and have never made such a use of it. If I now show this figure to someone and say, “Now try to _see_ it as a wire frame!” – must he understand me? What if he says, “Do you mean something other than that I should, following the text of the book, which speaks of a wire frame, look at the figure?” And if he does not understand me, what can I do? And if he understands me, how does this manifest itself? Not precisely by him saying that he now sees the figure as a wire frame?

### <span class="fac">[190\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/190.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">681</span> It is therefore the tendency to use that word expression, a characteristic manifestation of the experience. (And a manifestation is not a symptom.)

### <span class="fac">[190\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/190.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">682</span> Are there other manifestations of this experience? Wouldn’t the following process be conceivable: I present someone with a wire frame, a glass cube, a box, etc., and ask him, “Which of these things does the figure represent?” He replies, “The wire frame.”

### <span class="fac">[190\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/190.webp),[191\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/191.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">683</span> Should we then say that he saw the figure as a wire frame, – even though he did not have the experience of seeing it once as this and once as something else?

### <span class="fac">[191\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/191.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">684</span> Let us think: someone asks, “Do we all see a printed ‘F’ in the same way?” Well, one could try the following experiment: We show different people an F and ask the question, “Which way is the F facing, to the right or to the left?” Or we ask: “If you were to compare an F with a profile view of a face, where would the front be, and where the back?” But some might not understand these questions. They are analogous to questions of the kind: “What color does the sound ‘a’ have for you?” or “Does ‘a’ appear yellow or white to you?” etc. If someone did not understand these questions, if he declared that they are nonsense, could we say that he does not understand German, or the meanings of the words “color,” “sound,” etc.? On the contrary: if he has learned to understand these words, then he can react to those questions ‘with understanding’ or ‘without understanding.’

### <span class="fac">[191\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/191.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">685</span> “Do we all see an F in the same way?” – This does not yet mean anything, as long as it has not been established how we experience ‘in what way’ one sees it. But if I now also say, for example, “For me, an F faces to the right and a J faces to the left,” – may I say: whenever I see an F, does it face in this, or in some direction? What reason would I have to say such a thing?!

### <span class="fac">[191\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/191.webp),[192\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/192.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">686</span> Let us suppose that the question had never been posed: “In which direction is a ‘F’ facing?” – but only this: “If you were to draw an eye & a nose on a ‘F’ & a ‘J’, would it look to the right or to the left?” This would also be a psychological question. & in it, there would be no talk of a ‘seeing’ in one way or another, but rather of a _tendency_ to do one thing or the other.

### <span class="fac">[192\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/192.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">687</span> One instance of the use of the expression ‘looking in this direction’ is, for example, when one says to an architect, “With this arrangement of the windows, the façade looks _that_ way.” Similarly, one uses the expression: “This arm interrupts the _movement_ of the sculpture” or “The movement should proceed _in this way_” (while perhaps making a gesture).

### <span class="fac">[192\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/192.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">688</span> The question of whether it is a case of seeing or interpreting arises from the fact that an interpretation becomes an expression of _experience_. And the interpretation is not an indirect description, but rather its _primary_ expression.

### <span class="fac">[192\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/192.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">689</span> But why don't we see this immediately, but rather think that there must be a direct expression here, and that the phenomenon is just too elusive, not quite describable, and that we must in any case resort to indirect representation in order to achieve communication with others? We tell ourselves: It is impossible for us to have an experience that is essentially connected with things that lie entirely outside the sphere of immediate perception, without adding something to the figure in our imagination. One could, for example, say: “You claim that you see the figure as a wire frame. Do you perhaps also know whether it is made of copper wire or iron wire? And why should it then be _wire_? – This shows that the word “wire” does not necessarily have to be part of the description of the experience.

### <span class="fac">[192\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/192.webp),[193\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/193.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">690</span> Let us now consider this kind of explanation: when one holds one’s nose while eating, the foods lose all flavor except for sweetness, bitterness, saltiness, and acidity. So, let us say that the particular ‘taste’ of bread, for example, consists of this ‘taste’ in the narrower sense and the aroma, which is precisely what is lost when we do not breathe through our nose. Why should it not be similar when seeing something as something? For example: the _eye_ does not distinguish the figure as a wireframe from the figure as a box, etc. That is, in a sense, the aroma that the brain adds to what is seen. On the other hand, the eye also distinguishes different aspects: it phrases, as it were, the visual image; and _one_ phrasing is _one_ _interpretation_, the other more appropriate to the other. (_Empirically_ more appropriate.) Think, for example, of certain _involuntary_ interpretations that we give to one or another part of a piece of music. We say: this interpretation forces itself upon us. (This is, after all, an experience.) And the interpretation can be explained by certain purely musical _relationships_. – But we do not want to explain, but rather describe.

### <span class="fac">[193\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/193.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">691</span> Look at the triangle so that o is the base and C is the apex; and now look at it so that o is the base and B is the apex. – What are you doing? – Above all: – Do you know what you are doing? No. “Well, perhaps it is the gaze that first fixes on the ‘base’ and then goes to the ‘apex’.” But can you say that in another context the gaze could not wander in exactly the same way, without you having seen the triangle in this way? Try this as well. Look at the triangle so that it (like an arrow tip) points in the direction A, and then in the direction B.

### <span class="fac">[193\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/193.webp),[194\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/194.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">692</span> Who do we say sees the triangle as an arrow pointing to the right? The one who has simply learned to use it as such an arrow & has always used it that way? No. That certainly doesn't mean that we say that he sees it _differently_, or that we don't know how he sees it. Here, it is not yet a question of seeing it one way or another. – But how is it in a case where I correct the other person & say, “What is depicted there is not an arrow pointing to the right, but one pointing upwards,” & then I explain to him the practical consequence of this interpretation. He says, “I have always understood the triangle as an arrow pointing to the right.” – Is this a case of seeing? No; because it could mean “Whenever I have encountered this sign, I have always followed it _in this way_.” Whoever says this wouldn't necessarily understand the question “But did you _see_ it as an arrow pointing to the right?”

### <span class="fac">[194\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/194.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">693</span> We say of _the one_ who sees the triangle now one way, now another, that he says this of himself, that he utters these words with the sign of understanding, or hears them; but also of the one who says, for example, “Now the triangle points in this direction, earlier it pointed in the other direction,” and who, when asked whether the triangle has changed its shape or position, answers: it was not so. And so on.

### <span class="fac">[194\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/194.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">694</span> Let us consider the case of the picture of two wheels rotating in opposite directions. First, I can see the movement in the picture as either _one_ or the _other_. Second, I can also take it to be one or the other.

### <span class="fac">[194\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/194.webp),[195\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/195.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">695</span> The _somewhat curious_ phenomenon of seeing something one way or another only arises when one recognizes that the visual image, in _one_ sense, _remains the same_, and something else, which one might call “perception,” can change. If I consider the image to be this or that, let’s say two wheels moving against each other, then this does not yet involve dividing the impression into visual image and perception. – Should I then say that the separation is the phenomenon that interests me? Or should we ask: Which _reaction_ interests me? The one that shows that one takes one bowl for another (and thus also that one takes a bowl for something else)? Or the one that shows that one observes a change and at the same time that nothing has changed in the visual image?

### <span class="fac">[195\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/195.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">696</span> It is also possible that I say: “I always took it to be a bowl; now I see that it is not” – without being conscious of any change in the ‘aspect’. I simply mean: I now see something different, I now have a different visual impression. Suppose someone shows me something and asks what it is. I say, “It is a cube.” To which he replies: “So, that is how you see it.” – Should I understand these words differently than: “So, that is what you take it to be”?

### <span class="fac">[195\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/195.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">697</span> When I look at the objects around me, I am not conscious of the fact that there is such a thing as visual perception.

### <span class="fac">[195\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/195.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">698</span> “I see this figure as a spatial corner”: why don’t you simply take it as true – if he speaks German and is credible? – I have no doubt that it is true. But what he said is a temporal sentence. Not one about the essence of this phenomenon; but one that says: this has happened.

### <span class="fac">[196\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/196.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">699</span> The expression of the experience is: “I now see it as a pyramid; now as a square with its diagonals.” – What is ‘it,’ which I see one way and then another? Is it the drawing? And how do I know that it is the same drawing both times? Do I only know it, or do I also see it? – What if it were shown that the drawing had always changed a little when it is seen as something else; or that the visual image was then a little different. For example, a line might then appear a little thicker or thinner than before.

### <span class="fac">[196\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/196.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">700</span> Should I say that the different aspects of the figure are associations? And what would that help me?

### <span class="fac">[196\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-244/196.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">701</span> It seems as if something changes in the visual image of the figure; and yet nothing changes. And I cannot say, “A new interpretation always occurs to me.” Yes, it probably is, but it is also immediately embodied in what is seen. A new aspect of the drawing always occurs to me – which I see as remaining the same. It is as if it is always being given a new dress, and yet every dress is always the same as the other. One could also say: “I not only interpret the figure, but I also put the interpretation on it.”
