### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#31v.2+32r.1) <span class="fac">[31v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/31v.webp),[32r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-31v-2--32r-1}

“If someone is truly happy, you can tell.” But one cannot describe the genuine expression. – It is, of course, not _always_ the case that one recognizes the genuine expression, or knows whether the expression is genuine. Yes, there are cases where one does not speak of genuine or artificial. Someone smiles & their other reactions do not correspond to either genuine or feigned joy. We might say, “I don’t understand them.” It is neither the picture (pattern) of genuine joy, nor that of feigned joy. Could they not behave like a colorblind person to a normal person with normal vision, with respect to being a normal, feeling human being?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#32r.2) <span class="fac">[32r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-32r-2}

Based on my knowledge of his character, I could reliably state that he will react in such and such a way in this situation, and it would also be possible for others to rely on my judgment, without them being able to demand that I justify my judgment with a verifiable description.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#32r.3+32v.1) <span class="fac">[32r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32r.webp),[32v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-32r-3--32v-1}

A painter would have depicted the expression of tender joy – and I see the picture and say, “Perhaps she is feigning it.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#32v.2) <span class="fac">[32v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-32v-2}

It is at least conceivable that in a country, the court relies on a person’s statement regarding their capabilities, if the witness has known them for some time. Today, for example, one also asks a psychiatrist whether a particular person is capable of suicide. It is assumed that _experience_ does not generally contradict such a statement.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#32v.3+33r.1) <span class="fac">[32v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/32v.webp),[33r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-32v-3--33r-1}

I am trying to describe the laws or rules of evidence for experiential sentences: Does one really characterize what is meant by the mental in doing so?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#33r.2+33v.1) <span class="fac">[33r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33r.webp),[33v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-33r-2--33v-1}

The characteristic of the mental seems to be that one must guess it in the other from external signs and only _know_ it from oneself. But if, through closer consideration, this view dissolves, it does not mean that the inner becomes the outer, but rather that “outer” and “inner” are no longer regarded as properties of evidence. “Inner evidence” means nothing, and therefore neither does “outer evidence.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#33v.2) <span class="fac">[33v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-33v-2}

However, there is ‘evidence for the inner’ and ‘evidence for the outer.’


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#33v.3) <span class="fac">[33v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-33v-3}

“I only ever perceive their _outer_ side.” If that makes sense, it must define a concept. But why shouldn’t I say that I perceive their doubts? (_They_ cannot perceive them.)


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#33v.4) <span class="fac">[33v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-33v-4}

Yes, I can often describe their inner state as I perceive it, but not their outer state.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#33v.5+34r.1) <span class="fac">[33v\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/33v.webp),[34r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-33v-5--34r-1}

The connection between inner and outer belongs to these concepts. We do not make this connection in order to conjure away the inner. There are inner _concepts_ and outer _concepts_.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#34r.2) <span class="fac">[34r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-34r-2}

What I want to say is that the inner differs from the outer through its _logic_. And that, indeed, logic explains the expression “the inner,” making it comprehensible.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#34r.3+34v.1) <span class="fac">[34r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34r.webp),[34v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-34r-3--34v-1}

We do not need the concept of “mental” (etc.) to justify the fact that some of our inferences are indeterminate, etc. Rather, this indeterminacy, etc., explains to us the use of the word “mental.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#34v.2) <span class="fac">[34v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-34v-2}

“Of course, I only see the outer, actually.” But am I not really only talking about the outer? I say, for example, under what circumstances people say this or that. And I always mean _outer_ circumstances. So, it is _as if_ I wanted to explain (quasi define) the inner through the outer. And yet it is not so.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#34v.3) <span class="fac">[34v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-34v-3}

Is this because the language-game is something outer?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#34v.4) <span class="fac">[34v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/34v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-34v-4}

_No_ evidence teaches us the psychological expression.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#35r.1) <span class="fac">[35r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/35r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-35r-1}

“mental” is not a metaphysical, but a logical epithet for me.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36r.2) <span class="fac">[36r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36r-2}

“I see the exterior & imagine an interior to it.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36r.3) <span class="fac">[36r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36r-3}

When mien, gesture, and circumstances are clear, then the interior seems to be the exterior; only when we cannot read the exterior does an interior seem to be hidden behind it.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36r.4+36v.1) <span class="fac">[36r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36r.webp),[36v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36r-4--36v-1}

There are inner and outer concepts, inner and outer ways of considering a person. Yes, there are also inner and outer facts – as there are, for example, physical and mathematical facts. But they do not stand side by side like plants of different kinds. For what I have said sounds as if it were saying: All these facts occur in nature. And what is wrong with that?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36v.2) <span class="fac">[36v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36v-2}

The inner is not only connected with the outer in experience, but also logically.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36v.3) <span class="fac">[36v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36v-3}

The inner is logically connected with the outer, not just empirically.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#36v.4+37r.1) <span class="fac">[36v\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/36v.webp),[37r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/37r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-36v-4--37r-1}

“If I investigate the laws of evidence for the mental, then I investigate the _essence_ of the mental.” Is that true?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#37r.2) <span class="fac">[37r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/37r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-37r-2}

Yes. The _essence_ is not something that can be pointed out; it can only be described in its features.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#37r.3+37v.1) <span class="fac">[37r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/37r.webp),[37v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/37v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-37r-3--37v-1}

But doesn’t this contradict a certain prejudice? Of course, we can enumerate the _properties_ of the inkwell one by one, but must not its _essence_ be fixed once & for all? Is it not given to us together with the object that we have before us? What we have before us is not, after all, the ‘use of a word’! Certainly not; but the concept ‘inkwell’, which is nevertheless necessary here, does not stand tangibly before us, nor does what is before us contain this concept within itself. & to represent it, it is not enough to simply give someone an inkwell. And this is not because people are too inclined to extract the concept from the object.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#37v.2+38r.1) <span class="fac">[37v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/37v.webp),[38r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/38r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-37v-2--38r-1}

I can show someone an object because its _color_ strikes one, & I want to demonstrate _it_ to the other person, but this already presupposes a certain game between us.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#38r.2) <span class="fac">[38r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/38r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-38r-2}

Yes, he may be amazed when he sees the object, but that he is ‘amazed at the color,’ that the color is the _basis_ of his amazement, & not merely the _cause_ of his experience, this requires him to have the concept of color, not just seeing.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#38r.3) <span class="fac">[38r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/38r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-38r-3}

Someone states on his word of honor that the other person believed this and that. – One can ask him, “How do you know that?” & he can answer, “He assured me of it in all seriousness, & I know him well.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#38v.1) <span class="fac">[38v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/38v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-38v-1}

If I say “I don’t know how to deal with him,” that bears very little similarity to the case: “I don’t know how this mechanism works.” I believe it means _approximately_: I cannot predict his behaviour with the same certainty as I can with people ‘with whom I am familiar’.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#38v.2+39r.1) <span class="fac">[38v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/38v.webp),[39r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-38v-2--39r-1}

The question of the evidence for what one has experienced must be connected with the certainty or uncertainty of a prediction of the other’s behaviour. But it is not quite like that, because one rarely predicts the other’s reaction. I mean that unpredictability must be _one_ essential property of the mental. Just as the infinite diversity of expression is.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#39r.2) <span class="fac">[39r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-39r-2}

What, for example, speaks in favor of, and what speaks against, the idea that a dog has a mental life? Surely, it is not its shape, color, or its anatomy. Therefore, it is its behaviour.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#39r.3+39v.1) <span class="fac">[39r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39r.webp),[39v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-39r-3--39v-1}

Those who say that a dog has no mind base their assertion on what it can do, & what it cannot do. For if one were to say that a dog cannot hope, from what would one infer this? And whoever says that a dog _has_ a mind can only base this on the behaviour that one observes in the dog. “Just look at the dog’s face & its movements, & you will see that it has a mind.” But what is it about the face? Is it merely the similarity to the facial expressions of a human? Is it, at least among other things, the lack of stiffness?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#39v.2) <span class="fac">[39v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-39v-2}

The _important_ subtle nuances of behaviour are not predictable.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#39v.3+40r.1) <span class="fac">[39v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/39v.webp),[40r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-39v-3--40r-1}

But does that mean: If they were predictable, would we not speak of an inner self in humans as opposed to the outer self? – But should we also clearly imagine such predictability? Does it, for example, imply that we would not ask him to make a decision?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#40r.2) <span class="fac">[40r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-40r-2}

Imagine we encountered a person who has no mind. Why shouldn’t something like that occur as an abnormality? So, a human body would have come into the world with certain life functions, but without a mind. Now, what would that look like?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#40r.3+40v.1) <span class="fac">[40r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40r.webp),[40v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-40r-3--40v-1}

The _only_ thing I can imagine is that this human body acts automatically & not like ordinary human bodies.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#40v.2) <span class="fac">[40v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-40v-2}

If it is said “Man consists of a body & a mind,” then this would not be contradicted by such a phenomenon. For this would not be a (real) man, but something else, & indeed, something very rare. But how can one know that it _never_ occurs? Only – what would this phenomenon actually look like?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#40v.3) <span class="fac">[40v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/40v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-40v-3}

Or should it not be a phenomenon at all? Should mentality not be recognizable?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41r.1) <span class="fac">[41r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41r-1}

Can there be heartlessness that has no expression? Would that be what we call “heartlessness”?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41r.2) <span class="fac">[41r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41r-2}

One could also say: How would a human body have to act, so that one would not be inclined to speak of inner and outer states of the person? Again and again, I think: “machine-like.”


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41r.3) <span class="fac">[41r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41r-3}

The most finely articulated behavior of a human is perhaps language with its tone and facial expressions.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41r.4+41v.1) <span class="fac">[41r\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41r.webp),[41v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41r-4--41v-1}

Could a being without a mind exhibit signs of pain? If it merely screamed & writhed, one could still regard that as an automatic reaction, but if it contorted its face in a painful way & looked as if it were suffering, then we would probably feel as if we were seeing _into it_. But if it were now to always make exactly the same suffering face?


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41v.2) <span class="fac">[41v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41v-2}

It is as if he were made transparent to us through a human _expression_.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#41v.3+42r.1) <span class="fac">[41v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/41v.webp),[42r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-41v-3--42r-1}

Whoever has a mind must be capable of pain, joy, sorrow, etc., etc. And if he is also capable of remembering, making decisions, and planning something, then he needs verbal expression.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#42r.2) <span class="fac">[42r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-42r-2}

It is not as if I have direct evidence for my own mind, but he only has indirect evidence for his mind. Rather, he has evidence for it, but I do not.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#42r.3) <span class="fac">[42r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-42r-3}

If one says that this evidence only makes the mental probable, then that is ambiguous and can mean both true and false things. But if it is true, then not because the evidence is empirically related to the mental (like a symptom to a disease).


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#42v.1) <span class="fac">[42v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-42v-1}

Why shouldn’t one say, “The evidence of the mental in the other is the external”? Only there is not external and internal evidence for the internal.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#42v.2) <span class="fac">[42v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-42v-2}

And the evidence, insofar as it is uncertain, is not so because it is only external evidence.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#42v.3+43r.1) <span class="fac">[42v\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/42v.webp),[43r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/43r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-42v-3--43r-1}

That the actor can depict sorrow shows the uncertainty of the evidence, but that he can depict the _sorrow_ also shows the reality of the evidence.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#43r.2) <span class="fac">[43r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/43r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-43r-2}

It is not the relationship of inner to outer that explains the uncertainty of the evidence, but rather this relationship is used as an image for that uncertainty.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#43r.3) <span class="fac">[43r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/43r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-43r-3}

One can not only portray the mental on the stage; a wound or a mountain is also simulated. So, it is not solely characteristic of the mental that it can be portrayed.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#43v.2+44r.1) <span class="fac">[43v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/43v.webp),[44r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/44r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-43v-2--44r-1}

Why do we say, “I didn’t know what was going on behind that forehead,” even though it may be quite irrelevant to us what is going on behind a person’s forehead. Our uncertainty does not relate to processes in the inner world; if it does relate to the mental, then the mental has its expression in the physical. An uncertainty concerning the inner corresponds to an uncertainty about the external. As an uncertainty about the result of a calculation corresponds to an uncertainty about the sign that will be at the end of the calculation.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#44r.2) <span class="fac">[44r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/44r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-44r-2}

And that does _not_ mean that, in general, the uncertainty about something mental can be expressed as uncertainty about something external. Just as grammar essentially has an expression in facial expressions, I may not be able to describe a facial expression in any other way than through the word “sad”.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#44r.3+44v.1) <span class="fac">[44r\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/44r.webp),[44v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/44v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-44r-3--44v-1}

Could one testify in court: “I know that at the time, he was thinking about …”? Well, such a statement might be admissible, or it might not. Perhaps it would be judged that someone who has known the defendant for so many years can infer from his facial expression, etc., what he is thinking in a particular case. Perhaps, however, such a statement would not be admissible in any case, & the opinion would be that even no _utterance_ of the defendant may be reproduced if this is only to describe his mental processes.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#44v.2+45r.1) <span class="fac">[44v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/44v.webp),[45r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/45r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-44v-2--45r-1}

“I don’t know these people.” And why would I want to know them? – Isn’t it their reactions that I don’t know? The ones that I can’t, for example, predict; the ones that always surprise me? “He seems to be reacting illogically.” And that means: inconsistently.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#45r.2+45v.1) <span class="fac">[45r\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/45r.webp),[45v\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/45v.webp)</span> {#ms-173-45r-2--45v-1}

If one is not familiar with certain things, it means that one is familiar with others. And this is sometimes expressed by saying that one ‘can imagine’ what is going on in the other person. So it sounds as if knowing what is going on in the other person is an _imagining_ of this process. If, for example, I know that someone hates me, I feel a kind of copy of this hatred. Here, everything is wrong. One uses the words “to imagine the hatred (etc.) of another,” yes, and mental images may also play a role in this, or one may perhaps make a face that is similar to that of the person filled with hatred.


### [Ms-173](/ms-173/#45v.2+46r.1) <span class="fac">[45v\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/45v.webp),[46r\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ms-173/46r.webp)</span> {#ms-173-45v-2--46r-1}

The language-game is designed from the outset in such a way that a comparison with other language-games can lead to the picture of ‘outside-inside’. But in addition to this, there is also the actual uncertainty that attaches to guessing the other person’s mental processes.
