### <span class="fac">[1\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">1.</span> “But how can a rule teach me what I have to do at _this_ point? – Whatever I do, it must be brought into agreement with the rule through some interpretation.” – No, that is not how it should be put; rather _so_: Every interpretation hangs, together with what is being interpreted, in the air; it cannot serve as a support for it. Interpretations alone do not determine the meaning.

### <span class="fac">[1\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">2.</span> “So, is whatever I do compatible with the rule?” – Let me put it this way: What does the expression of the rule – let’s say, the signpost – have to do with my actions? What connection exists between them? – Something like this: I have been trained to react in a certain way to this sign, & now I react that way.

### <span class="fac">[1\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">3.</span> But now you have only indicated a causal connection. You have only explained how it came about that we now follow the signpost; not what this “following the sign” actually consists in. No; I have done more. I have suggested that people only follow signposts when they constantly follow them.

### <span class="fac">[1\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">4.</span> Is what we call “following a rule” something that only _one_ person, and only once in their life, could do? – That is, of course, a remark about the _grammar_ of the expression ‘following the rule’.

### <span class="fac">[1\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">5.</span> It could not have been that someone followed a signpost _only once_. It could not have been that _only once_ a communication was made; an order given; or understood. – Following a rule, making a communication, giving an order, or understanding, playing a game of chess, are _customs_. (Practices, institutions.)

### <span class="fac">[1\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/1.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">6.</span> To understand a sentence means to understand a language. To understand a language means to _master_ a technique.

### <span class="fac">[2\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/2.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">7.</span> It is, of course, conceivable that in a people that does not know games, two people sit down at a chessboard & perform the moves of a game of chess; yes, even with all the accompanying mental states. And if _we_ saw this, we would say that they were playing chess. But now imagine that a game of chess, according to certain _rules_, is translated into a series of actions that we are not accustomed to associate with a _game_; for example, uttering cries & stamping with the feet. And suppose these two people, instead of playing the familiar form of chess, shout & stamp; & that this could be translated into a game of chess according to appropriate _rules_. Would we still be inclined to say that they were playing a game; & on what grounds could one say this?

### <span class="fac">[2\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/2.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">8.</span> As things stand, I can invent a game that is never played by anyone. – But would the following also be possible: that humanity has never played games; but once, someone invented a game that was then, admittedly, never played?

### <span class="fac">[2\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/2.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">9.</span> “This is what is so _remarkable_ about the _intention_, about the mental process, that it does not require the existence of custom or technique. It is conceivable, for example, that two people, in a world where no one ever plays, begin a game of chess, and then are interrupted.” But isn’t the game of chess defined by its rules? And how are these rules present in the _mind_ of the one who intends to play chess?

### <span class="fac">[2\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/2.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">10.</span> Following a rule is analogous to obeying an order. One is trained to do so, & now one reacts to it in a certain way. But what if one person reacts _so_, & another _differently_ to the order & training? Who is right then?

### <span class="fac">[3\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/3.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">11.</span> Imagine you arrive as a researcher in an unknown country with a language that is completely foreign to you. Under what circumstances would you say that the people there are giving commands, understanding commands, obeying them, rebelling against commands, etc.?

### <span class="fac">[3\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/3.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">12.</span> The shared human way of acting is the frame of reference by means of which we interpret a foreign language.

### <span class="fac">[3\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/3.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">13.</span> Let us imagine that the people in that country are engaged in ordinary human activities & that, as it seems, they make use of an articulated language. If one observes their activities, they are understandable & appear ‘logical’. But if one tries to learn their language, one finds that it is impossible. For there is no regular correlation between what they say, the sounds they make, & the activities; nevertheless, their speech is not superfluous: for if one gags one of them, this has similar consequences as with us. Without sounds, their actions become confused (as I would like to express it). Should we say that these people have a language; commands, communications, etc.? What we call “language” lacks regularity.

### <span class="fac">[3\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/3.webp),[4\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/4.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">14.</span> So, I explain what “command” and “rule” mean through “regularity”? How do I explain to someone the meaning of “regular,” “uniform,” “equal”? – To someone who, let’s say, only speaks French, I will explain these words through their French equivalents. But someone who does not yet possess these concepts, I will teach the words through _examples_ and through _practice_. And in doing so, I will share with him no less than what I myself know. I will show him, in this instruction, equal colors, equal lengths, equal figures, let him find and produce them, etc. I will guide him, for example, to continue series ornaments, e.g., 🖵

on command ‘uniformly.’ – And also to continue a series 🖵

_so_: 🖵 I demonstrate it to him; he imitates me; and I influence him through utterances of approval, rejection, expectation, encouragement. I let him proceed, or I hold him back, etc. Imagine you are witnessing such instruction. In it, no word would be explained by itself, no logical circle would be made.

### <span class="fac">[4\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/4.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">15.</span> The phrase “and so on,” and “and so on ad infinitum,” will also be explained in this instruction. (It can also serve as a gesture, among other things.)

### <span class="fac">[4\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/4.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">16.</span> “But are you really explaining to him what you yourself understand? Aren’t you letting him _guess_ the essence? You give him examples; but he has to guess their tendency. Thus, your intention.” – Any explanation that I can give to myself, I also give to him. “He guesses what I mean” would mean: various interpretations of my explanation occur to him, and he guesses at one of them. He could therefore ask in this case. And I could, and would, answer him.

### <span class="fac">[4\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/4.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">17.</span> “However you instruct him in continuing the series ornament – how can he _know_ how to continue it independently?” – Well, how do I know? – – – If that means: do I have reasons? then the answer is: the reasons will soon run out. And then I will act, without reasons.

### <span class="fac">[5\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">18.</span> “How can I follow a rule?” – If this is not a question about the causes, then it is a question about the justification that I can give for the fact that I act _so_ according to the rule. If I have exhausted the justification, then I have now reached the hard rock; and my spade bends back. I am then inclined to say: “That is simply how I act.”

### <span class="fac">[5\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">19.</span> Remember that sometimes we demand an Erklärung, not because of its content, but because of the form of the Erklärung. Our demand is an architectural one, & the Erklärung is a kind of simulated pilaster.

### <span class="fac">[5\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">20.</span> If someone I fear gives me the command to continue the series, then I will quickly act with complete certainty, & the lack of reasons will not bother me.

### <span class="fac">[5\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">21.</span> “But this beginning of the series could obviously be interpreted in different ways (through algebraic expressions, for example), & so one had to first choose _one_ such interpretation.” – Not at all! Under certain circumstances, doubt was possible. But that does not mean that one doubted, or even could doubt. (This is related to what can be said about the psychological ‘atmosphere’ of a process.)

### <span class="fac">[5\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">22.</span> Could intuition alone dispel this doubt? — If it is an inner voice, how do I know _how_ to follow it? And how do I know that it will not mislead me? For if it can guide me _correctly_, then it can also mislead me. [Intuition as an unnecessary excuse]

### <span class="fac">[5\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/5.webp),[6\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6.webp),[6a\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">23.</span> “So you are saying that the agreement of people decides what is right & what is wrong?” – Right & wrong are what we say; & in language, people are in agreement. This is not an agreement of opinions, but of forms of life.

### <span class="fac">[6a\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">24.</span> “But doesn’t understanding extend further than all examples?” – A very remarkable expression, & quite natural!

### <span class="fac">[6a\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">25.</span> But is that _everything_? Isn't there an even deeper explanation; or must the _understanding_ of the explanation not be even deeper? – Yes, do I even have a deeper understanding myself? Do I have more than I give in the explanation? – But where then does the feeling come from that I have more? Is it that I interpret the unlimited as a length that extends beyond every length?

### <span class="fac">[6a\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">26.</span> An instruction that aims to remain confined to the examples presented, and one that goes beyond them, differ from each other.

### <span class="fac">[6a\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">27.</span> It is, of course, probably to be distinguished: “etc.,” which is an abbreviation of the way of writing, from that which is _not_. “Etc. ad inf.” is _not_ an abbreviation of the way of writing.

### <span class="fac">[6a\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">28.</span> The _gesture_ of saying “and so on” has a function comparable to that of pointing to an object or to a place.

### <span class="fac">[6a\[7\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/6a.webp),[7\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/7.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">29.</span> A person can encourage themselves, give themselves orders, obey, scold, punish themselves, pose a question, and answer it. One could therefore also imagine people who speak only in monologues. Their activities are accompanied by self-talk. – A researcher who observes them and listens to their speech might succeed in translating their language into ours. (He would, incidentally, thereby be in a position to correctly predict the actions of these people, because he also hears them make plans and decisions.) But would a language also be conceivable in which one could write down or express one’s inner experiences – one’s feelings, moods, etc. – for one’s own use? – Can’t we do that in our ordinary language? – But that is not what I mean. The words of this language should refer to what only the speaker can know; to their immediate, private sensations. Another person naturally cannot understand this language.

### <span class="fac">[7\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/7.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">30.</span> How do words relate to sensations? – There does not seem to be any problem in this; for do we not talk about sensations every day, and name them? But how is the connection between the name and what is named established? The question is the same as: How does a person learn the meaning of the names of sensations? E.g., of the word “pain.” In broad terms, it is as follows: words are connected with the original, natural expression of the sensation and put in its place. A child has hurt itself, it screams; and now the adults speak to it and teach it exclamations and later sentences. They teach the child a new pain-behaviour. “So you are saying that the word ‘pain’ actually means screaming?” – On the contrary; the verbal expression of the pain replaces the screaming and does not describe it.

### <span class="fac">[7\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/7.webp),[8\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/8.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">31.</span> In what respect are my sensations _private_? – Well, only I can know whether I really have pain; another can only presume it. – That is, in one way, false, in another way nonsensical. When we use the word “know” in the way it is normally used (and how else should we use it?), then others very often know it when I am in pain. – Yes, but not with the certainty with which I myself know it! – One cannot say of me (except perhaps in jest) that I _know_ that I am in pain. What should that mean, except perhaps that I _have_ pain? One cannot say that others learn my sensation _‘only’_ through my behaviour; for one cannot say of me that I learned it. I have it. That is correct: it makes sense to say of others that they are in doubt as to whether I am in pain, but not to say it of myself.

### <span class="fac">[8\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/8.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">32.</span> “The other cannot have my pain.” – That is nonsense. Which are _my_ pains? What counts here as a criterion of identity? Consider what makes it possible to speak of “_two exactly identical_” objects in the case of physical objects. For example, to say: “This armchair is not the same one that you saw here yesterday, but it is an exactly identical one.” As far as it _makes sense_ to say that my pain is the same as his, so far can we both have the same pain. (It would also be conceivable that two people experience pain at the same – not just homologous – location. Whether this is the case with Siamese twins, I do not know.) I have seen someone in a discussion about this subject strike his chest and say: “But he cannot have _**this**_ pain!” – The answer to this is that by emphatically stressing the word “this,” one does not define a criterion of identity. Rather, the emphasis merely reflects the case that such a criterion already exists and we only need to remind ourselves of it.

### <span class="fac">[8\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/8.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">33.</span> Replacing the word “equal” with “identical” (for example) is also a typical device in philosophy. It is as if we were talking about shades of meaning, and it is only a matter of hitting the right nuance with our words. And that is what philosophizing is about only when the task is to give a psychologically accurate account of the temptation to say this or that. What we are trying to say is, of course, not philosophy; rather, it is its raw material. What a mathematician, for example, is inclined to say about the objectivity and reality of mathematical facts is not a philosophy of mathematics, but something that philosophy would have to _deal with_.

### <span class="fac">[8\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/8.webp),[9\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/9.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">34.</span> What about the language that describes my inner experiences, and which only I can understand? _How_ do I designate my sensations with words? – In the way we usually do? Are my sensation words linked to my natural expressions of sensation? – In this case, my language is not ‘private’. Someone else could understand it, just as I do. – But what if I possessed no natural expressions of sensation, but only the sensation itself? And now I simply associate names with the sensations and use these names in a description. – Let's consider a simple case. I want to keep a diary about the recurrence of a certain sensation. To this end, I associate it with the sign “E” and write this sign in a calendar on each day that I have the sensation. I want to first note that I cannot express a definition of this sign. – But I can give myself a kind of ostensive definition! – How? By pointing to the sensation? – Not in the usual sense; but I speak or write the sign, and in doing so, I concentrate my attention on the sensation. I point, as it were, to it internally. – But what is the purpose of this ceremony? Because it seems to be just a ceremony! After all, a definition is meant to establish the meaning of a sign. – Well, that is precisely what happens through the concentration of attention; for in this way, I imprint the connection of the sign with the sensation on my memory. – “I imprint it on my memory” can only mean: this process causes me to remember the connection correctly in the future. But in our case, I have no criterion for the correctness. One might say here: what always appears correct to me is correct – – – and that means only that one cannot speak of ‘correct’ here.

### <span class="fac">[9\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/9.webp),[10\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/10.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">35.</span> “Well, I _believe_ that this is the sensation E again.” – You probably believe that you believe it! So, the one who wrote the sign in the calendar _didn’t_ actually write anything down? – Don’t take it for granted that someone writes something down when they write signs – in a calendar, for example. A note has a function; and “E” does not have one, _at least_, not yet.

### <span class="fac">[10\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/10.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">36.</span> What reason do we have to call “E” the sign for a _sensation_? For “sensation” is a word in our general language, understandable to all. The use of this word therefore requires a justification that everyone can understand. – And it would also be of no use to say: it doesn’t have to be a _sensation_; if he writes “E”, he has _something_ – and that is all we can say. But “have” and “something” also belong to the general language. – In the end, the philosopher is reduced to wanting to utter only an unarticulated sound. – And such a sound, under certain conditions, is an expression.

### <span class="fac">[10\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/10.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">37.</span> Let us now consider a use for the entry “E”. I make the following observation: whenever I have a certain sensation, I see on a manometer that my blood pressure rises. This enables me to announce an increase in my blood pressure without the aid of an apparatus. A useful result. And now it seems to be quite irrelevant whether I have correctly recognized the sensation or not. Let us assume that I consistently made a mistake in identifying it; it would make no difference. And this already shows that the assumption of this mistake was only an illusion. (We were turning a knob that looked as if it could be used to adjust something on the machine; but it was just a decorative ornament and not connected to the mechanism.) And what reason do we have here to call “E” the designation for a sensation? Perhaps the way in which it is used in this language-game. – And why a “certain sensation”, i.e., the same one each time? Only because I use the same sign each time.

### <span class="fac">[10\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/10.webp),[11\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/11.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">38.</span> “Imagine a person who could not remember what the word ‘pain’ means; and therefore calls something different by that name each time – but nevertheless uses the word in agreement with the usual sign and conditions of pain!” – The wheel does not belong to the machine that can be turned without something else moving along with it.

### <span class="fac">[11\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/11.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">39.</span> “When I say ‘I am in pain,’ I am in any case justified before myself.” – Does this mean: “If the other person knew what I call ‘pain,’ he would admit that I am using the word correctly”?

### <span class="fac">[11\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/11.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">40.</span> Using the word without justification does not mean using it incorrectly.

### <span class="fac">[11\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/11.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">41.</span> If I imagine something, doesn't something actually happen? Well, something happens – and why then do I make a fuss? Probably to communicate what is happening. – But how does one communicate anything at all? When does one say that something is being communicated? – What is the language-game of communicating? I would like to say: You take it for granted that one can communicate something to someone. That is, we are so accustomed to communication through speech, in conversation, that it seems to us that the whole point of communication lies in this: that another person understands the meaning of the words (an ethereal thing); as if absorbing it into his brain. If he then does something with it, that is no longer part of the direct purpose of language. One might say: “The communication causes me to _know_ that the other person is in pain; it causes this mental phenomenon; everything else is immaterial to the communication.” What this peculiar phenomenon of knowledge is – one can take one's time with that. Mental processes are peculiar. (It would be similar to say: “The clock shows us the time – – – what time is, has not yet been decided; and what one uses the time for does not belong here.”)

### <span class="fac">[11\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/11.webp),[12\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/12.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">42.</span> How is it, for example, with the word “red” – should I say that this designates something ‘facing us all,’ and that everyone should actually have another word to designate his _own_ sensation of red? (Or is it like this: the word “red” designates something that is familiar to us all; and for each person, also something that is familiar only to him? (Or perhaps better: it _refers_ to something that is familiar only to him.)

### <span class="fac">[12\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/12.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">43.</span> What is essential about the private experience is not that everyone has his own copy, but that no one knows whether the other person also has _this_ one, or something else. For example, it is possible – although not verifiable – to assume that one part of humanity has one visual impression of red, and another part has a different one.

### <span class="fac">[12\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/12.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">44.</span> It does not help us to understand the function of the word “red” to say that it “_refers_ to” the private, instead of “designating” it; but it is the psychologically more accurate expression for a certain experience in philosophizing. It is as if, when uttering the word, I cast a sidelong glance at my own sensation, as if to say to myself, I already know what I mean by it.

### <span class="fac">[12\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/12.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">45.</span> Look at the blue of the sky, and say to yourself: “How blue the sky is!” – If you do it spontaneously – not with philosophical intentions – then it does not occur to you that this color impression belongs only _to you_. And you have no hesitation in addressing this exclamation to another person. And if you point to something while saying the words, it is the sky. I mean: You do not have the feeling of pointing inward, which often accompanies ‘naming the sensation’ when one is thinking about ‘private language.’ You also do not think that you should actually point to the color with your hand, not just with your attention. (Consider what it means to ‘point with one's attention at something.’)

### <span class="fac">[12\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/12.webp),[13\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/13.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">46.</span> “But don’t we at least mean something quite specific when we look at a colour & name the colour impression?” It is as if we were separating the colour **impression**, like a film, from the seen object. (This should arouse our suspicion.)

### <span class="fac">[13\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/13.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">47.</span> But how is it possible that one is tempted to believe that one is once naming the colour that is familiar to everyone with a word, namely: the ‘visual impression’ that I am receiving now? How can there even be a temptation here? – In these cases, I do not apply the same kind of attention to the colour. When I say – as I would like to say – the colour impression that belongs to me, I immerse myself in the colour. Approximately, as if I could not get enough of looking at a colour. Therefore, it is easier to create this experience when one looks at a bright colour that makes a strong impression.

### <span class="fac">[13\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/13.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">48.</span> “I know how the colour green appears to me.” – Well, that makes sense! – Indeed; what use of the sentence are you thinking of?

<span class="series-number">52.</span> “But doesn't what you say come down to the fact that there is no pain without pain-behaviour?” – It comes down to this: one can only say of a living person, and something similar to it (something that behaves similarly), that it has sensations; that it sees; that it is blind; that it hears; that it is deaf; that it is conscious, or unconscious.

### <span class="fac">[14\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/14.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">53.</span> “But in a fairy tale, can’t the pot also _see_ & hear?” (Certainly; but it can also _speak_.) “But the fairy tale only invents what is not the case; it does not speak _nonsense_.” – That is not so simple. Is it untruth, or nonsense, to say that a pot speaks? Do we form a clear picture of the circumstances under which we would say that a pot speaks? (When children play with trains, their game is connected with their knowledge of trains. ### <span class="fac">[15\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/15.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">54.</span> Where does the very idea come from that beings, objects, could ‘feel something’? Was I led to this by my upbringing, which made me aware of the feelings within me, and now am I transferring this idea to objects outside of me? I recognize that there is something (in me) that I can call “pain” without contradicting the way others use the word? – I do not transfer my idea to stones and plants, etc. Could I not imagine that I have terrible pain and am turning into a stone while it lasts? Yes, how do I know, when I close my eyes, that I have not turned into a stone? – And if that has now happened, in what sense will _the stone_ have pain? In what sense can it be said of the stone? Yes, why should pain even have a bearer here?! And can it be said of the stone that it has a mind and _that_ is in pain? What does a mind, what do pains have to do with a stone? Only of something that behaves like a human can it be said that it _has_ pain. Because it must be said of a body, or, if you like, of a mind that has a body. And how can a body _have_ a mind?

<span class="series-number">56.</span> Look at a stone and imagine it has sensations! One says to oneself: How could one even come up with the idea of attributing a _sensation_ to a _thing_? And so a corpse also seems to be completely _inaccessible_ to pain. – Our attitude towards the living is not the same as towards the dead. All our reactions are different. – If someone says, “It can’t just be because the living thing moves in such and such a way and the dead one doesn’t,” I want to point out to him that here is a case of the transition ‘from quantity to quality.’

### <span class="fac">[16\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/16.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">57.</span> Think of the recognition of _facial expression_; the description of facial expression, which does not consist in giving the _measurements_ of the face! Also, think about how one can imitate a person’s face without seeing one’s own in a mirror.

### <span class="fac">[16\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/16.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">58.</span> But isn’t it absurd to say that a _body_ has pain? – And why does one feel an absurdity in it? In what sense does my hand not have pain; rather, I am in my hand? What is this a question of: Is it its _body_ that feels pain? – How is it to be decided? How does it manifest that it is _not_ the body? – Well, something like this: If someone has pain in their hand, the _hand_ does not say so (unless it writes it), and one does not offer comfort to the hand, but to the person suffering and looks them in the eye.

### <span class="fac">[16\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/16.webp),[17\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/17.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">59.</span> I turn to stone, & my pains continue. – And what if I were now mistaken & it were no longer _pain_? – – – But I cannot be mistaken here – it means nothing to doubt whether I have pain! – I.e.: if someone said, “I don’t know if what I have is pain, or something else,” we would think that he doesn’t know what the word “pain” means & would explain it to him. – How? – Perhaps through gestures, or by pricking him & saying: “Look, that’s pain.” He could understand this explanation of a word like any other, correctly, incorrectly, or not at all. & whichever he does, he will show it in the use of the word, as he does in other cases. If he now, for example, said, “Oh, I know what ‘pain’ means, but whether _this_ is pain that I have here, I don’t know” – then we would just shake our heads & have to regard his words as a strange reaction, with which we don’t know what to do. (It would be something like when we hear someone say seriously: “I clearly remember believing just before my birth that…”) This expression of doubt does not belong to the language-game. But if now the expression of sensation, human behaviour, is excluded, then it seems as if I _may_ doubt again. The fact that I am tempted here to say that one can take sensation to be something other than what it is comes from the fact that, if I think away the normal language-game with the expression of sensation, I now need a criterion of identity for it; & then the possibility of error would also exist.

### <span class="fac">[17\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/17.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">60.</span> I certainly do not identify my sensation by criteria, but I use the same expression. But that does not _end_ the language-game; that is where it begins. But does it not begin with the sensation – which I describe? – Perhaps the word “describe” has served us well here. I say “I describe my state of mind” & “I describe my table.” One must remember the differences in language-games.

### <span class="fac">[17\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/17.webp),[18\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/18.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">61.</span> If I say of myself that I only know from my own case what the word “pain” means, must I not say _that_ of others too? & how can I generalize the _one_ case in such an irresponsible way? – Well, everyone says it of himself, that he only knows of himself what pain is! – Suppose that everyone had a box in which there was something that we call a “beetle.” No one can look into anyone else’s box; & everyone says that he knows only what a beetle is from looking at _his_ beetle. Now, it could well be that everyone has a different thing in his box. Yes, one could imagine that such a thing constantly changed. – But if the word “beetle” has a use for all these people, then it is not the use of designating a thing. The thing in the box does not belong to the language-game at all, not even as an _object_: for the box could also be empty. – No; the thing in the box can be ‘shortened’; it is removed, whatever it is. That is to say: if one constructs the grammar of the expression of sensation after the model of ‘object & designation’, then the object falls away as irrelevant to the consideration.

### <span class="fac">[18\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/18.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">62.</span> And what is “I only know of the _individual_ case…” supposed to be, anyway? An empirical proposition? No. – A grammatical one? So I think to myself: Let everyone say of themselves that they only know what pain is from their own pain. Not that people actually say this, or are even willing to say it. But if everyone were to say it – well, it could be a kind of exclamation. And even if it is meaningless as a communication, it is still an image; and why shouldn't we want to conjure up such an image in our minds? Instead of the words, imagine a painted, allegorical image. Yes, when we reflect philosophically, we often see just such an image. Formally, a pictorial representation of our grammar. Not facts; but rather, as it were, illustrated idioms.

### <span class="fac">[18\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/18.webp),[19\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/19.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">65.</span> If one has to imagine the pain of another person according to the model of one's own, then this is not such an easy matter: for I am supposed to imagine pains, which I _feel_, after _the_ pains that I _feel_, pains which I do _not_ feel. In my imagination, I should not simply make a transition from one location of pain to another. For example, from pains in the hand to pains in the arm. Because I am not supposed to imagine that I am experiencing pain in a particular part of his body. (Which would also be possible.) Pain-behaviour can indicate a painful spot, but the suffering person is the one who complains.

### <span class="fac">[19\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/19.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">66.</span> “I can only _believe_ that the other person is in pain, but I _know_ it when I have it.” – Yes; one can decide to say “I believe he is in pain” instead of “He is in pain”; and “I know I am in pain” instead of “I am in pain.” But that is all. – What looks like an explanation, or a statement about mental processes, is in reality a substitution of one way of speaking for another, which, while we are philosophizing, seems more appropriate to us. Try, in a real case, to doubt the other person's fear or pain!

### <span class="fac">[19\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/19.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">67.</span> “But you must admit that there is a difference between pain-behaviour with pain and pain-behaviour without pain.” – Admit? What difference could be greater! – “And yet you always come to the conclusion that the sensation itself is nothing.” – Not at all. It is not something, but it is not nothing either! The result was only that nothing would serve the same purpose as something, about which nothing can be said. We only rejected the grammar that insists on imposing itself on us. The paradox disappears only when we break radically with the idea that language always functions in _one_ way, always serves the same purpose: to convey thoughts – whether they are thoughts about houses, pain, good and evil, or whatever.

### <span class="fac">[21\[6\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/21.webp),[22\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/22.webp)</span>

### <span class="fac">[23\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/23.webp),[24\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/24.webp)</span>

It is difficult; but is it impossible?

### <span class="fac">[25\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/25.webp),[26\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/26.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">91.</span> The armchair speaks to itself: “…” _Where_ does it say it? In one of its parts? Or outside of itself, in the air around it? Or not _anywhere_? But what then is the difference between the speaking of this armchair and that of another one standing next to it? – But how is it then with the human being: where does _he_ speak to himself? How is it that this question seems meaningless – and no designation is needed except that this human being is speaking to himself – while the question of _where_ the armchair speaks to itself seems to demand an answer? I believe the reason is this: We want to know _how_ the armchair should correspond to a human being. Whether the head is, for example, at the top of the back; and so on.

### <span class="fac">[26\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/26.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">92.</span> What is it like when one speaks to oneself inwardly; what is happening there? – How am I supposed to explain it? Well, just like this, in the way you can teach someone the meaning of the expression “to speak to oneself”. And as children we learn this meaning. – Only that no one will say that the person who teaches it to us tells us ‘what is happening there’.

### <span class="fac">[26\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/26.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">93.</span> Rather, it seems to us as if the teacher, in this case, imparts the meaning to the student; without telling it to him directly; that, however, the student is eventually brought to give himself the correct ostensive explanation. And herein lies our illusion.

### <span class="fac">[26\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/26.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">94.</span> He does a _calculation_ in his head. He uses the result, let’s say, when building a bridge or a machine. – Are you saying that he found this number without doing a _calculation_? That it somehow, after a kind of daydreaming, just occurred to him? Surely there must have been a _calculation_, and one was done. Because he _knows_ that he calculated, and how he calculated; and the correct result could not be explained without a _calculation_. – But what if I said: “It _seems to him_ that he calculated. And why should the correct result be explainable? Isn’t it already incomprehensible enough that he was able to **calculate** without a word or a written symbol?” –

### <span class="fac">[26\[5\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/26.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">95.</span> Is calculation in one's head, in a certain sense, more unreal than calculation on paper? Is it the real calculation – calculation in one's head? – Is it similar to calculation on paper? – I don't know if I should call it similar. Is a piece of white paper with black lines on it similar to a human body?

### <span class="fac">[27\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/27.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">96.</span> Are Adelheid & the bishop playing a _real_ game of chess? – Of course. They are not merely pretending to play – as could indeed happen in a play. – But this game, for example, has no beginning! – But it does; like every proper game of chess. –

### <span class="fac">[27\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/27.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">97.</span> Is mental calculation any less real than calculation on paper? – One might be inclined to say so, but one can also be brought to the opposite view by saying that paper, ink, etc., are merely logical constructions. “I performed the multiplication … in my head” – do I perhaps not believe such a statement? – – – But was it really a multiplication? It was not just ‘a’ multiplication, but _this_ one – in my head. This is the point where I go wrong. For I now want to say: “There _was some_, mental process _corresponding_ to the multiplication on paper. So that it would make sense to say: _This_ process in the mind corresponds to _this_ process on paper.” And it would then make sense to speak of a method of depiction, according to which the image of the sign represents the sign itself.

### <span class="fac">[27\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/27.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">98.</span> Here, one would like to ask again: “What is it like, what is happening when one calculates in one's head?” And in the particular case, the answer could be: “I first add 17 and 18, then I subtract 39…” But that is not the answer to our question. What it means to calculate in one's head is not explained in this way.

### <span class="fac">[27\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/27.webp),[28\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/28.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">99.</span> Would it be conceivable that one learned to calculate in one's head without ever calculating in writing or orally? – “To learn” probably means: to be brought to the point of being able to do it. And the question is only what will be considered the criterion for whether one can do it. Is it also possible that only mental calculation is known to a people, and no other? Here one must ask: “What would that look like?” – One would therefore have to imagine this as a borderline case, and then ask: whether we still want to apply the concept of ‘mental calculation’ here, or whether it has lost its point (for us) under such circumstances.

### <span class="fac">[28\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/28.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">100.</span> “But why do you have so little confidence in yourself? You always know what ‘calculating’ means. So if you say that you calculated in your imagination, then that is what happened. If you hadn’t calculated, you wouldn’t say so. Likewise – if you say that you see something red in your imagination, then it will be red. You do know what ‘red’ is. – And further: you don’t always rely on agreement with others; for often you report that you saw something that no one else saw.” – But I do have confidence in myself; I say without hesitation that I calculated this in my head, that I imagined this color. The difficulty is not that I doubt whether I really imagined something red. But _this_: if the imagination is a depiction of reality, can I then ask: “What must a correct image of this color look like,” or “What must it be like”? Can I learn that?

### <span class="fac">[28\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/28.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">101.</span> The _deep_ aspect easily escapes one.

### <span class="fac">[28\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/28.webp),[29\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/29.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">102.</span> “I’m not sure whether I can imagine that this chair leg has pain.” – And if I can – what then? In what respect is that interesting? What connections does it have with the rest of life? – I can perhaps also imagine (although it is not easy) that each of the people I see on the street has pain, but skillfully conceals it. And it is important: that I imagine a skillful concealment. That I don’t just say: “His mind has pain; but what does that have to do with his body!” – And if I imagine that now – how do I do it? I look at someone and think, “It must be hard to laugh when one has such pain,” and much of the like. I am, as it were, playing a role, ‘acting’ as if the others have pain.

### <span class="fac">[29\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/29.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">103.</span> “When I imagine that he has pain, … goes on,” or “actually, only … goes on in me.” This analysis leads us nowhere. Another person then says: “I think I can also imagine it, _without_ thinking … at the same time.” That is all quite irrelevant. An abortive attempt to circumvent the philosophical difficulty.

### <span class="fac">[29\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/29.webp),[30\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/30.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">104.</span> “But when I imagine that someone who is laughing actually has pain, I don’t imagine pain-behaviour, because I see the opposite of that. _What_ then am I imagining?” – I have already said it – and I don’t necessarily imagine that _I_ am feeling pain. – – – “But how does it happen, then, when I imagine that?” – Where, I mean, outside of philosophy, do we use the words “I can imagine that he has pain,” or “I imagine that …,” or “Imagine that …!” – – – For example, one says to someone who has to play a role in a play: “You must imagine here that this person…” – and he is not told what he _actually_ should do. Therefore, that analysis is not relevant at all. – We now observe the actor who is imagining the suffering of the other.

### <span class="fac">[30\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/30.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">105.</span> Under what circumstances would we ask someone: “What actually went on in you, how did you imagine _that_?” And what kind of answer do we expect?

### <span class="fac">[30\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/30.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">106.</span> There is an ambiguity about what role the ability to imagine plays in our investigation. In what respect does it ensure the sense of a sentence.

### <span class="fac">[30\[4\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/30.webp),[31\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/31.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">107.</span> “I can quite easily imagine someone acting in such a way and yet seeing nothing reprehensible in the action.” And now follows a description of how one should imagine this. “I can imagine a tribe in which it is considered indecent to count, except for amusement.” This means approximately the following: I could easily elaborate on this image. “It makes sense to speak of an endless row of trees. I can imagine a row of trees continuing without end.” That is, roughly: If it makes sense to say that the row of trees comes to an end here, then it must make sense to say that it does not come to an end here; and thus also: that it comes to an end nowhere. The visual image is approximately that of a row of trees continuing ‘indefinitely’. Such an image, of course, conceals the sense of that expression as little as it _explains_ it. “I can imagine our standards always contracting when…” means: if our standards were to behave in this way, we would not hesitate to say…. This explains the sense of a particular mode of expression. “I can imagine how the other person has pains in his stomach!” (I could, for example, add: I am doing so right now.) Does this explain what it means for the other person to have pains? (I can more easily imagine the other person’s pains if I myself am not feeling quite well.)

### <span class="fac">[31\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/31.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">108.</span> The feeling of the unbridgeable gulf between consciousness and brain process: how is it that this does not enter into the considerations of everyday life? The idea of this kind of difference is associated with a faint dizziness; which occurs when we perform logical tricks. (It is a sign of _confusion_, not of the _difficulty_ of the subject. Set theory.) When does this feeling arise? Well, if I, for example, direct my attention in a certain way to my consciousness and say to myself: _**this**_ is supposed to be produced by a brain process! as if I were touching my forehead. – But what can it mean to “direct my attention to my consciousness”? It is nothing more remarkable than the fact that there is such a thing! What I called it (for these words are not used in everyday life) was an act of looking. I looked straight ahead, but _not_ at any particular point or object. My eyes were wide open, my eyebrows not furrowed – as they usually are when a particular object interests me. No such interest preceded the looking. My gaze was ‘vacant’; or _similar_ to that of a person who is admiring the illumination of the sky and drinking in the light. Now, consider that there was nothing paradoxical about the sentence that I uttered as a paradox – _this_ is produced by a brain process. I could have uttered it during an experiment that was carried out to show that the light effect that I see is produced by the stimulation of a particular part of the brain. – But I did not utter the sentence in the context in which it would have had an everyday and non-paradoxical sense. And my attention was not of the kind that would have been appropriate for the experiment. (My gaze would then have been ‘intent’, not ‘vacant’.)

### <span class="fac">[32\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/32.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">109.</span> Here we have a case of introspection; not unlike the one through which W. James brought out that the ‘self’ consists mainly of ‘peculiar motions in the head and between the head and throat’. And what James’s introspection showed was not the meaning of the word “self” (in so far as this means something similar to “person”, “human being”, “he himself”, “I myself”), nor an analysis of such a thing, but the state of attention of a philosopher who is uttering the word “self” to himself and wants to analyze its meaning. (And much can be learned from this.)

### <span class="fac">[32\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/32.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">110.</span> “People agree with one another that they see, hear, feel, etc. (even if some are blind and some are deaf). They thus attest to the fact that they have _consciousness_. “But how remarkable! To whom am I actually making a communication when I say, “I have consciousness”? What is the purpose of saying this to myself, and how can the other person understand me? – Well, sentences such as “I see,” “I hear,” “I am conscious,” certainly have their use. I tell the doctor, “Now I can hear again in this ear”; I tell someone who believes I am unconscious, “I am conscious again,” and so on.

### <span class="fac">[32\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/32.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">111.</span> So, am I observing myself and noticing that I see, or that I am conscious? And why even talk about observation at all! Why not simply say: “I notice that I am conscious”? – But what is the purpose of the words “I notice” here – why not say: “I am conscious”? – But do the words “I notice” not here indicate that I am paying attention to my consciousness? (Which is not usually the case.) – If that is the case, then the sentence “I notice that…” does not say that I am conscious, but that my attention is directed in such and such a way. But isn’t it a particular experience that causes me to say, “I am conscious again”? – _Which_ experience? In what situation do we say this?

### <span class="fac">[33\[1\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/33.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">112.</span> Is the fact that I have consciousness an empirical fact? – But is it not said of human beings that they have consciousness; but of a tree or a stone, that they do not? – How would it be if it were different? – Would all people be unconscious? – No; not in the ordinary sense of the word. But I, for example, would not have consciousness – – – as I actually do have it now.

### <span class="fac">[33\[2\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/33.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">113.</span> But can I not imagine that the people around me are automatons, have no consciousness, even if their way of acting is the same as always? – If I imagine this now – alone in my room – I see the people with a blank look (as if in a trance) going about their business – and the idea is perhaps a little eerie. – But now try to hold on to this idea in ordinary interaction, or on the street! Say to yourself, for example: “These children there are mere automatons; all their liveliness is merely pretense.” And these words will either become completely meaningless to you; or you will create in yourself some kind of strange feeling, or something similar. To see a living person as an automaton is quite analogous to seeing some figure as a borderline case, or a variation of another, e.g., a window cross as a swastika.

### <span class="fac">[33\[3\]](https://cdn.wittgensteinnachlass.com/2000px/webp/Ts-241b/33.webp)</span>

<span class="series-number">114.</span> It seems paradoxical to us that in _one_ account, physical and conscious states are mixed together: “He suffered great pain and tossed and turned restlessly.” This is quite ordinary; why does it seem paradoxical to us? Because we want to say that the sentence is about what is tangible and what is intangible. – – – But do you find anything wrong with me saying: “These 3 supports give the building stability”? Are three and stability tangible? – – – See the sentence as an instrument, and its sense as its use!
